# 06 — Excluded & Contested (with reasons)

To keep the bar honest, what **failed** is recorded as carefully as what passed. (Carried from
Dive 1, now in `../_archive/dive-1-original/`; the independent dive took these on trust, so Dive 1's
evidence-based exclusions stand.)

## EXCLUDED — failed the bar

| Claim | Why excluded |
|---|---|
| **Learning styles / VARK** (teach to a learner's "style") | Pashler et al. (2008) reviewed 70+ studies for the required "meshing" interaction (style × matched instruction). Found essentially **none**; several contradicted it. "No adequate evidence base." Persists as belief (Nancekivell 2020) but is a **neuromyth**. → Tailor to **prior knowledge and the task**, never to a "style." |
| **Pomodoro technique** (25/5 timers) | 2025 scoping review (32 studies, ~5,270 ppl): evidence **moderate-to-low**, and outcomes measured were **fatigue, self-rated focus, distractibility, motivation** — *not* learning retention or transfer. A time-management/attention aid measured on the **wrong outcomes**. → May be *mentioned* as an optional scheduling scaffold; **never** counted as a learning technique. |
| **Rereading** | Dunlosky et al. (2013): **low utility.** Produces fluency (familiarity) but little durable learning — the canonical thing retrieval practice beats. |
| **Highlighting / underlining** | Dunlosky et al. (2013): **low utility.** Passive; marks too much; no transfer benefit. |
| **Passive summarization** (note-copying as primary study) | Dunlosky et al. (2013): **low utility** — benefit depends on summarizing skill novices lack. *Distinguish* from genuinely **generative** restructuring/mapping/self-explaining, which works (`02`, generative umbrella). |
| **Keyword mnemonic / imagery for text** | Dunlosky et al. (2013): **low utility** — narrow, fragile, doesn't generalize. (Mnemonics have niche use for arbitrary paired associates, not understanding.) |
| **"Left-brain / right-brain" learners** | No scientific basis; folk neuromyth. |
| **Multitasking / "I learn better with divided attention"** | Divided attention during encoding reliably **harms** memory formation. |

## CONTESTED — included only with warning labels (see `05 Part C`)

- **Growth mindset** — small & conditional (real for at-risk learners in supportive contexts;
  Yeager 2019). Not a foundation. The only robust takeaway is "praise process, not person."
- **Deliberate practice** — real principle, but the "10,000 hours" determinism is overstated
  (only ~4% of variance in education; Macnamara 2014).

## METHODOLOGICAL caution that touches everything (see `07`)

- **Published effect sizes are inflated** (publication bias): education meta-analyses avg d≈0.40
  vs. pre-registered RCTs d≈0.06. This isn't an exclusion — it's a discount applied to *all*
  magnitudes, and a reason to weight **classroom-replicated** techniques (retrieval, spacing)
  highest.

## The rule

Excluded items were excluded because they either have **no effect on learning** or are measured
on **outcomes that aren't learning** (focus, satisfaction, self-rated confidence). If a popular
technique can't show a learning-outcome effect that replicates, it does not enter the engine —
regardless of how popular it is.
