## Correction 1 — Growth mindset: the first dive was too dismissive

**What `../research/05` says now:** growth mindset is "WEAK… d = 0.08… ~0.02 in the best
studies… do not lean on it," citing Sisk et al. (2018).

**What the independent dive found:** that's only half the story. The **largest experiment ever
run** — **Yeager et al. (2019), *Nature***, the National Study of Learning Mindsets, **N =
12,490** ninth-graders, nationally representative, individually randomized — found that a brief
(two 25-minute online sessions) growth-mindset intervention produced:

- **+0.10 grade points** in core subjects for **lower-achieving** students, persisting into
  sophomore year;
- effects **concentrated where school culture/peer norms supported** the mindset (it didn't work
  everywhere — context-dependent).

**Reconciliation:** Sisk and Yeager actually *agree the average effect is small.* They differ on
meaning: Sisk frames small-and-near-zero-on-average as "don't bother"; Yeager shows that a
**cheap, scalable** intervention with a **small but real, targeted** effect (at-risk students, in
supportive contexts) can still be **worthwhile**. The honest position is **not** "growth mindset
is bunk" — it's:

> Growth-mindset effects are **small and conditional**, but **real for at-risk learners in
> supportive environments.** It is a *minor, targeted* tool, not a foundation, and not a
> substitute for retrieval/spacing/feedback.

**Proposed edit to `../research/05 Part C`:** soften "WEAK — do not lean on it" to "**SMALL &
CONDITIONAL** — real for at-risk learners in supportive contexts (Yeager 2019); not a
foundation." Keep the warning against treating it as a primary lever; drop the implication that
it does essentially nothing. *(Note: this does not change the engine's behavior much — the
feedback protocol already does the one thing that's solid: praise effort/process, not the
person.)*

## Correction 2 — Add the effect-size-inflation caveat to the overview

**What's missing:** `../research/00-overview.md` presents effect sizes (g ≈ 0.5–0.7, etc.)
without flagging that **education/psychology effect sizes are systematically inflated** by
publication bias (meta-analyses avg d ≈ 0.40 vs. pre-registered RCTs d ≈ 0.06; see `03`).

**Proposed edit:** add a short note to the overview and/or `02` stating that quoted effect sizes
are **optimistic upper bounds**, that techniques are ranked by **robustness/replication** (esp.
classroom replication) rather than headline magnitude, and that the honest claim is "well-
supported and worth doing," not "guaranteed effect size X."
