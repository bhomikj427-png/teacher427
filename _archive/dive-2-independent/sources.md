# Sources — Independent Dive

Deliberately weighted toward sources, author groups, and review bodies **not used in the first
dive** (`../research/sources.md`), plus the red-team / criticism literature.

## Transfer of retrieval practice

- **Pan, S. C., & Rickard, T. C. (2018).** Transfer of Test-Enhanced Learning: Meta-Analytic
  Review and Synthesis. *Psychological Bulletin, 144*(7), 710–756. (192 effect sizes; transfer
  d = 0.40.) https://pdf.retrievalpractice.org/transfer/Pan_Rickard_2018.pdf

## Red-team / limits

- **van Gog, T., & Sweller, J. (2015).** Not New, but Nearly Forgotten: The Testing Effect
  Decreases or Even Disappears as the Complexity of Learning Materials Increases. *Educational
  Psychology Review, 27*(2), 247–264.
  - Rebuttal: **Karpicke, J. D., & Aue, W. R. (2015).** The Testing Effect Is Alive and Well with
    Complex Materials. *Educational Psychology Review.*
    https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-015-9309-3
- **Interleaving needs initial blocking:** Hwang et al. (2025), "Undesirable Difficulty of
  Interleaved Practice…" *Language Learning*
  (https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/lang.12659); Kang, S. H. K., "Interleaved Training
  and Category Learning"; executive-function moderation
  (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10658001/).
- **Aptitude–treatment interactions / individual differences:** Kalyuga — expertise reversal
  effect and ATI; "Working Memory and Instructional Fit: Reintroducing Aptitude–Treatment
  Interaction" (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12189472/).
- **Cognitive Load Theory falsifiability critique:** discussion of germane-load circularity and
  Sweller's subsequent removal of germane load as a separate category. (Overview:
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognitive_load and review
  https://www.researchgate.net/publication/331280470)

## Effect-size inflation / replication

- **"Neglect of publication bias compromises meta-analyses of educational research." (2021).**
  *PLOS ONE.* (800 education meta-analyses avg d ≈ 0.40 vs. pre-registered RCTs d ≈ 0.06.)
  https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0252415 ·
  https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8174709/
- **"Meta-analyses in psychology often overestimate evidence for and size of effects."**
  https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10320355/

## Growth mindset — the corrective evidence

- **Yeager, D. S., et al. (2019).** A national experiment reveals where a growth mindset improves
  achievement. *Nature, 573*(7774), 364–369. (N = 12,490; +0.10 GPA for lower-achievers in
  supportive school contexts.) https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-019-1466-y ·
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31391586/
- Contrast with the first dive's **Sisk et al. (2018)** — both agree the *average* effect is
  small; they differ on whether the targeted effect is worthwhile.
