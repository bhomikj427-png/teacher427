# 01 — Independent Corroboration

These are confirmations of the first dive's core, drawn from **author groups and reviews the
first dive did not cite**. Convergence from independent sources is the strongest signal that a
finding is real rather than an artifact of one lab or one reviewer's bias.

## 1. The whole foundation, confirmed by a different flagship review

**Soderstrom & Bjork (2015), "Learning versus Performance: An Integrative Review"**
(*Perspectives on Psychological Science*). Independently establishes the exact foundation of
`../research/01`: **performance during training is an unreliable index of learning.** Conditions
can *improve* short-term performance while *failing* to produce durable learning — and certain
manipulations have *opposite* effects on performance vs. learning. This is the theoretical spine
of "desirable difficulties," confirmed by the original lab but in a separate, later, integrative
review. The first dive's most important single idea survives independent scrutiny.

## 2. A different author group, the same core technique list

**Weinstein, Madan & Sumeracki (2018), "Teaching the Science of Learning"** (*Cognitive
Research: Principles and Implications*, open access). A tutorial review by a **completely
different team** identifies six strategies with "robust support from decades of research":

1. **Spaced practice** ✓ (matches `../research/02 §2`)
2. **Interleaving** ✓ (`02 §6`)
3. **Retrieval practice** ✓ (`02 §1`)
4. **Elaboration** ✓ (matches elaborative interrogation / self-explanation, `02 §3–4`)
5. **Concrete examples** ✓ (matches worked examples, `02 §7`)
6. **Dual coding** ✓ (`02 §8`)

This is near-perfect overlap with the first dive's "what cleared the bar," produced
independently. Strong corroboration.

## 3. Retrieval practice works in *real classrooms*, not just labs

**Agarwal, Bain & Chamberlain — a 5-year applied program** with **1,400+ middle-school
students** (and the related systematic review, Agarwal et al. 2021, *Educational Psychology
Review*):

- Regular **no-stakes quizzes** covering ~one-third of taught material led students to score a
  **full grade level higher** on the quizzed material vs. the unquizzed material — same teacher,
  same class, real curriculum.
- **Delayed quizzes** were especially potent for retention.
- Quizzes produced **transfer** to novel items, and **quizzes with feedback** improved
  metacognitive awareness.

This directly answers the "but does it work outside the lab?" objection for the engine's #1
technique. It does.

## 4. Testing effect *transfers* — confirmed by independent meta-analysis

**Pan & Rickard (2018), "Transfer of Test-Enhanced Learning"** (*Psychological Bulletin*): 192
effect sizes, 122 experiments, N = 10,382, 40+ years of research. Testing yields
**transferable** learning at **d = 0.40** (medium) relative to restudy. (Boundary conditions in
`02`.) The benefit isn't confined to identical re-tests — it generalizes, which is what we
actually care about for understanding.

## 5. A major institutional review reaches the same shortlist

**Education Endowment Foundation (EEF) — "Cognitive Science Approaches in the Classroom"**
(University of Birmingham). Reviewed spaced learning, interleaving, retrieval practice, managing
cognitive load, schemas, multimedia/dual coding, embodied learning — and found the strongest
support for **retrieval practice, spaced practice, and worked examples**. Same top tier the
engine is built on, from an independent commissioning body. (Its important *caveat* about
classroom evidence is in `02`.)

## Bottom line

Four independent sources — a flagship review (Soderstrom & Bjork), a different cognitive-science
team (Weinstein et al.), a multi-year classroom program (Agarwal), and an institutional evidence
review (EEF) — **converge on the same core** the first dive selected. The engine's foundation is
not an artifact. It replicates.
