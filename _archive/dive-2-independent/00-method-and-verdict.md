# Independent Research Dive — Method & Verdict

This is a **second, independent** literature review of the science of teaching and learning,
done deliberately *after* and *separate from* the first dive (`../research/`) to test whether
the first dive was sound or just fluently generated.

## How this was kept independent

1. **Different sources.** I deliberately pulled from author groups the first dive did *not*
   lean on — Soderstrom & Bjork (2015), Weinstein/Madan/Sumeracki (2018), the EEF evidence
   review, Pan & Rickard (2018), Agarwal et al., Yeager et al. (2019).
2. **Red-team posture.** Instead of searching for support, I searched for the strongest case
   *against* each technique: "does the testing effect transfer?", "when is blocking better than
   interleaving?", "is cognitive load theory falsifiable?", "are education effect sizes
   inflated?" A technique that survives a hostile search is trustworthy.
3. **Looked for the first dive's blind spots** — places where it may have been one-sided.

## The verdict (headline)

The first dive is **substantially sound on its core**, but it was **too confident on effect
sizes** and **one-sided on growth mindset**. Summary:

| First-dive claim | Independent verdict | Key basis |
|---|---|---|
| Retrieval practice = strongest | **CONFIRMED & strengthened** | Independent group (Weinstein 2018); 5-yr real-classroom data (Agarwal); transfer holds at d=0.40 (Pan & Rickard 2018) |
| Spacing = strongest | **CONFIRMED** | Weinstein 2018; EEF review |
| Self-explanation / elaboration | **CONFIRMED** | Weinstein 2018 lists "elaboration" |
| Worked examples + dual coding | **CONFIRMED** | Weinstein 2018 ("concrete examples", "dual coding"); EEF |
| Learning ≠ performance (the foundation) | **CONFIRMED** | Soderstrom & Bjork (2015), independent flagship review |
| Interleaving (with "don't start too early") | **CONFIRMED & sharpened** | Block first to a threshold, *then* interleave; novices need initial blocking |
| Adapt to the learner (expertise reversal) | **CONFIRMED & deepened** | Aptitude–treatment interactions: prior knowledge & working-memory capacity moderate everything |
| Growth mindset "near zero" | **CORRECTED** → small but *real* for at-risk students | Yeager 2019 (Nature), largest RCT: +0.10 GPA for lower-achievers in supportive schools |
| The stated effect sizes themselves | **QUALIFIED DOWNWARD** | Publication bias: 800 education meta-analyses avg d=0.40 vs. pre-registered RCTs avg d=0.06 |
| Lab findings → classroom | **QUALIFIED** | EEF: real-classroom evidence is "limited"; but Agarwal's classroom data is positive |
| Cognitive Load Theory as framework | **QUALIFIED** | "Germane load" was unfalsifiable; Sweller himself dropped it as a separate load |

## What this means for the engine

- **The ranking is robust.** Retrieval and spacing remain the top two under a hostile,
  independent review — and they are also the two with the *best real-classroom* evidence. The
  engine is built on the right foundation.
- **Be humbler about magnitudes.** Treat the cited effect sizes as *optimistic upper bounds*,
  not promises. Real-world gains are smaller. This doesn't change *what* to do — it changes how
  loudly we should claim it.
- **Two specific fixes** are recommended for the first dive (see `04-corrections...`): soften
  the growth-mindset dismissal, and add the publication-bias caveat to the overview. These are
  proposed, not yet applied to `../research/` — your call.
