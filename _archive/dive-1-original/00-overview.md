# Research Overview — The Science of Teaching and Learning

This folder is the **evidence base** for the `ece'` teaching engine. Nothing enters the
teaching manual unless it is recorded and justified here first.

## The evidentiary bar (non-negotiable)

A technique is included **only** if it clears all three:

1. **Replicated empirical support** — meta-analyses or multiple independent studies, not a
   single paper and never a popular book's claim alone.
2. **A real, measured effect size** — it must *measurably* improve learning, not merely
   feel productive.
3. **Measured on learning outcomes** — retention, transfer, comprehension. **Not**
   "engagement," "focus," "self-rated confidence," or satisfaction.

Exclusions are themselves justified with evidence. We write down *why* something is out.

## How the files relate

- **01 — How learning works.** The cognitive architecture (working memory, long-term
  memory, cognitive load, retrieval vs. storage strength). This is the *why* that every
  technique below is derived from.
- **02 — Evidence-based learning techniques.** The techniques a learner/teacher applies.
- **03 — Teaching methods.** How to structure instruction and sequence guidance.
- **04 — Assessment & feedback.** How to measure understanding and respond to it.
- **05 — Motivation & metacognition** (plus the explicitly excluded myths).
- **sources.md** — full citations.

## The single most important idea

Across all of this, one principle recurs: **learning is caused by effortful retrieval and
reconstruction, not by exposure.** Re-reading and watching feel productive because they are
*fluent* — but fluency is an illusion of competence. The methods that work are the ones that
make the learner *generate, retrieve, and struggle a little* (Bjork's "desirable
difficulties"). The teaching engine is built around manufacturing that productive struggle.
