# A3 — Kinds of Information (the hub)

> **Why this unit is the hub:** "learning depends on what is being learned" (title of CRK22).
> Every method in A4 and every teacher in Part B is attached to a row of the table in §3.
> **Threshold concept 3:** the best method depends on the kind of information.
> Confidence tags: `settled` / `contested` / `uncertain` / `suspect`. Source IDs → `sources.md`.

---

## 1. Why one taxonomy isn't enough

Four research traditions sort "kinds of information", each for a different purpose:
| Tradition | Sorts by | Gives us |
|---|---|---|
| Krathwohl 2002 (K02) — revised Bloom | *What* the knowledge is about | The row labels (readable, widely used) |
| Gagné 1984 (G84) | What the learner can *do* afterwards | The rule that methods generalize *within* a category, not between categories |
| KLI — Koedinger et al. 2012 (KCP12, restated in CRK22) | How the knowledge is *applied* (fixed or variable situation and response) | The link from kind of knowledge to learning process to method |
| Memory systems (Sq04), skill stages (A82, FP67), element interactivity (S10, CKS17), SOLO (BC82) | Where it's stored, how it matures, how hard it is to hold, how connected it is | The *mechanism*: why the methods differ |

The table in §3 combines them. The S1 decision is confirmed in this sprint against full text of
K02, G84 and CRK22.

## 2. The building blocks, verified

### 2.1 Krathwohl's knowledge dimension (K02, full text) `settled` as a classification
| Type | Definition (verbatim) | Subtypes |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Factual** | "The basic elements that students must know to be acquainted with a discipline or solve problems in it" | terminology; specific details and elements |
| **B. Conceptual** | "The interrelationships among the basic elements within a larger structure that enable them to function together" | classifications and categories; principles and generalizations; theories, models and structures |
| **C. Procedural** | "How to do something; methods of inquiry, and criteria for using skills, algorithms, techniques, and methods" | subject-specific skills and algorithms; techniques and methods; **criteria for when to use which procedure** |
| **D. Metacognitive** | "Knowledge of cognition in general as well as awareness and knowledge of one's own cognition" | strategic; knowledge about cognitive tasks (including contextual and conditional knowledge); self-knowledge |

- It is crossed with six cognitive processes: Remember, Understand, Apply, Analyze, Evaluate,
  Create (K02 Table 3).
- Krathwohl **relaxed the strict ladder**: categories may overlap; for example, *explaining*
  (Understand) can be more complex than *executing* (Apply) (K02, p. 215).
- Note: conceptual knowledge is *defined* as relationships among the basic elements. Concepts
  are built out of facts, not opposed to them.

### 2.2 Gagné's learned capabilities (G84, full text) `settled` as a classification / `contested` (dated)
- Five outcomes (verbatim): "(a) intellectual skills (procedural knowledge), (b) verbal information
  (declarative knowledge), (c) cognitive strategies (executive control processes), (d) motor
  skills, and (e) attitudes."
- **The key claim:** "results indicating the effects on learning of most principal independent
  variables can be generalized within these categories but not between them."
  - This is the oldest version of "the method depends on the kind of information".
  - Each category differs in "(a) substantive type of relevant prior learning, (b) manner of
    encoding for long-term storage, and (c) requirements for retrieval and transfer."
- Intellectual skills = concepts, rules and procedures, which Gagné equates with procedural
  knowledge (condition → action rules: "IF the goal is to generate a plural… THEN…").
  - So for Gagné, *concepts* are a skill: applying a category to new instances, not reciting a
    definition.
- `contested` (dated): pre-dates most cognitive-load and memory-systems work. Kept for the
  generalization rule and for the two rows Krathwohl lacks (motor skills, attitudes).

### 2.3 KLI knowledge components (CRK22 full text, restating KCP12) `settled` (framework) / `contested` (evidence base still small)
- A **knowledge component (KC)** = "a stable unit of cognitive function that is acquired and modifiable".
- KCs are classified by **application condition** and **response**:
  - **Constant–constant** = facts. "The capital of France is Paris": one situation, one answer,
    verbatim retrieval.
  - **Variable–variable** = skills. Equation solving: many different problems trigger it, the
    answer differs each time, and it "require[s] creating generalizations beyond the studied
    information".
  - Mixed types exist: variable condition with constant response, e.g. classifying (many
    instances → one category label), as in "category label" in CRK22.
- **Three learning processes:**
  - memory & fluency building
  - induction & refinement
  - understanding & sense-making
- **Link to instruction (verbatim, CRK22):** "For simple constant KCs such as facts, memory and
  fluency processes are more relevant. Conversely, for variable KCs such as skills, induction and
  refinement processes are more relevant… e.g., retrieval practice for facts and studying
  examples for skills."
- **Direct test (CRK22):**
  - The type of concept interacted with the type of training (d = 0.38, p = .038): extra
    practice favoured facts, extra example study favoured skills.
  - The benefit was not tied to the question type on the final test.
  - Single study, moderate effect → `contested` until replicated.

### 2.4 Mechanism columns
- **Memory system (Sq04, A2 §3):**
  - Facts and concepts are *declarative* (conscious, can form fast, fragile without retrieval).
  - Fluent skills and perceptual tuning are largely *nondeclarative* (gradual, expressed in performance).
- **Skill maturation (A82, FP67):**
  - Procedures start declarative (you follow remembered steps: slow, effortful, error-prone).
  - With practice they are compiled into direct condition → action rules (fast, automatic).
  - Motor skills follow the parallel cognitive → associative → autonomous stages.
  - So "procedural" is a *trajectory*, not a fixed state. `settled`
- **Element interactivity (S10, CKS17):**
  - How many elements must be held in working memory *together* to understand the material.
  - It is **not a property of the material alone** `settled`. CKS17's abstract (read S11a): the
    element interactivity effect "relies on changes in element interactivity due to changes in
    the type of material being learned, while the expertise reversal effect also relies on changes
    in relative levels of element interactivity but in this case, due to changes in relative levels
    of expertise." (The earlier "intrinsic load is determined by an interaction…" sentence came
    from a secondary report and has been dropped as a quote.)
  - The same circuit-analysis step is many interacting elements for a novice and one chunk for an expert.
- **SOLO level (BC82):** the *depth of structure* a response shows: one point → several
  unconnected points → points related into a whole → generalized to new situations. It describes
  how *connected* knowledge is, so it measures understanding quality inside any row.

Cell confidence:
- Rows 1, 3, 4: `settled` (K02 + G84 + CRK22/KCP12 + A82 agree).
- Row 2: `settled` (checked S11a at the KLI full-text manuscript, v5.00 "accepted with revisions",
  cited as KCP12): categories are **variable-condition, constant-response KCs** ("category-
  recognition rules with many-to-one mappings"), and **induction and refinement** processes are
  the ones "relevant to Variable-Constant and Variable-Variable KCs", working on the *condition*
  part ("when does this apply?"). Those processes are **non-verbal** — learners may induce
  features they cannot state — which is why varied examples beat a stated definition alone.
  Perceptual chunking is listed among the same mechanisms (→ row 7).
- Row 5: `uncertain` as a separate row. It is Krathwohl's Cc/Db and Gagné's cognitive strategies;
  split out because the learner's exams and Part B methods (interleaving, contrasting cases) target
  it directly.
- Row 7: `contested` (upgraded from `uncertain`, S11a). The *existence* of perceptual learning in
  complex symbolic domains is supported: KMS10 (abstract read) reports three maths PLMs —
  matching across representations, seeing equation structure "but not solving equations" →
  faster equation solving, and transfer to novel measurement/fraction problems; MPDK16 (class-
  randomized efficacy study, 6th grade, poster full text) found gains in 2–3 of 4 modules (max
  ES .48) with a significant 1-year delayed composite. **Not yet settled:** both come from the
  developers' lab and the RCT is not peer-reviewed (backlog).
- Row 8: `settled` (FP67, Sq04).

### 3.1 How each row fails (the symptom tells you the row)
| Row | Typical failure | What it looks like | Mechanism (source) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Facts | Not retrievable when needed | "I know I saw it…"; fine with notes, blank without | Low retrieval strength; no retrieval practice or spacing (BB92, A1) |
| 2 Concepts | Classifies by surface features | Calls two resistors "in series" because they sit in a line on the page | Novices categorize by surface, experts by deep structure (CFG81 → A5); too few varied examples |
| 3 Principles | Can state it, can't use or explain it | Recites KVL, can't say why it holds or apply it to an odd circuit | Unconnected pieces = multistructural, not relational (BC82) |
| 4 Procedures | Can follow, can't produce; or slow and error-prone | Understands the worked solution, stalls on a fresh problem | Still at the declarative stage; not compiled by practice (A82) |
| 5 Conditional | Right method, wrong situation | Uses mesh analysis where nodal is two lines | Methods practised in blocks, never chosen between (KoB08 logic → A4 interleaving) |
| 6 Metacognitive | Confident and wrong | Rereads to "learn", predicts a high score, scores low | Fluency mistaken for learning (A1 §6, KoB08) |
| 7 Perceptual | Slow to "see" the structure | Has to reason step by step through what an expert sees at a glance | Too little classified exposure (KMS10, MPDK16; `contested`) |
| 8 Motor | Inconsistent execution | Joints sometimes good, sometimes cold | Still at the cognitive/associative stage (FP67) |

## 4. Biologically primary vs secondary (Geary, G08) — a cross-cutting lens, not a column `contested`

- **Primary knowledge:** what humans evolved to acquire without instruction (native language,
  face recognition, basic social and physical intuitions). Easy, fast, motivated.
- **Secondary knowledge:** culturally invented material (reading, mathematics, circuit theory).
  Effortful, needs explicit instruction, and is constrained by working memory.
- **Why it's a lens rather than a column:** every row of §3, as taught in school or university, is
  secondary. The distinction explains why none of it "comes naturally" and why methods that work
  for primary learning (immersion, discovery through play) do not carry over automatically.
  Sweller's commentary (same issue) uses it to justify explicit instruction for secondary content
  (→ A4, M17).
- `contested`: the evolutionary framing is a theory, not directly testable for each skill.
  Treat it as an explanatory lens, not as evidence for a specific method.

## 5. What this changes (the payoff)

1. **"What's the best study method?" has no answer without "for which row?"** (G84 within- vs
   between-category rule; CRK22 interaction).
   - Retrieval practice for row 1 and for the knowledge part of row 6.
   - Worked examples, then practice, for row 4.
   - Many varied, mixed examples for rows 2, 5 and 7.
   - Explanation and sense-making for row 3.
   - Full mapping and evidence grades in **A4**.
2. **Most subjects mix rows.** A circuits chapter has facts (units), concepts (series vs parallel),
   principles (KVL), procedures (mesh analysis) and conditional knowledge (which method when).
   Diagnose the row before choosing the method.
3. **A row is relative to the learner.** Element interactivity drops as chunks form (CKS17).
   What needs a worked example today may need only practice next month (expertise reversal → A4).
4. **Row 3 is where "understanding" lives, and its quality is measured by SOLO level**, not by
   whether facts can be recalled. This is the formal version of Justin Sung's "higher-order
   learning" (J2, J6) → audit in S10.

## 7. Uses
- **As a learner:** before choosing how to study a chapter, sort its content into the §3 rows.
  Match methods row by row (A4), not one method for the whole chapter.
- **As a teacher:** write objectives as row + process ("classify connections as series or
  parallel" = row 2, Understand/Classify). Diagnose which row a student is failing on before
  re-teaching.
- **For the engine:** the ROUTE step (procedure → worked example; concept → productive failure)
  is a two-row version of this table. A3 suggests ROUTE could key on all rows. → **S12 engine
  proposal candidate** (design sign-off first; no edit).
