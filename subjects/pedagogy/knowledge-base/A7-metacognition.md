# A7 — Metacognition and Self-Regulated Learning

> **Information type:** A3 row 6 (metacognitive). Unlike other rows, it *steers* how every other
> row gets studied: what to study next, when to stop, which method to use.
> Builds on A1 §6 (learners cannot feel which method works). Engine principle 5 ("make
> self-assessment honest") is this unit.
> Confidence tags: `settled` / `contested` / `uncertain` / `suspect`. Source IDs → `sources.md`.

## 1. Two jobs: monitoring and control `settled`

- **Monitoring** = judging your own learning ("Do I know this? Will I remember it on Friday?").
- **Control** = acting on that judgment ("Study this again", "Stop", "Switch method").
- **Why it matters:** control can only be as good as monitoring. A wrong judgment leads to a
  wrong decision, even with a good method available.
- **Example (DR12):** students who judged themselves to know definitions they didn't stopped
  studying those items too early, and that predicted lower retention. Overconfidence costs
  learning *through* control. (Numbers `uncertain` — abstract not available; claim via citing
  papers.)

## 2. Why monitoring fails `settled`

- **Learners hold a faulty model of how they learn** and so misjudge and mismanage study (BDK13).
- **Judgments lean on the wrong cues:**
  - current performance during study — what A1 calls performance, not learning (SB15)
  - the feeling of fluency — how easily the material comes now (BDK13; A1 §3: retrieval
    strength, not storage strength)
- **Feedback does not easily fix it.** In KoB08, most learners still preferred massing after
  their own test showed spacing worked better (A1 §6).
- **Mechanism (A1 + A2):** right after study the material sits in working memory or has high
  retrieval strength. It feels known. That feeling predicts tomorrow's recall poorly.

## 3. The fix that works: judge after a delay, by trying to retrieve `settled`

- **Delayed-JOL effect (RT11, meta):** judging "will I remember this?" a little *after* study,
  rather than immediately, makes judgments much better at telling known from unknown items:
  **relative accuracy g = 0.93** (112 effect sizes, N = 4,554). Memory itself gains slightly
  (g = 0.08, 98 ES).
- **Mechanism:** a delayed judgment involves a retrieval attempt from long-term memory, which
  matches the conditions of the real test. An immediate judgment reads what is still active
  in working memory.
  - Boundary (`uncertain`, secondary): the gain appears when the judgment is made from the cue
    alone (so the learner must try to recall the answer), not when the answer is visible.
- **Practical form:** don't ask "Do I get this?" with the page open. Close it, wait, and try to
  produce the answer. A delayed self-test is the honest monitor. That is retrieval practice used
  for monitoring as well as learning.

## 4. Replace the feeling with a criterion `settled` (principle) · `uncertain` (exact numbers)

- **Successive relearning (RDS13):** practise retrieval until each item is recalled correctly
  (a criterion), then relearn it to criterion again in later sessions. Tested in a real course,
  it improved exam scores and long-term retention compared with students' own study.
- **Why this is a metacognitive fix:** the decision to stop no longer rests on a feeling. It
  rests on a demonstrated recall, repeated across days. One correct recall in a session shows
  current retrieval strength only (A1 §3); repeated recall across spaced sessions is evidence
  of storage.
- Exact gains (e.g. "≈40% higher a month later") are from a later review by the same group →
  verify S11.

## 5. Self-regulated learning: the whole cycle `settled` (framework) · `uncertain` (training size)

- **Zimmerman's cycle (Z02; phase labels at secondary level, full text S11):**
  1. **Forethought** — analyse the task, set goals, plan strategies; motivation beliefs
     (self-efficacy, interest).
  2. **Performance** — self-control (using the strategies) and self-observation (tracking what
     happens).
  3. **Self-reflection** — judge the result, attribute causes, react; this feeds the next
     forethought.
- **Can it be taught?** A meta-analysis of school interventions (DB08) reports positive effects
  on achievement (mean ES ≈ 0.69 from a search snippet — `uncertain`; moderators unread). Treat
  as "trainable, size unknown here" until S11.
- **Link to A3:** row 6 has two parts. *Knowledge* (that rereading creates false fluency) is
  learned like any principle. *Regulation* (actually changing behaviour mid-session) is learned
  like a skill, through practice with feedback (A3 §3 row 6).

## 6. What a well-calibrated learner does (synthesis)

| Moment | Weak monitor | Calibrated move | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Choosing a method | "This feels productive" | Choose by A3 row (A4) and check with a delayed test | KoB08, BDK13 |
| Deciding what's known | Judge with the page open | Close it, wait, recall | RT11 |
| Deciding when to stop | "I got it once" / "feels familiar" | Criterion recall, repeated on later days | RDS13 |
| After a test | "Bad luck / I'm bad at this" | Which items, which row, which method next | Z02 self-reflection |
| Planning | Hours on a timetable | Items × criterion × spaced dates | C06, RDS13 |

## 7. Uses
- **As a learner:** judge only by closed-book recall after a gap; keep a list of items recalled
  vs missed; stop an item only after criterion recall on two or more separate days.
- **As a teacher:** have students predict their score before a quiz, then compare (exposes the
  gap); teach the delayed self-test as the way to check; set criteria, not hours.
- **For the engine:** principle 5 and the "never accept 'I understand'" rule are the RT11 and
  DR12 findings in practice. The start-of-session recall is a delayed judgment by design. A7
  adds no new rule; it gives the existing ones their evidence. Possible S12 note: show the
  learner their own prediction vs result on CHECKs (calibration feedback) — evidence for
  calibration *training* not read yet → `uncertain`.
