# Matrix — Part A kinds of information × Part B teachers and methods × grade

> S12e (2026-11-16). **This is a synthesis file: it adds no new external claim.** Every cell is
> copied from a Part A or Part B section that was already verified at Stage 2 (S11a–S12d); the
> section is named in each row so a reader can go to the evidence. If this file and a source
> section ever disagree, **the section wins** and this file is the bug.

> / observational · **X** debunked or myth-wrapped. A grade with "/" = *method or package / the
> mechanism inside it*. "−" after a grade = the low end of that band.

> **5** conditional (when to use what) · **6** metacognitive · **7** perceptual · **8** motor ·
> (9 attitudes — out of scope, A3 §3).

## 3. What the matrix shows (read across, not down)

1. **Coverage is lopsided.** Rows 1, 3 and 4 have many S-graded entries; row 5 has three S
   entries (two of them maths-specific or theory); row 6 has one (Wieman, on the
   feeling-of-learning gap); **rows 7 and 8 have no S-graded teacher or method at all** — their
   best mechanism (perceptual classification; staged motor practice) rests on Part A, not on any
   tested package. A learner who needs pattern recognition (reading schematics, waveforms) is
   working from the thinnest part of the evidence.
2. **The S column is the same few mechanisms under different names.** Every S entry in rows 1–5
   is retrieval, spacing, worked examples → fading, self-explanation, interleaving, or frequent
   responses with feedback (A4 §1). Historical (B1), legendary (B3) and branded (B5) entries reach
   S only *through* those mechanisms (B1, B3, B5 patterns).
3. **Package grade < mechanism grade, almost everywhere.** Many of the 61 rows carry a split grade
   in the form "T or M package / S mechanism" (exact count to be taken in S12e-rest). The tested packages that reach S as a whole are
   few: DI (with COI), classroom quizzing, successive relearning, interleaved maths homework,
   active learning in STEM, and CLT's worked examples for novices.
4. **The X grades are all famous numbers or slogans, not mechanisms:** 2σ (Bloom), 0.4–0.7
   (formative assessment), 10,000 hours (Ericsson), pure discovery for novices (Bruner), the
   Hattie league table as a decision tool. Each has a real mechanism underneath graded M or S.
5. **Many rows mean "frame", not "method".** Bjork, Bloom, Wiliam, Nuthall, Vygotsky and Gagné
   apply to every row; they tell you *how to think about* a lesson, and their own testable claims
   are graded separately.
6. **Same method, different row, different grade.** Interleaving is S for maths problem types
   (row 5), M for categories in general (row 2), and reversed for word lists (row 1 — BR19).
   Productive failure is M for principles (row 3) and ≈0 for procedures (SK21 procedural g −0.03).
   A grade belongs to a *method × row* pair, never to a method alone (M94).

## 4. Uses

- **As a learner:** find your material's row in §1, use the *mechanism* line first, and pick any
  teacher's package only for its S parts. For rows 7–8, use high-volume practice with feedback
  and treat any branded system as untested.
- **As a teacher:** §1 tells you which named method fits the lesson's information type; §2 tells
  you what to tell colleagues when they cite a name ("which part, for which kind of material?").
- **For this engine:** the engine's loop already uses every S mechanism in rows 1–5. The two gaps
  the matrix exposes are row 7 (no perceptual-classification routine in the teaching loop) and
  row 6 (calibration practice is `uncertain` in A4). Both are **proposals for the learner**, not
  edits (design-first rule); logged for the S13 audit note.

## Misconceptions owned (matrix)
- M94 — A method graded "strong" works for every kind of material.
- M95 — If many famous teachers used a method, that adds up to strong evidence.
- M96 — The best-known name for a method is the version with the evidence.
