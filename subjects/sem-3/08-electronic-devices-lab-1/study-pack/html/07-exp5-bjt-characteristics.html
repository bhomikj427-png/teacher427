What you already have: a diode is one pn junction (Exp 1). It conducts forward above ≈ 0.7 V.

Three terms first:

  • BJT (bipolar junction transistor): three layers, n-p-n for an NPN. The layers are the emitter (E, heavily doped), the base (B, very thin, lightly doped) and the collector (C). That makes two junctions: base–emitter (BE) and base–collector (BC).
  • Common emitter (CE): the emitter is shared by the input (B–E) and the output (C–E).
  • h-parameters: four numbers that describe how the transistor responds to small changes. Exp 6–8 amplifiers are built on them.
Manual Exp 5, aim

To plot the input and output characteristics of an NPN transistor in CE configuration and determine h-parameters from the characteristics. Viva form: "Your output curves are flat lines spaced 5.5 mA apart for 20 µA steps of base current. What is hfe, and what does a flat line tell you?"

Guess first

Commit to a number for hfe, and to a one-word meaning of "flat", before reading on.

The BJT in five lines

  1. Forward-bias the BE junction (VBE ≈ 0.6–0.7 V). The emitter injects electrons into the base.
  2. The base is thin, so almost all of them cross it and are swept into the collector by the reverse-biased BC junction. A few recombine in the base: that is the base current.
  3. So a small base current controls a large collector current: IC = β IB, with β ≈ 100–300 (≈ 290 for the BC547B).
  4. KCL: IE = IB + IC.
  5. Which region the transistor is in depends on how the two junctions are biased:
Region BE junction BC junction Behaviour Used as
Active forward reverse IC = βIB, nearly independent of VCE amplifier (Exps 6–8, 11)
Saturation forward forward VCE ≈ 0.2 V, IC < βIB closed switch
Cut-off reverse reverse IC ≈ 0 open switch

The two families of curves

  • Input characteristic: IB against VBE, at fixed VCE. It is just the BE diode's forward curve.
  • Output characteristic: IC against VCE, at fixed IB. One curve per IB.

h-parameters in CE form (small changes, written in lowercase):

vbe = hie ib + hre vce   ic = hfe ib + hoe vce
Parameter Name Read from Formula
hie (= h11) input resistance input curve, VCE fixed ΔVBE/ΔIB
hre (= h12) reverse voltage gain input curves, IB fixed ΔVBE/ΔVCE
hfe (= h21) forward current gain output curves, VCE fixed ΔIC/ΔIB
hoe (= h22) output admittance one output curve, IB fixed ΔIC/ΔVCE

The manual swaps the names for h21 and h22, and writes "1/hoe = ΔIC/ΔVCE". The table above is the correct version. ΔI/ΔV is a conductance, so hoe is in siemens and 1/hoe is the output resistance.

The circuit, and one fix

Trap: the 62 kΩ makes the manual's table impossible

With 62 kΩ in the collector, IC ≤ 10 V / 62 kΩ = 0.16 mA. The transistor saturates as soon as IB passes about 0.6 µA, so "VCE" is no longer the 10 V you set, and the IB = 20/40 µA curves of the observation table can never appear. That is why the manual's own output plot only reaches about 210 µA. The fix: replace R2 with a wire so Vce sits directly on the collector, and drive the base with a current source for the output curves. The device and the directives stay the same.

Input characteristics:

  1. Draw Fig 5.1 with R2 replaced by a wire. Q1: Pick New Transistor → BC547B. Label the base node base.

  3. Run, click the base lead to plot Ib(Q1), then right-click the x-axis and set it to V(base).

Sense check: hre is tiny (the output barely affects the input), and hoe is small (the flat lines tilt only slightly). That is why simple amplifier analysis often drops both.

Write in your record

Result (BC547B, CE): hie ≈ 1.57 kΩ, hre ≈ 4.2 × 10⁻⁵, hoe ≈ 89 µS, hfe ≈ 272. State the operating point used: VCE = 5 V, IB ≈ 20 µA.

Theory for the record: a BJT has emitter, base and collector regions forming two junctions. For amplification the BE junction is forward-biased and the BC junction reverse-biased (active region): carriers injected from the emitter cross the thin base and reach the collector, so IC = βIB and IE = IB + IC. In CE configuration, the input characteristic (IB vs VBE at constant VCE) resembles a forward diode curve. The output characteristics (IC vs VCE at constant IB) show saturation, active and cut-off regions. The h-parameters hie, hre, hfe, hoe are the slopes of these curves at the operating point and form the small-signal model.

Check 1

(a) Using the table, find hfe at VCE = 10 V between IB = 40 and 60 µA. (b) Why is the output curve for IB = 0 flat at zero? (c) Which region is the transistor in at VCE = 0.1 V and IB = 40 µA, and how can you tell?

Answers

Opening question. hfe = 5.5 mA / 20 µA ≈ 275. "Flat" means IC does not depend on VCE: the transistor acts as a current source controlled by IB. That is the active region, and the slight tilt is hoe.

Check 1. (a) (17.64 − 12.16) mA / 20 µA = 5.48 mA / 20 µA = 274. (b) With no base current, the BE junction isn't injecting carriers, so the transistor is cut off and only a leakage current (nA) flows. (c) Saturation. VCE is below about 0.2–0.3 V, the steep left-hand part of the curve, where IC is less than βIB and depends strongly on VCE.
