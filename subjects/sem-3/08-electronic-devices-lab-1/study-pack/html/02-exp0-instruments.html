What you already have: voltage, current, and a sine wave v(t) = Vp sin(2πft) from C&S.

Three terms first:

  • Peak Vp: the sine's height above its centre line.
  • Peak-to-peak Vpp: top to bottom, = 2Vp.
  • RMS: the steady DC value that would heat a resistor equally. For a sine, Vrms = Vp/√2.
Manual Exp 0, aim

Generation and measurement of the DC voltage and AC signals. Viva form: "The DSO shows 4 divisions peak-to-peak at 2 V/div, and one cycle spans 4 divisions at 0.25 ms/div. Give Vpp, Vrms and f."

Guess first

Write your three numbers before reading on.

The four instruments

Instrument What it does The knobs that matter
DC power supply gives a steady voltage voltage knob; current-limit knob (set it low to protect the circuit); + and − terminals
Function generator gives a sine, square or triangle wave waveform buttons, frequency range + fine knob, amplitude, DC offset (keep at 0 unless asked)
DSO (digital storage oscilloscope) draws voltage against time VOLTS/DIV (vertical scale), TIME/DIV (horizontal scale), coupling DC/AC, trigger, Auto-set, Measure menu
DMM (digital multimeter) gives one number: V, A or Ω knob position; black lead in COM; red lead in VΩmA (or 10A for currents above 200 mA)

Reading the screen (the answer to the question)

  • Volts: Vpp = (divisions top to bottom) × (VOLTS/DIV) = 4 × 2 = 8 V. So Vp = 4 V.
  • RMS of a sine: Vrms = Vp/√2 = 4/1.414 = 2.83 V. This is what a DMM on AC volts should show.
  • Time: T = (divisions per cycle) × (TIME/DIV) = 4 × 0.25 ms = 1 ms, so f = 1/T = 1 kHz.

Trap: the manual's RMS formula

The manual writes Vrms = Vp/√2 but has you read "Vp" as peak-to-peak. Divide peak by √2, or peak-to-peak by 2√2. Using Vpp/√2 gives an RMS twice too large.

Trap: the meter and the wave shape

A basic DMM is calibrated for sine waves. On a square or triangle wave it can read wrong unless it says "True RMS". Basic meters also lose accuracy at higher frequencies, so keep the generator at about 1 kHz or lower for the meter comparison.

Do it (hands-on)

DC:

  1. Set the supply to a value (e.g. 5 V), with the current limit low.
  2. Connect the DSO probe tip to + and the probe's ground clip to −. Coupling DC.
  3. Count the divisions the trace rises from the 0 V line, and multiply by VOLTS/DIV.
  4. Read the same output with the DMM on DC volts.

AC:

  1. Connect the generator output to the DSO (probe tip to signal, ground clip to ground).
  2. Press Auto-set, or set VOLTS/DIV and TIME/DIV so that 1–2 cycles fill the screen.
  3. Count Vpp divisions and the divisions in one period T. Calculate Vrms and f.
  4. Measure the same output with the DMM on AC volts.
  5. Repeat for the square and triangle waves.
Write in your record

Observation table columns: wave shape · divisions for Vpp · VOLTS/DIV · Vpp · divisions for T · TIME/DIV · T · f = 1/T · Vrms (calculated) · Vrms (DMM).

Result: average error of the DSO reading = (|DMM − DSO| / DMM) × 100 averaged over the readings.

Theory for the record: a function generator produces sine, square and triangle voltages of set amplitude and frequency. A DSO samples the input, stores it digitally and draws voltage against time on a grid. Amplitude = divisions × VOLTS/DIV, and period = divisions × TIME/DIV, with f = 1/T. A DMM measures DC/AC voltage, current and resistance. On AC it displays RMS, which for a sine equals Vpeak/√2. A regulated DC supply holds its output constant against changes in load and line.

Check 1

(a) A triangle wave is 6 V peak-to-peak. What is its RMS? (b) The probe's ground clip is left unconnected. What does the trace typically look like? (c) Why must the DMM be on the mA port, and in series, to measure a current?

Answers

Opening question. Vpp = 8 V, Vrms = 2.83 V, f = 1 kHz.

Check 1. (a) Vp = 3 V, so Vrms = 3/√3 = 1.73 V. (b) A noisy, fuzzy trace (often showing 50 Hz mains pickup), because the probe has no reference point. (c) An ammeter must carry the same current as the branch, so it goes in series. It has a very low resistance, so it must never be placed across a source: that is a short circuit, and it blows the meter's fuse.
