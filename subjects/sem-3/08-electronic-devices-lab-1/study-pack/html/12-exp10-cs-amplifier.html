Exp 10 · JFET common-source amplifier: frequency response

What you already have: the BJT's CE amplifier (Exp 6), meaning bias, coupling and bypass capacitors, gain, and −3 dB points. The JFET's gm (Exp 9).

Two terms first:

  • Common source (CS): the FET's version of CE. Input at the gate, output at the drain, source common.
  • Self-bias: the source resistor Rs lifts the source above the gate, which makes VGS negative without a negative supply.
Manual Exp 10, aim

To plot the frequency response of a common-source amplifier. Viva form: "Estimate the gain from gm and the resistors, and say why the output is 180° out of phase."

Guess first

CE gain was RC/re. What do you think replaces 1/re for a FET?

The circuit and its bias

Part Job
RA 6.8 MΩ, RB 1 MΩ gate divider: VG = 12 × 1/7.8 ≈ 1.54 V. Megohms are fine because the gate draws no current.
Rs 2.2 kΩ self-bias: VGS = VG − IDRs is negative
Cs 100 µF bypasses Rs for AC, giving full gain (as CE did in Exp 6)
R1 3.3 kΩ drain load: converts the ID change into a voltage change
C1, C2 coupling: block DC, pass the signal

Q-point (LTspice .op): VG = 1.54 V, VS = 3.17 V, so VGS = −1.63 V. ID = 3.17/2.2k = 1.44 mA, and VD = 7.25 V. The drain sits well above the source, so the JFET is in saturation ✓.

Gain by hand: the small-signal model is a current source gmvgs into the drain.

Av = −gm (R1 ∥ R2) = −gm × (3.3 kΩ ∥ 10 kΩ) = −gm × 2.48 kΩ

For the 2N5434 at this Q-point, gm ≈ 9.7 mS (from its model: IDSS ≈ 65 mA, VP ≈ −1.9 V, using gm = (2IDSS/|VP|)(1 − VGS/VP)). So Av ≈ −24. The minus sign is the 180° phase inversion: as vgs goes up, ID goes up, and the drain voltage VDD − IDR1 goes down.

Calculation

  • Transient gain = VO(pp)/Vin(pp) = 4.73/0.2 = 23.6.
  • AC midband gain = 24.3.
  • BW = fH − fL = 4.48 MHz − 32 Hz ≈ 4.5 MHz.

The low cut-off comes from Cs (with Rs ∥ 1/gm ≈ 100 Ω) and from C2 (with R1 + R2). The high cut-off comes from the JFET's gate–drain and gate–source capacitances (35 pF each in its model), Miller-multiplied.

Write in your record

Result: maximum gain ≈ 24.3 (27.7 dB); transient gain 4.73 V/0.2 V = 23.6; output 180° out of phase; fL ≈ 32 Hz, fH ≈ 4.5 MHz, bandwidth ≈ 4.5 MHz.

Theory for the record: in a common-source amplifier the signal is applied to the gate and taken from the drain, with the source common (bypassed to AC ground). The gate divider and source resistor set a negative VGS that biases the JFET in saturation. A gate-voltage change produces a drain-current change gmvgs, which develops an output voltage across the drain load: Av = −gm(RD ∥ RL). The output is inverted (180°). The input resistance is very high (set by the gate divider). The gain is lower than a BJT CE stage, but the input resistance and noise performance are better. Coupling/bypass capacitors set fL; device capacitances set fH.

Check 1

(a) Remove Cs. What happens to the gain, and why? (b) Why can the gate resistors be megohms here, when the BJT's bias resistors were kilohms? (c) Estimate the input resistance seen by the source.

Answers

Opening question. gm replaces 1/re: Av = −gm(RD ∥ RL) ≈ −24. The output is inverted because a rising gate voltage raises ID, which increases the drop across R1 and lowers the drain voltage.

Check 1. (a) The gain drops: Rs now gives negative feedback (as in Exp 8). Av ≈ −gmRD'/(1 + gmRs) = −24/(1 + 9.7m × 2.2k) ≈ −1.1. (b) The JFET gate takes no current, so the divider is not loaded. A BJT's base current would make megohm resistors drop volts and wreck the bias. (c) Rin ≈ RA ∥ RB = 6.8 MΩ ∥ 1 MΩ ≈ 0.87 MΩ.
