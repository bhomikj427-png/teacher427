The rule: copy at the edge, then hold

At each rising edge, Q becomes whatever D is at that instant. Between edges Q holds still, whatever D does.

Four flip-flops, one clock

Each flip-flop has its own D and Q. Because they share CLK, one edge copies all four bits at once. That is a 4-bit register.

Shift registers & shift counters · Stop 3 · Shift register

Everything moves one place

Each flip-flop copies its neighbour's old value, because all of them sample at the same edge (stop 1). So the 1 moves one place: 0 1 0 0.

Shift registers & shift counters · Stop 4 · Ring counter

The 1 goes round and round

1000 → 0100 → 0010 → 0001 → 1000. Four flip-flops, four states: a mod-4 counter. Each Q is high in exactly one state, so no extra gates are needed to read it.

Shift registers & shift counters · Stop 4 · Ring counter

One catch

It must start with exactly one 1. Start at 0000 and the zeros circulate forever: it never counts. Real designs preset it to 1000 at power-on.

Two neighbouring bits are enough

Look at state 1100 in the table: it is the only state where Q1 = 1 and Q2 = 0. So a 2-input AND decodes it. Every Johnson state has a pair like this.

Shift registers & shift counters · Stop 6 · Decode + lock-out

The other 8 patterns

4 bits have 16 patterns and Johnson uses 8. The other 8 form their own loop. Start in one (say 0100 after a power glitch) and the counter never returns. Fix: reset to 0000 at power-on.
