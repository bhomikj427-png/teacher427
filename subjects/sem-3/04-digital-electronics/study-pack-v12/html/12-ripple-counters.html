12 — Asynchronous (ripple) counters

The cheapest counter: chain toggling flip-flops so each clocks the next. It costs no gates, but its outputs settle one after another. 13 removes that delay.

Build

1 · Ripple up and down counters

Predict

FF0 toggles on every falling clock edge. FF1 toggles whenever Q0 falls. What sequence does Q1Q0 go through?

Terms

Counter: steps through a fixed sequence, one step per clock pulse. Asynchronous (ripple): only the first flip-flop gets the clock; each other is clocked by the one before.

  • Every flip-flop toggles (T = 1 or J = K = 1). Reading Q2Q1Q0: 0, 1, 2, … 7, 0: an up counter. Each Q is half the frequency of the one before: a frequency divider.
  • Clock each stage from Q' instead and it counts down (with positive-edge flip-flops the rule swaps).
  • The flaw, ripple delay: each stage waits for the one before. 011 → 100 passes through 010 and 000. Worst settling time n·tpd; the clock period must exceed it, and anything reading the outputs sees false states.

Answer. 00, 01, 10, 11, 00: it counts up, and Q1 runs at a quarter of the clock frequency.

Check 1

A 4-bit ripple counter uses flip-flops with tpd = 25 ns. Worst settling time and highest usable clock frequency?

2 · MOD-N by reset, and cascading

Deck 10 slides 12–13

Design an asynchronous decade counter.

Guess first

A decade counter has 10 states. How many flip-flops, and how do you stop it going on to 10, 11, … 15?

MOD less than 2n. Smallest n with 2n ≥ MOD. Let it count normally, detect the first unwanted state with a NAND, and drive every flip-flop's active-low CLEAR. Cascading (deck 10 slides 16–17): clock a second counter from the last output of the first: MOD-M then MOD-N gives MOD-(M × N).

Answer. 8 < 10 ≤ 16: 4 flip-flops, all toggling, each clocked by the Q before it. The first unwanted state is 10 = 1010, the first with Q3 and Q1 both 1: a 2-input NAND on Q3, Q1 drives all CLEARs. The count goes 0000 … 1001, shows 1010 for a few nanoseconds, and clears to 0000.

Check 2

(a) Deck 10 slide 14 Which outputs go to the NAND for MOD-7? For MOD-12? (b) Flip-flops for MOD-60? (c) A 10 kHz clock drives a MOD-10 counter: frequency of its last output?

Exam form

Ripple counter. Circuit, waveforms, up vs down connection, divide-by-2 per stage, ripple delay n·tpd and the false states.

MOD-N. Number of flip-flops; first unwanted state; NAND → CLEAR; cascading.

Traps

  • Detecting state N − 1 instead of N.
  • Including outputs that are 0 in state N in the NAND (only the 1s go in).
  • Forgetting the momentary false state before clear.

  2. A 1 MHz clock drives a ripple counter. Frequency at Q0? At Q3?
  3. A 3-bit ripple counter with tpd = 20 ns: fmax?
  4. How do you make a ripple counter count down?
  5. MOD-6 by reset: NAND inputs?
  6. How would you build MOD-24 from smaller counters?

Answers

Check 1. 4 × 25 = 100 ns; fmax = 10 MHz.

Check 2. (a) MOD-7: clear at 111, Q2, Q1, Q0. MOD-12: clear at 1100, Q3, Q2. (b) 32 < 60 ≤ 64: 6. (c) 1 kHz.

Self-test.

  1. 3 flip-flops; clear at 101: NAND on Q2, Q0.
  2. 500 kHz; 62.5 kHz.
  3. 60 ns: 16.7 MHz.
  4. Clock each stage from the previous Q' (negative-edge flip-flops).
  5. Clear at 110: Q2, Q1.
  6. Cascade MOD-3 and MOD-8 (or MOD-4 and MOD-6): 3 × 8 = 24.
