1 · The yardsticks: noise margin, fan-out, delay, power

Deck 14 slides 4–12

Define noise margin and fan-out. For standard TTL (VOH(min) = 2.4 V, VOL(max) = 0.4 V, VIH(min) = 2.0 V, VIL(max) = 0.8 V; IOH = 400 μA, IOL = 16 mA, IIH = 40 μA, IIL = 1.6 mA) calculate both.

Guess first

A gate promises its HIGH output is at least 2.4 V and accepts anything above 2.0 V as HIGH. Why the gap?

Terms

VOH(min), VOL(max): worst output levels guaranteed. VIH(min), VIL(max): input thresholds guaranteed. IOH, IOL: current an output supplies HIGH and sinks LOW. IIH, IIL: current one input takes.

NMH = VOH(min) − VIH(min)   NML = VIL(max) − VOL(max)

Fan-out = the smaller of IOL/IIL and IOH/IIH. Fan-in = number of inputs.

Yardstick Definition
Propagation delay tp input change to output change at 50% points; average of tPHL and tPLH
Rise, fall time 10% → 90% and 90% → 10% of the swing
Power V × average supply current; static + dynamic (grows with f)
Speed–power product tp × power: energy per switch, smaller is better
Integration SSI < 12 gates, MSI 12–99, LSI 100–9999, VLSI ≥ 10 000

Answer. NMH = 0.4 V, NML = 0.4 V. Fan-out: 16/1.6 = 10 and 400/40 = 10: 10. With tp ≈ 10 ns and 10 mW, speed–power = 100 pJ.

2 · The TTL NAND gate

Deck 14 slides 24–25

Draw the circuit of a standard TTL NAND gate with totem-pole output and explain its operation. Why must two totem-pole outputs not be connected together?

Guess first

The output stage has one transistor to pull up and one to pull down. What if both conduct at once?

  • Q1, one emitter per input, does the AND.
  • Any input LOW: Q1's base current leaves through that emitter, Q2 is OFF, Q4 OFF; Q3 gets base current and pulls the output HIGH through the diode.
  • All inputs HIGH: Q1's base current flows through its collector into Q2; Q2 ON drives Q4 into saturation: output LOW. The diode keeps Q3 OFF.
  • Totem pole: active pull-up and pull-down: low output resistance both ways, fast.

A floating TTL input acts HIGH but picks up noise: tie unused inputs. Sub-families: 74, 74L, 74H, 74S (Schottky clamp stops saturation: faster), 74LS (the common one), 74AS, 74ALS.

Answer. Output = (AB)'. Join two totem poles with one HIGH and one LOW and Q3 of one and Q4 of the other short +5 V to ground: a large current, possible damage, invalid level. Shared lines use open-collector (no Q3, external pull-up; joined outputs give wired-AND) or tristate outputs.

3 · Tristate TTL

Deck 05 slides 44–48

What is tristate logic? Explain how the third state is obtained in a TTL gate, and give one use.

Guess first

How can an output be neither 0 nor 1?

The third state is high impedance (Hi-Z): the output is disconnected, neither pulling up nor down. Many outputs can share a bus if only one is enabled. Circuit: the disable signal pulls an extra emitter of Q1 LOW (Q2, Q4 OFF) and, through a diode, Q3's base LOW (Q3 OFF): both output transistors OFF.

EN IN OUT
0 X Hi-Z
1 0 0
1 1 1

Answer. Three output states (0, 1, Hi-Z), made by turning both totem-pole transistors off; used on shared data buses. It keeps the fast active pull-up; but two enabled at once fight (bus contention), so enables are decoded.

4 · ECL and the family comparison

Deck 14 slide 14

Why is ECL the fastest logic family? Compare TTL, ECL and CMOS.

Guess first

A saturated transistor holds extra charge. What must happen to it before the transistor can switch off?

RE sets a nearly constant current, steered to whichever of Q1 (input) or Q2 (reference VBB) has the higher base. Never switched off, never saturated, small swing: about 1 ns. Costs: highest power (current always flows), small noise margin (1 ≈ −0.8 V, 0 ≈ −1.7 V), negative supply (−5.2 V). Gives OR and NOR outputs.

Answer. No saturation means no storage delay, and the swing is small. The table compares the three (typical textbook values).

5 · CMOS gates and interfacing

Deck 14 slides 19–22

Draw a CMOS inverter and a 2-input CMOS NAND and explain them. Why does CMOS use almost no static power?

Guess first

Is there any steady input for which both inverter transistors are ON?

Inverter: input LOW → pMOS ON, output VDD; HIGH → nMOS ON, output 0. NAND: two nMOS in series to ground, two pMOS in parallel to VDD; NOR is the reverse; n inputs, 2n transistors.

  • Static power ≈ 0; dynamic P = CVDD2f. Full swing, large noise margin; input is a capacitor, so huge fan-out; wide supply range; dense.
  • Weak points: static-sensitive oxide; slower with large loads. Never leave an input floating.
Driving Problem Fix
TTL → CMOS (5 V) TTL HIGH may be 2.4 V; 74HC needs ≈ 3.5 V pull-up resistor, or 74HCT
CMOS → TTL CMOS must sink IIL = 1.6 mA per TTL input CMOS buffer (4049, 4050), or LS TTL
Different supplies levels differ level shifter, or open collector with pull-up

Answer. In either steady state one network is OFF, so there is no path from VDD to ground: power is used only while switching.

Traps

  • Fan-out from one state only: take the smaller of the two.
  • NML = VIL − VOL, not VOL − VIL.
  • Saying CMOS uses no power: dynamic power grows with f.
  • Floating TTL input = HIGH; floating CMOS input = undefined.
