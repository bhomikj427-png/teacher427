Every synchronous circuit is flip-flops with logic between them. How fast can its clock run? Three numbers per flip-flop and two per logic path answer it.

The questions this file answers

  • practice tpd = 8 ns, tsu = 3 ns, th = 2 ns, logic 12 ns longest and 1 ns shortest. Find fmax and check hold.
  • practice Compare the maximum clock of a ripple counter (12) and a synchronous counter (13).

Build

1 · Setup, hold and propagation delay

Predict

A D flip-flop samples at the edge. What do you think happens if D is changing at exactly that instant?

Terms

Setup time tsu: D must be steady this long before the edge. Hold time th: and this long after. Propagation delay tpd: edge to valid Q. Metastability: if setup or hold is broken, Q can hang between 0 and 1 for an unpredictable time.

Answer. The flip-flop may go metastable: an in-between output that resolves to 0 or 1 after an unknown time (17 shows the synchronizer that guards against it).

Check 1

Define tsu, th and tpd in one line each, with a sketch.

2 · fmax and the hold check

practice

A synchronous circuit has tpd = 8 ns, tsu = 3 ns, th = 2 ns, and combinational delays of 12 ns (longest path) and 1 ns (shortest). Find the maximum clock frequency and check the hold time.

Guess first

Data leaves one flip-flop at an edge. By when must it reach the next one?

Setup (longest path): launched at one edge, the data must pass the flip-flop and the slowest logic and arrive tsu before the next edge:

Tclk ≥ tpd + tcomb,max + tsu   fmax = 1/Tclk,min

Hold (shortest path): the new data must not arrive while the next flip-flop is still holding: tpd + tcomb,min ≥ th. The period is not in this inequality, so slowing the clock cannot fix a hold violation: add delay to the short path.

Answer. Tmin = 8 + 12 + 3 = 23 ns, fmax = 43.5 MHz. Hold: 8 + 1 = 9 ns ≥ 2 ns ✓.

Check 2

(a) The longest path gains 4 ns. New fmax? Does the hold check change? (b) A synchronous counter has tpd = 10 ns, one 5 ns AND gate between flip-flops, tsu = 5 ns. fmax?

Exam form

Define tsu, th, tpd, metastability with a timing sketch. Setup inequality → fmax. Hold inequality and why the clock does not appear. Ripple counter fmax = 1/(n·tpd) for comparison.

Traps

  • Leaving out tpd or tsu from the period.
  • Using the longest path for the hold check.
  • Trying to fix hold by slowing the clock.

Self-test

  1. tpd = 5 ns, longest logic 10 ns, tsu = 2 ns. fmax?
  2. tpd = 2 ns, shortest logic 0.5 ns, th = 3 ns. Is hold met? If not, what is the fix?
  3. Why can't a slower clock fix a hold violation?
  4. Compare fmax of a 4-bit ripple counter (tpd = 10 ns) and the synchronous counter of Check 2(b).

Answers

Check 1. tsu: D steady before the edge; th: D steady after it; tpd: edge to valid Q. Sketch as in step 1.

Check 2. (a) 8 + 16 + 3 = 27 ns: 37.0 MHz. Hold unchanged: it uses the shortest path. (b) 10 + 5 + 5 = 20 ns: 50 MHz.

Self-test.

  1. 17 ns: 58.8 MHz.
  2. 2.5 < 3: violated; add at least 0.5 ns of delay to the short path.
  3. Hold compares two delays after the same edge; the period is not involved.
  4. Ripple: 4 × 10 = 40 ns, 25 MHz. Synchronous: 50 MHz, and its period does not grow with the number of bits.
