Map

Files 09–13 treated a flip-flop as perfect: it copies D at the edge, instantly. File 12 already met one real delay (a ripple counter's stages settle one after another). This file gives every flip-flop three timing numbers and every logic path two, and turns them into the two rules a synchronous circuit must obey: the clock may not be too fast (setup), and the fastest path may not be too fast (hold). Files 15–17 build machines that obey them.

Symbols

Symbol What it means in this file Typical value
tpd clock-to-Q delay: time from the clock edge until Q shows its new value; set by the flip-flop's transistors 8 ns
tsu setup time: how long D must be steady before the edge for the flip-flop to read it reliably 3 ns
th hold time: how long D must stay steady after the edge 2 ns
tcomb,max, tcomb,min delay of the slowest and of the fastest gate path between two flip-flops: sum of the gate delays along it 12 ns, 1 ns
T, f clock period and frequency, f = 1/T 25 ns, 40 MHz
Tmin, fmax smallest period (largest frequency) the circuit works at 23 ns, 43.5 MHz
δ clock skew: how much later the clock edge reaches the capturing flip-flop than the launching one (negative = earlier) 0 – 3 ns

One flip-flop → logic → flip-flop path with tpd = 8 ns, tsu = 3 ns, th = 2 ns, tcomb,max = 12 ns, tcomb,min = 1 ns (KB 03 §6). Steps 1–7 all use it; step 8 applies the same sums to the counters of files 12 and 13.

Build

1 · Clock-to-Q delay: the output is late

A flip-flop is a camera pointed at its D input: at the clock edge it takes a picture, and Q shows the picture. Like a real camera, it needs a moment before the picture is ready.

Predict

A D flip-flop with tpd = 8 ns is clocked at 50 MHz. D went to 1 long before the edge at 10 ns. When does Q go to 1, and what fraction of a clock period has passed by then?

Terms

Propagation delay tpd (clock-to-Q): the time from the active clock edge until Q shows the new value; the transistors inside need it to switch, so it is fixed by the chip (data-sheet number). Clock period T: the time from one rising edge to the next, T = 1/f.

Time (ns) CLK D Q
5 0 1 0 (old value)
10 rising edge 1 0: the picture is taken, not yet shown
18 1 1 1 (10 + tpd)
25 0 1 1

Every signal that comes from a flip-flop is therefore tpd late, and anything that uses Q (the logic of step 5, the next stage of a ripple counter) starts only then.

Answer to the Predict. T = 1/50 MHz = 20 ns. Q goes to 1 at 10 + 8 = 18 ns, 40 % of the period after the edge.

Check 1

(a) The same flip-flop is clocked at 100 MHz. What fraction of the period does tpd take now? (b) Why does the next flip-flop in a chain not see the new Q at the same edge?

2 · Setup time: D must be steady before the edge

Step 1 said the camera takes the picture at the edge. A picture of something moving is blurred; the flip-flop's "picture" needs D to stand still for a moment first.

Predict

D changes 1 ns before the edge, and the flip-flop's data sheet says tsu = 3 ns. Is the new D reliably stored?

Terms

Setup time tsu: the time before the clock edge during which D must not change; inside the flip-flop the new value needs this long to reach the part that stores it.

The rule reads: the last change of D must happen at least tsu before the edge. With tsu = 3 ns and an edge at 20 ns, D may change up to 17 ns and not later. Setup is broken when the data arrives late: when the path feeding D is slow, or the clock is fast (step 5).

Answer to the Predict. No. 1 ns before the edge is inside the 3 ns setup window: the flip-flop may store the old value, the new one, or neither (step 4).

Check 2

The edge is at 40 ns and tsu = 3 ns. Which of these D changes are safe: at 30 ns, 37 ns, 38.5 ns?

3 · Hold time: D must stay steady after the edge

The camera's shutter does not close at the exact instant of the edge either: D must stay still a little after it.

Predict

D changes 1 ns after the edge, and th = 2 ns. Is the value present at the edge stored safely?

Terms

Hold time th: the time after the clock edge during which D must not change; the flip-flop's input is still being closed off during it.

Same figure as step 2: the hold part of the window runs from the edge to th after it. The rule reads: the first change of D after an edge must come at least th after that edge. Hold is broken when new data arrives too early: in step 6 this happens when the path from the previous flip-flop is very short.

D changes at (edge at 20 ns, tsu = 3, th = 2) inside the window 17–22 ns?
15 ns no: safe
18 ns yes: setup broken
21 ns yes: hold broken
23 ns no: safe (it is read at the next edge)

Answer to the Predict. No. 1 ns after the edge is inside the 2 ns hold window.

Check 3

Edge at 20 ns, tsu = 3 ns, th = 2 ns. How long is the window in which D must not change, and from when to when?

4 · Metastability: what happens when the window is broken

Steps 2 and 3 said "not reliably stored". Here is what the flip-flop actually does then.

Predict

D changes exactly at the edge. Name three things Q could do.

Terms

Metastability: the state of a flip-flop whose setup or hold was broken: Q sits at a level between 0 and 1 for a time that cannot be predicted, then falls to 0 or 1 at random.

A flip-flop stores a bit in two cross-coupled gates (file 09's latch), which settle in one of two stable states. Between them is a balance point, like a ball on top of a hill: a D change caught by the edge can leave the gates near that point, and they take an unbounded time to roll off to one side. Logic reading Q meanwhile may see 0, 1 or a level that some gates read as 1 and others as 0.

The cure is never "hope": obey setup and hold everywhere (steps 5–6), and pass a signal from outside the clock's control through a synchronizer first (file 17).

Answer to the Predict. Q may (1) take the new value, (2) keep the old one, or (3) go metastable: hang between 0 and 1 for an unknown time before resolving to either.

Check 4

Why is metastability worse than simply storing the wrong bit?

5 · The longest path sets fmax

Steps 1–2 gave two pieces: data leaves a flip-flop tpd after an edge, and must arrive at the next flip-flop tsu before the following edge. Between them sits the logic.

Predict

Running example: tpd = 8 ns, slowest logic path 12 ns, tsu = 3 ns. What is the shortest clock period that works? Will it run at 40 MHz? At 50 MHz?

Terms

Launching / capturing flip-flop: the flip-flop whose Q starts a path, and the one whose D ends it; both are clocked by the same edge train. Longest (critical) path: the slowest chain of gates between them, delay tcomb,max.

Put the pieces together, line by line:

  1. At the edge at time 0 the launching flip-flop's Q changes at tpd (step 1).
  2. The change passes the slowest logic: it reaches the capturing D at tpd + tcomb,max.
  3. The capturing flip-flop needs it tsu before its next edge, at T (step 2): tpd + tcomb,max ≤ T − tsu.
  4. So T ≥ tpd + tcomb,max + tsu, and fmax = 1 / (tpd + tcomb,max + tsu).

Read in words: one clock period must cover the flip-flop's delay, the slowest logic, and the next flip-flop's setup time. If the circuit fails setup, slowing the clock fixes it.

Clock T (ns) data ready (ns) = Tmin spare
40 MHz 25 23 2 ns: works
43.5 MHz 23 23 0: the limit
50 MHz 20 23 −3 ns: fails

Answer to the Predict. Tmin = 8 + 12 + 3 = 23 ns, fmax = 1 / 23 ns = 43.5 MHz. 40 MHz works (2 ns spare); 50 MHz fails (3 ns short).

Check 5

The longest path gains 4 ns (another gate). New Tmin and fmax?

6 · The shortest path and the hold check

Step 5 asked whether data arrives soon enough. Step 3 raises the opposite worry: at the same edge that the capturing flip-flop reads its D, the launching flip-flop starts sending the next value. It must not arrive before the hold window closes.

Predict

Running example: the fastest path from launching Q to capturing D has 1 ns of logic. tpd = 8 ns, th = 2 ns. Is hold met? Would slowing the clock help if it were not?

Terms

Shortest path: the fastest chain of gates between the two flip-flops, delay tcomb,min; it may be a plain wire (0 ns).

  1. At the edge (time 0) the capturing flip-flop reads D; it needs D unchanged until th (step 3).
  2. At the same edge the launching flip-flop's new Q appears at tpd, and through the fastest logic reaches D at tpd + tcomb,min.
  3. Safe if it arrives after the window: tpd + tcomb,min ≥ th.

Read in words: the fastest new data must not overtake the hold time. Both sides are measured from the same edge, so T does not appear: slowing the clock cannot fix a hold violation. The fix is to add delay (buffers) in the short path.

Answer to the Predict. 8 + 1 = 9 ns ≥ 2 ns: hold met, with 7 ns spare. No: the clock period is not in the inequality, so slowing the clock would not help.

Check 6

tpd = 2 ns, shortest logic 0.5 ns, th = 3 ns. Is hold met? If not, what is the fix and how much?

7 · Clock skew: the two edges do not arrive together

Steps 5 and 6 assumed both flip-flops see the edge at the same instant. On a real board the clock wire is longer to some flip-flops than to others.

Predict

The capturing flip-flop's clock arrives 3 ns after the launching one's. Does that help setup or hurt it? Does it help hold or hurt it?

Terms

Clock skew δ: how much later the clock edge reaches the capturing flip-flop than the launching one; set by wire lengths and buffers in the clock path. Positive = capture late, negative = capture early.

Redo steps 5 and 6 with the capturing edges moved δ later:

  1. Setup: the data must be ready tsu before the capturing edge, now at T + δ: tpd + tcomb,max + tsu ≤ T + δ, so T ≥ tpd + tcomb,max + tsu − δ.
  2. Hold: the capturing flip-flop's hold window now ends at δ + th: tpd + tcomb,min ≥ th + δ.

A late capture clock gives the slow data more time but keeps the hold window open longer for the fast data. An early capture clock (δ < 0) does the reverse. When the sign is unknown, designers assume the worst for each: + |δ| on the setup side and + |δ| on the hold side (UCSD CSE140 and Cornell ECE4740 timing notes, KB 03 §6).

Skew δ Tmin = 23 − δ fmax hold: 9 ≥ 2 + δ?
−3 ns 26 ns 38.5 MHz 9 ≥ −1: met
0 23 ns 43.5 MHz 9 ≥ 2: met
+3 ns 20 ns 50 MHz 9 ≥ 5: met
+8 ns 15 ns 66.7 MHz 9 ≥ 10: violated by 1 ns

At δ = +8 ns, again the clock cannot fix it: add at least 1 ns to the short path. A 2 ns buffer gives 8 + 3 = 11 ≥ 10 and leaves the slow path untouched.

Answer to the Predict. A late capture clock helps setup (Tmin falls from 23 to 20 ns, fmax rises to 50 MHz) and hurts hold (the 9 ns arrival must now beat 2 + 3 = 5 ns; it still does).

Check 7

Running example with δ = +7 ns. Is hold met? What are Tmin and fmax?

8 · Counters compared: ripple against synchronous

Files 12 and 13 built the same counts two ways. Step 5's sum now says which one can be clocked faster.

Predict

4-bit counters with tpd = 10 ns: a ripple counter (file 12), and a synchronous counter with one 5 ns AND gate before each T input and tsu = 5 ns (file 13). Which has the higher fmax, and what happens to each at 8 bits?

Terms

Settling time of a ripple counter: the time for a change to pass through all n stages, n · tpd, since each stage is clocked by the one before (file 12).

Counter What one clock period must cover 4 bits 8 bits
ripple every stage in turn: n · tpd 40 ns, 25 MHz 80 ns, 12.5 MHz
synchronous one flip-flop, one gate level, setup: tpd + tAND + tsu 20 ns, 50 MHz 20 ns, 50 MHz (if the enables stay one gate deep)

The ripple counter's period grows with every stage, because the stages wait for each other. The synchronous counter's flip-flops all switch tpd after the same edge, so its period is step 5's single path, whatever n is (as long as the AND gates feeding the T inputs stay one level deep, file 13).

Answer to the Predict. The synchronous counter: 50 MHz against 25 MHz at 4 bits; at 8 bits the ripple counter drops to 12.5 MHz while the synchronous one stays at 50 MHz.

Check 8

A 3-bit ripple counter uses flip-flops with tpd = 20 ns. What is its fmax, and what would a synchronous version with a 5 ns AND and tsu = 5 ns reach?

Exam form

Definitions (with one timing sketch: CLK, D with the shaded window, Q late by tpd). tpd: edge to valid Q. tsu: D steady before the edge. th: D steady after the edge. Metastability: setup or hold broken → Q between 0 and 1 for an unknown time, then random.

fmax (setup, longest path). 1. T ≥ tpd + tcomb,max + tsu. 2. fmax = 1 / (tpd + tcomb,max + tsu).

Hold check (shortest path). tpd + tcomb,min ≥ th; T is not in it, so the fix is delay in the short path, never a slower clock.

With skew δ (capture late = positive). T ≥ tpd + tcomb,max + tsu − δ; tpd + tcomb,min ≥ th + δ.

Counters. Ripple fmax = 1 / (n · tpd); synchronous fmax = 1 / (tpd + tgate + tsu).

Traps

  • Leaving tpd or tsu out of the period.
  • Using the longest path for the hold check (it needs the shortest).
  • Trying to fix a hold violation by slowing the clock.
  • Getting the skew sign backwards: a late capture clock helps setup and hurts hold.
  • Using one tpd for a ripple counter: it is n · tpd.

Self-test

  1. tpd = 5 ns, longest logic 10 ns, tsu = 2 ns. fmax?
  2. tpd = 2 ns, shortest logic 0.5 ns, th = 3 ns. Is hold met?
  3. Why can't a slower clock fix a hold violation?
  4. tpd = 6 ns, longest logic 9 ns, shortest 1 ns, tsu = 2 ns, th = 1 ns, capture clock 2 ns late. Find Tmin, fmax, and check hold.
  5. A design must run at 100 MHz with tpd = 4 ns and tsu = 2 ns. What is the most logic delay allowed between two flip-flops?
  6. Name the three things a flip-flop's Q can do when D changes inside the setup–hold window.

Answers

Check 1. (a) T = 10 ns: 8/10 = 80 %. (b) It reads D at the edge, and the new Q appears only tpd later; it is read at the following edge.

Check 2. Window 37–40 ns before the edge: 30 ns safe; 37 ns is exactly at the limit (safe, just); 38.5 ns breaks setup.

Check 3. tsu + th = 5 ns, from 17 ns to 22 ns.

Check 4. A wrong bit is at least a valid 0 or 1 that every gate reads the same way. A metastable Q may be read as 1 by one gate and 0 by another, and it settles at an unknown time, possibly after the next edge.

Check 5. 8 + 16 + 3 = 27 ns, fmax = 37.0 MHz. The hold check does not change: it uses the shortest path.

Check 6. 2 + 0.5 = 2.5 ns < 3 ns: violated. Add at least 0.5 ns of delay to the short path.

Check 7. Hold: 9 ≥ 2 + 7 = 9: met, with no margin. Tmin = 23 − 7 = 16 ns, fmax = 62.5 MHz.

Check 8. Ripple: 3 × 20 = 60 ns, 16.7 MHz. Synchronous: 20 + 5 + 5 = 30 ns, 33.3 MHz.

Self-test.

  1. 5 + 10 + 2 = 17 ns: 58.8 MHz.
  2. 2 + 0.5 = 2.5 < 3: no, violated by 0.5 ns.
  3. Hold compares two delays measured from the same edge; the period does not appear in tpd + tcomb,min ≥ th.
  4. Tmin = 6 + 9 + 2 − 2 = 15 ns, fmax = 66.7 MHz. Hold: 6 + 1 = 7 ≥ 1 + 2 = 3: met.
  5. T = 10 ns: 4 + tcomb + 2 ≤ 10, so tcomb,max ≤ 4 ns.
  6. Take the new value, keep the old one, or go metastable (hang between 0 and 1, then resolve at random).
