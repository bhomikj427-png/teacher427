F1 · The feedback concept and the gain with feedback

Professor's feedback deck · slide 32 "Stabilization of Gain" (table for β = 0.01) An amplifier has a feedback factor β = 0.01. Find the gain with feedback when the open-loop gain is 1000, and again when it is 10 000. What do you notice?
Guess first: the open-loop gain grows ten times. Does the gain with feedback grow ten times?
Open-loop gain A: the gain of the amplifier alone, with no feedback. · Feedback factor β: the fraction of the output that is returned to the input. (Not the transistor's β.) · Closed-loop gain Af: the gain of the whole circuit with feedback connected.
{{ fb_block() }}

Feedback means taking part of the output and returning it to the input. In negative feedback the returned signal opposes the input (it is subtracted). In positive feedback it aids the input (Part G).

Derivation. The feedback network returns Vf = βVo. The amplifier sees only the difference Vi = Vs − Vf and multiplies it by A:

Vo = A(Vs − βVo)   ⟹   Vo(1 + βA) = AVs   ⟹   Af = Vo/Vs = A / (1 + βA)

Loop gainβAthe gain once round the loop: through the amplifier and back through the feedback network

The key result. If βA ≫ 1, then Af ≈ A/(βA) = 1/β. The gain no longer depends on the amplifier at all, only on the feedback network, which is made of stable resistors.

The four basic amplifiers (slides 2–6), named by what they take in and give out: voltage amplifier (V in, V out; wants high Ri, low Ro), current amplifier (I in, I out; low Ri, high Ro), transconductance amplifier (V in, I out; high Ri, high Ro), transresistance amplifier (I in, V out; low Ri, low Ro). F2 pairs one feedback connection with each.

Answer. A = 1000: Af = 1000/(1 + 0.01 × 1000) = 1000/11 = 90.9. A = 10 000: Af = 10 000/101 = 99.0. The amplifier's own gain changed by 900%, but the gain with feedback changed by only 9%, and both values are close to 1/β = 100. Feedback trades gain for stability.
Slip in the slide. The table on slide 32 prints 98.3 for A = 5000. The formula gives 5000/51 = 98.0. (Its other entries, 90.9, 99.0 and 99.5, are correct.)

F2 · The four feedback topologies

Practice · feedback deck slides 16–27, 41–45 A CE amplifier has an unbypassed emitter resistor RE. Identify the feedback topology and say what it does to the input and output resistance.
Guess first: is the voltage across RE proportional to the output voltage or to the output current?
Sampling: how the feedback network senses the output: its voltage (connected across the load) or its current (connected in series with the load). · Mixing: how the feedback signal is combined with the input: as a voltage in series, or as a current in parallel (shunt).

Two rules cover the whole table (slide 40). A series connection at a port raises the resistance there by (1 + βA). A shunt connection lowers it by (1 + βA). At the output, voltage sampling is the shunt connection (so Zo falls) and current sampling is the series connection (so Zo rises). In every case Af = A/(1 + βA), with A and β in the units of that row.

Why it makes sense. Voltage sampling fights any change in the output voltage, so the output behaves like a better voltage source: lower Zo. Current sampling holds the output current steady, like a better current source: higher Zo.

Answer. The voltage across RE is Vf = IoRE, proportional to the output current, and it sits in series with the input (Vbe = Vs − Vf). So it is current-series feedback, with β = Vf/Io = −RE. Both the input and the output resistance are raised by (1 + βA). With heavy feedback the voltage gain becomes about −RC/RE, set by two resistors.

Disadvantages. The gain is lower, so more stages may be needed. And if phase shifts inside the amplifier add up to 180° at some frequency, the feedback there becomes positive and the amplifier can oscillate. Part G makes that happen on purpose.

Answer. 1 + βA = 1 + 0.1 × 1000 = 101. Af = 1000/101 = 9.90. fHf = 20 kHz × 101 = 2.02 MHz. Voltage-series: Zif = 2 k × 101 = 202 kΩ, Zof = 20 k/101 = 198 Ω. Distortion = 10%/101 = 0.099%. Check: gain × bandwidth is 1000 × 20 kHz = 20 MHz before and 9.90 × 2.02 MHz = 20 MHz after.
