## L20 bjt-construction

### slide 1
Bipolar Junction Transistors. The transistor is a three-layer semiconductor device consisting of either two n- and one p-type layers of material or two p- and one n-type layers. The former is called an npn transistor, the latter a pnp transistor. So there are two types of BJT: pnp transistor, npn transistor.

### slide 2
In each transistor note: there are two junctions, so a transistor can be considered as two diodes connected back to back; there are three terminals; the middle section is thinner than the others.

### slide 3
Naming of Transistor Terminals. Transistor has three sections of doped semiconductor. The section on one side is called "emitter" and the opposite side "collector". The middle section is called "base". Transistor symbol.

### slide 4
1) Emitter: the section on one side that supplies carriers. Emitter is always forward biased w.r.t. base so it can supply carriers. For "npn transistor" emitter supply holes to its junction. For "pnp transistor" emitter supply electrons to its junction.

2) Collector: the section on the other side that collects carriers. The collector is always reverse biased w.r.t. base. For "npn transistor" collector receives holes to its junction. For "pnp transistor" collector receives electrons to its junction.
[verbatim; same swap as slide 4]

### slide 6
3) Base: the middle section which forms two pn junctions between emitter and collector is called Base.

### slide 7
Some important factors to be remembered: the transistor has three regions named emitter, base and collector. The base is much thinner than the other regions. Emitter is heavily doped so it can inject a large amount of carriers into the base. Base is lightly doped so it can pass most of the carriers to the collector. Collector is moderately doped.

### slide 8
The junction between emitter and base is called emitter-base junction (emitter diode) and the junction between base and collector is called collector-base junction (collector diode). The emitter diode is always forward biased and collector diode is reverse biased. The resistance of the emitter diode is very small (forward) and the resistance of the collector diode is high (reverse).

## L21 bjt-operation

### slide 1
Transistor Operation. Working of npn transistor: forward bias is applied to the emitter-base junction and reverse bias to the collector-base junction. The forward bias in the emitter-base junction causes electrons to move toward the base. This constitutes emitter current, IE.
[picture: s001-image1.jpg, not viewed (npn biasing diagram)]

### slide 2
1) Working of npn transistor: as these electrons flow toward the p-type base, they try to recombine with holes. As the base is lightly doped only few electrons recombine with holes within the base. These recombined electrons constitute small base current. The remainder electrons cross the base and constitute collector current.
[picture: s002-image2.png, small, not viewed]

### slide 3
Working of pnp transistor: forward bias is applied to emitter-base junction and reverse bias to collector-base junction. The forward bias in the emitter-base junction causes holes to move toward the base. This constitutes emitter current, IE.
[picture: s003-image3.jpg, not viewed (pnp biasing diagram)]

### slide 4
2) Working of pnp transistor: as these holes flow toward the n-type base, they try to recombine with electrons. As base is lightly doped only few holes recombine with electrons within the base. These recombined holes constitute small base current. The remainder holes cross the base and constitute collector current.

### slide 6
Transistor Operating Modes. Active Mode: base-emitter junction is forward and base-collector junction is reverse biased. Saturation Mode: base-emitter junction forward and base-collector junction forward biased. Cut-off Mode: both junctions are reverse biased.

## L22 common-base

### slide 1
Transistor Connection. Transistor can be connected in a circuit in the following three ways: Common Base, Common Emitter, Common Collector.

### slide 2
Common Base Connection. The common-base terminology is derived from the fact that the base is common to both the input and output sides of the configuration. First figure shows common base npn configuration and second figure shows common base pnp configuration.

### slide 3
Common Base Connection. Current amplification factor (alpha): the ratio of change in collector current to the change in emitter current at constant VCB is known as current amplification factor, alpha. alpha = delta IC / delta IE at constant VCB. Practical value of alpha is less than unity, but in the range of 0.9 to 0.99.

Expression for Collector Current. Total emitter current does not reach the collector terminal, because a small portion of it constitutes base current.
