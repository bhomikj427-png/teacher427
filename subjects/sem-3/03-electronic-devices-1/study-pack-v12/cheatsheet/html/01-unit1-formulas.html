Unit 1 — Formula cheat sheet: semiconductors and current flow

One card per formula from v1.2 files 01a, 01b, 02a, 02b · each card: a question → the formula → every symbol defined → a figure → where it holds · answers at the end · every number recomputed in verify.py

Map

Count carriers (F1–F6)→Push them: drift (F7–F11)→Spread them: diffusion (F12–F15)→Lose them: recombination (F16–F19)→Charge → field (F20)

Constants used on every card. q = 1.6 × 10−19 C (charge of one electron, magnitude). kT = 0.0259 eV at 300 K (a typical thermal energy). VT = kT/q = 0.0259 V ≈ 26 mV (the same energy in volts). Silicon at 300 K: Eg = 1.12 eV, ni = 1.5 × 1010 cm−3 (some decks use 1010), ε = 1.04 × 10−12 F/cm.

Part 1 · Counting carriers

F1 · Mass-action law: n · p = ni2

01a step 8

Si doped with ND = 1016 cm−3, ni = 1.5 × 1010 cm−3, 300 K. Find n and p.

n · p = ni2   n-type: n ≈ ND, p = ni2/ND   p-type: p ≈ NA, n = ni2/NA
Terms

n: free electrons per cm3. p: holes (empty bond places, charge +q) per cm3. ni: the electron count in pure (intrinsic) silicon, where n = p = ni. ND: donor atoms per cm3 (P, As: 5 valence electrons, each gives one electron). NA: acceptor atoms per cm3 (B, Ga: 3 valence electrons, each makes one hole). Thermal equilibrium: steady temperature, no voltage, no light.

  • Doping raises one carrier and pushes the other down by the same factor: their product stays ni2.
  • Majority carrier: the one doping supplies (electrons in n-type). Minority: the other.
  • Holds only in equilibrium.

F2 · Intrinsic density: ni = √(NCNV) e−Eg/2kT

01b step 8

(a) NC = 2.8 × 1019, NV = 1.04 × 1019 cm−3, Eg = 1.12 eV, kT = 0.0259 eV. Find ni. (b) ni roughly doubles every 10 °C. By what factor does it grow from 25 °C to 45 °C?

ni = √(NCNV) e−Eg/2kT
Terms

Eg (band gap): the energy needed to break one bond and free an electron; 1.12 eV in Si. NC, NV: effective numbers of electron "seats" in the conduction band and of hole seats in the valence band, per cm3 (F4). e−Eg/2kT: how rare a bond-breaking kick is; tiny, and very sensitive to Eg and T.

  • Ge (Eg 0.66 eV) ≈ 2.4 × 1013, Si ≈ 1.5 × 1010, GaAs (1.42 eV) ≈ 1.8 × 106 cm−3.
  • Why semiconductor resistance falls when heated: more carriers.

F3 · Fermi–Dirac function f(E)

01b steps 2–3

(a) What is f(E) at E = EF? (b) At E − EF = 3kT? (c) In pure Si, EC is 0.56 eV above EF. Find f(EC).

f(E) = 11 + e(E − EF)/kT   hole chance = 1 − f(E)
Terms

State ("seat"): one allowed place for one electron at one energy. f(E): the probability that a seat at energy E is filled. EF (Fermi level): the energy where that probability is exactly ½. EC: bottom of the conduction band (lowest free-electron energy). EV: top of the valence band (highest bonded-electron energy).

  • Far below EF: f ≈ 1 (full). Far above: f ≈ 0 (empty). The change from 1 to 0 takes a few kT.

F4 · Carrier counts: n = NCe−(EC − EF)/kT

01b step 7

(a) EF is 0.25 eV below EC at 300 K. Find n. (b) EF − EV = 0.20 eV. Find p.

n = NC e−(EC − EF)/kT   p = NV e−(EF − EV)/kT
Terms

NC: the number of seats per cm3 that, if all placed exactly at EC, would hold the same electrons as the real band; 2.8 × 1019 cm−3 in Si, ∝ T3/2. NV: the same for holes at EV; 1.04 × 1019. Exponential: the chance a seat at the band edge is filled (the Boltzmann shortcut of f, valid when EF is more than 3kT from the edge, "non-degenerate").

  • Read it: the closer EF is to EC, the more electrons. Every 60 mV (0.06 eV) closer multiplies n by about 10.
  • Multiply n by p: EF cancels and n · p = NCNVe−Eg/kT = ni2. That is F1 proved.

F5 · Intrinsic Fermi level: EF at mid-gap

Deck slide 38

Show that the Fermi level in an intrinsic semiconductor lies at the middle of the forbidden gap.

EFi = EC + EV2 + kT2 lnNVNC ≈ EC + EV2
Terms

Intrinsic: undoped, so n = p. EFi (also Ei): the Fermi level of intrinsic silicon. Mid-gap: (EC + EV)/2, halfway between the band edges.

  • The ln term is kT/2 × ln(1.04/2.8) = −13 mV: under 2 % of the 0.56 eV half-gap, so EF ≈ mid-gap.

F6 · Doping moves EF: n = nie(EF − Ei)/kT

01b step 10

Si with ND = 1016 cm−3, ni = 1010 cm−3, 300 K. How far above mid-gap is EF? How far below EC?

n = ni e(EF − Ei)/kT   p = ni e(Ei − EF)/kT   ⇒   EF − Ei = kT ln(ND/ni)
Terms

Ei: the intrinsic Fermi level (≈ mid-gap), used as the reference. EF − Ei > 0: n-type (EF above mid-gap). < 0: p-type, with Ei − EF = kT ln(NA/ni).

Part 2 · Drift: current pushed by a field

F7 · Drift velocity: vd = μE

02a step 2

1 V across 100 μm of lightly doped Si gives E = 100 V/cm. μn = 1350 cm2/V·s. Find the electron drift velocity and compare with the thermal speed 1.17 × 107 cm/s.

vd = μE   (low field)   vd = μE1 + μE/vsat   (any field)
Terms

E (field): V/cm; the push per unit charge. vd (drift velocity): the average velocity along the field after the random motion cancels. μ (mobility): cm2/V·s; drift velocity per unit field; Si: μn ≈ 1350, μp ≈ 480. vsat: the speed limit, ≈ 107 cm/s in Si. Thermal speed vth: random speed from heat; gives no current because the directions cancel.

  • μ = qτ/m*: longer time between collisions (τ) or lighter effective mass (m*) → more mobile.
  • μ falls with doping (ions scatter) and with temperature (vibrating atoms scatter).

F8 · Drift current and conductivity: J = σE

Deck slide 51

(a) Conductivity and resistivity of intrinsic Si at 300 K, ni = 1010 cm−3, μn = 1500, μp = 450 cm2/V·s. (b) Effect of a 10 °C rise.

Jdrift = q(nμn + pμp)E = σE   σ = q(nμn + pμp)   ρ = 1/σ
Terms

J (current density): A/cm2; current through one cm2. σ (conductivity): (Ω·cm)−1; how much J one V/cm drives = how many carriers × how mobile. ρ (resistivity): Ω·cm; the material's resistance, independent of shape. Electrons and holes move opposite ways but both carry current along E, so the terms add.

F9 · Doped silicon: σ ≈ qNDμn

Deck slide 52

Si with ND = 5 × 1017 cm−3 has σ = 32 (Ω·cm)−1. (a) Find p. (b) What electron mobility does σ imply? (c) How many times more conductive than intrinsic Si (3.12 × 10−6)?

n-type: σ ≈ qNDμn   p-type: σ ≈ qNAμp
Terms

Once doped, the minority term is billions of times smaller than the majority term, so it is dropped. μ here is the mobility at that doping, lower than in pure Si.

F10 · Resistance of a bar: R = ρL/A

02a step 8

An n-type Si bar has ρ = 0.5 Ω·cm, length 1 mm, cross-section 100 μm × 100 μm. Find R and the current at 5 V.

R = ρLA   A = W × t
Terms

L: length along the current, cm. A: cross-section area, cm2; W width, t thickness. Convert μm to cm: 1 μm = 10−4 cm.

F11 · Sheet resistance: R = Rs × L/W

02a step 9

A doped layer is 1 μm thick, ρ = 0.01 Ω·cm. Find Rs. A resistor in it is 200 μm long, 10 μm wide. Find R.

Rs = ρ/t   (Ω/□)   R = Rs × LW
Terms

Rs (sheet resistance): the resistance of one square of the layer, any size; units Ω, said "ohms per square". L/W: the number of squares end to end along the resistor. Used in IC layout, where every layer has a fixed t.

Part 3 · Diffusion: current from a crowd spreading out

F12 · Fick's law: F = −D dn/dx

02b step 2

Electron density falls in a straight line from 1017 to 1016 cm−3 over 1 μm. Dn = 35 cm2/s. Find the gradient and the electron diffusion flux.

Fn = −Dn dndx   Fp = −Dp dpdx
Terms

Flux F: carriers crossing one cm2 per second. Gradient dn/dx: slope of n(x), cm−4. D (diffusion coefficient): cm2/s; how fast a carrier spreads; Si: Dn ≈ 35, Dp ≈ 12.4. Minus sign: carriers flow downhill, from more to fewer.

F13 · Diffusion current: Jn = +qDndn/dx, Jp = −qDpdp/dx

02b step 3

dn/dx = −1018 cm−4, Dn = 35 cm2/s. Find the electron diffusion current density and its direction.

Jn,diff = +qDn dndx   Jp,diff = −qDp dpdx
Terms

J = charge × flux. Electron charge is −q, so the minus sign of Fick flips to + for electrons. Holes (+q) keep the minus.

F14 · Total current: drift + diffusion

02b step 4

In n-type Si: n = 1016 cm−3, E = 10 V/cm along +x, dn/dx = −1018 cm−4, μn = 1350, Dn = 35. Find the drift, diffusion and total electron current densities.

Jn = qnμnE + qDndndx   Jp = qpμpE − qDpdpdx   J = Jn + Jp
Terms

Jn, Jp: total electron and hole current densities. J: what an ammeter sees, per cm2. In equilibrium the drift and diffusion parts cancel exactly: J = 0 (this is how the pn junction's built-in field forms, Unit 2 F21).

F15 · Einstein relation: D/μ = kT/q

Deck slide 57

(a) μn = 1350, μp = 480 cm2/V·s at 300 K. Find Dn, Dp. (b) Derive the relation from Jn = 0 in equilibrium.

Dnμn = Dpμp = kTq = VT
Terms

VT (thermal voltage): kT in volts, 0.0259 V at 300 K. D and μ are tied because the same collisions limit both spreading and drifting.

Part 4 · Generation, recombination, continuity

F16 · SRH recombination → Δp/τp

Deck slide 61

State the Shockley–Read–Hall net recombination rate. Show that in n-type Si with a small hole excess Δp it becomes R − G ≈ Δp/τp.

R − G = np − ni2τp(n + n1) + τn(p + p1)   ⇒   R − G ≈ Δpτp
Terms

EHP: electron–hole pair. G: pairs created per cm3 per s (heat, light). R: pairs destroyed per cm3 per s. Trap (G–R centre): a defect with an energy level ET inside the gap; in Si almost all recombination goes through traps. τn, τp: lifetimes, set by how many traps. n1, p1: n and p if EF sat at ET (= ni for a mid-gap trap). np − ni2: how far from equilibrium; zero in equilibrium.

F17 · Lifetime decay: Δp(t) = Δp(0)e−t/τp

02b step 10

Light makes Δp = 1014 cm−3 extra holes in n-type Si, τp = 1 μs. Light off at t = 0. Δp after 1 μs and 3 μs?

dΔpdt = −Δpτp   ⇒   Δp(t) = Δp(0) e−t/τp
Terms

Δp (excess): holes above the equilibrium value p0. τp (minority-carrier lifetime): average survival time of an extra hole. After one τ, 37 % remain; after 3τ, 5 %.

F18 · Continuity equation

Deck slides 62–64

Write the hole continuity equation. Reduce it to the steady-state, no-field form.

∂n∂t = +1q∂Jn∂x − (R − G)   ∂p∂t = −1q∂Jp∂x − (R − G)
Terms

Bookkeeping for a thin box: change in time = (in − out) − net loss. ∂J/∂x: how current changes across the box. Steady state: ∂/∂t = 0.

F19 · Diffusion length: L = √(Dτ)

02b step 12

Δp(0) = 1014 cm−3 held at x = 0 of a long n-region, no field. Dp = 12.4 cm2/s, τp = 1 μs. Find Lp and Δp at 50 μm.

Lp = √(Dpτp)   Δp(x) = Δp(0) e−x/Lp
Terms

Lp (diffusion length): the average distance an injected hole spreads before it recombines; the excess falls to 1/e (37 %) over each L.

Part 5 · Charge makes field

F20 · Poisson's equation: dE/dx = ρ/ε

Deck slide 65

(a) Write Poisson's equation for a semiconductor and explain each term. (b) A region holds only ionised donors, ND = 1016 cm−3. How fast does E grow with x, and by how much over 0.1 μm?

dEdx = ρε = qε(p − n + ND+ − NA−)   E = −dVdx
Terms

ρ(x): net charge per cm3 (here ρ is charge, not resistivity). ND+: donors that gave away their electron (fixed, +). NA−: acceptors that took an electron (fixed, −). ε: permittivity, Si 11.7ε0 = 1.04 × 10−12 F/cm. V: potential; the field is its downhill slope.

Answers

F1. n ≈ ND = 1016; p = (1.5 × 1010)2/1016 = 2.25 × 104 cm−3.

F2. (a) √(2.8 × 1019 × 1.04 × 1019) = 1.71 × 1019; e−1.12/0.0518 = 4.06 × 10−10; ni = 6.9 × 109 cm−3 (textbooks quote 1–1.5 × 1010: the result is very sensitive to Eg and NV). (b) Two doublings: × 4.

F3. (a) ½. (b) 1/(1 + e3) = 0.047. (c) 1/(1 + e21.6) ≈ 4 × 10−10.

F4. (a) 2.8 × 1019 × e−9.65 = 1.8 × 1015 cm−3. (b) 1.04 × 1019 × e−7.72 = 4.6 × 1015 cm−3.

F5. Intrinsic: n = p. NCe−(EC − EF)/kT = NVe−(EF − EV)/kT. Take ln: −(EC − EF) = ln(NV/NC)kT − (EF − EV). So 2EF = EC + EV + kT ln(NV/NC), giving EF = (EC + EV)/2 + (kT/2)ln(NV/NC). The second term is −13 mV, so EF ≈ mid-gap.

F6. EF − Ei = 0.0259 × ln(106) = 0.358 eV above mid-gap. EC − EF = 0.0259 × ln(2.8 × 1019/1016) = 0.206 eV. Check: 0.358 + 0.206 ≈ 0.56 eV, half the gap.

F7. vd = 1350 × 100 = 1.35 × 105 cm/s, about 87 times slower than vth.

F8. (a) σ = 1.6 × 10−19 × 1010 × 1950 = 3.12 × 10−6 (Ω·cm)−1; ρ = 3.2 × 105 Ω·cm. (b) ni doubles, μ barely changes: σ doubles.

F9. (a) p = 1020/(5 × 1017) = 200 cm−3. (b) μn = 32/(1.6 × 10−19 × 5 × 1017) = 400 cm2/V·s (down from about 1400 in pure Si: ion scattering). (c) 32/3.12 × 10−6 ≈ 107 times.

F10. L = 0.1 cm, A = 10−4 cm2. R = 0.5 × 0.1/10−4 = 500 Ω; I = 10 mA.

F11. Rs = 0.01/10−4 = 100 Ω/□; 20 squares; R = 2 kΩ.

F12. dn/dx = (1016 − 1017)/10−4 = −9 × 1020 cm−4. Fn = 3.15 × 1022 cm−2s−1, toward +x.

F13. Jn = 1.6 × 10−19 × 35 × (−1018) = −5.6 A/cm2: electrons move to +x, current flows to −x.

F14. Drift 21.6 A/cm2; diffusion −5.6 A/cm2; total 16.0 A/cm2 along +x.

F15. (a) Dn = 1350 × 0.0259 = 35 cm2/s; Dp = 480 × 0.0259 = 12.4 cm2/s. (b) In equilibrium EF is flat and n = NCe−(EC − EF)/kT, so dn/dx = −(n/kT) dEC/dx. The band slope is the field: dEC/dx = qE. So dn/dx = −nqE/kT. Set Jn = qnμnE + qDn(−nqE/kT) = 0 → μn = Dnq/kT → Dn/μn = kT/q.

F16. n-type, low injection: n ≈ n0 ≫ p, n1, p1, so the denominator ≈ τpn0. Numerator: n0(p0 + Δp) − ni2 = n0Δp (because n0p0 = ni2). R − G = n0Δp/(τpn0) = Δp/τp.

F17. 1014 × e−1 = 3.68 × 1013; 1014 × e−3 = 4.98 × 1012 cm−3.

F18. No field: Jp = −qDpdp/dx, so −(1/q)∂Jp/∂x = Dp∂2p/∂x2. Steady state, R − G = Δp/τp: Dp d2Δp/dx2 = Δp/τp, solved by F19.

F19. Lp = √(12.4 × 10−6) = 35.2 μm. Δp(50 μm) = 1014 × e−1.42 = 2.42 × 1013 cm−3.

F20. (a) As in the card. (b) dE/dx = qND/ε = 1.54 × 109 V/cm2; over 10−5 cm, E grows by 1.54 × 104 V/cm.
