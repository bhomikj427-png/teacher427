05 — Clippers, clampers, Zener, Schottky

Deck L10 (clippers slides 3–7, clampers slide 13) · syllabus Unit 2 · needs 04 (the three-step method)

Map

1 Series clipper→2 Parallel clipper→3 Clamper→4 Voltage multipliers→5 Breakdown, Zener regulator→6 Schottky diode

04 used the diode to pass one half of a wave. Here a battery shifts the level at which it switches (clippers), a capacitor shifts the whole wave (clampers), and breakdown, which 03b treated as a limit, becomes useful (Zener).

Build

1 · The series clipper

Deck L10 slide 3

Determine the output waveform. (Series clipper: a 5 V battery, then an ideal diode, then R. Input: 20 V peak sine.)

Guess first

Sketch vo for one cycle.

Terms

Clipper (limiter): removes part of a waveform and passes the rest unchanged. Transition voltage: the input value at which the diode changes state.

The diode is in series with the output, so there is output only while it conducts. The battery shifts the switching level.

  1. Transition voltage. Put the ideal diode at the edge (id = 0, vd = 0) and write KVL: vi + 5 = 0, so it switches at vi = −5 V.
  2. ON (vi > −5 V): vo = vi + 5.
  3. OFF (vi < −5 V): no current, vo = 0.

Answer. The output is the input lifted by 5 V while vi > −5 V, and zero otherwise. Peak 25 V.

Check 1

Reverse the battery. Find the new transition voltage and the peak of vo.

2 · The parallel clipper

Deck L10 slide 6

Triangular input of 16 V peak; ideal diode with its anode on a 4 V battery, across the output. Find vo. Repeat with a silicon diode (slide 7).

Guess first

While the diode is off, what does the output do?

The diode is across the output. OFF: the output follows the input. ON: it pins the output to the battery level. Two branches facing opposite ways clip both peaks, which squares up a sine.

Answer. The diode conducts whenever vi < 4 V and holds vo = 4 V; above 4 V it is off and vo = vi. With Si, the pinned level is 4 − 0.7 = 3.3 V.

Check 2

(a) Turn the diode around (cathode on the battery). Describe vo. (b) In which clipper type does the output follow the input while the diode is off?

3 · The clamper

Deck L10 slide 13

Determine vo. (Square-wave input +10 V and −20 V, f = 1000 Hz. C = 0.1 μF, R = 100 kΩ, ideal diode in series with a 5 V battery.)

Guess first

The input swings 30 V in total. What total swing will the output have?

Terms

Clamper: C, a diode and R that shift a whole waveform up or down without changing its shape. Time constant τ = RC: a capacitor needs about 5τ to charge or discharge.

On one half-cycle the diode conducts and charges C quickly. On the other it is off, and C can discharge only through the large R. If 5τ ≫ half-period, C holds its voltage like a battery in series with the input.

  1. Start with the half-cycle that turns the diode ON: vi = −20 V. The output is pinned to the battery: vo = 5 V.
  2. KVL in that state: −20 + VC − 5 = 0, so VC = 25 V.
  3. Other half, diode OFF: vo = vi + VC = 10 + 25 = 35 V.
  4. Check the swing: 35 − 5 = 30 V ✓.

Answer. vo is a square wave between 5 V and 35 V. τ = 100 kΩ × 0.1 μF = 10 ms, 5τ = 50 ms: 100 times the half-period of 0.5 ms ✓.

Check 3

(a) Remove the 5 V battery (diode straight to ground). Find the two output levels. (b) What goes wrong if R is made 100 Ω?

4 · Voltage multipliers

Predict

A clamper shifts a wave up by Vm. If a peak detector then follows it, what DC voltage does it hold?

Half-wave doubler. Positive half: D1 conducts and charges C1 to Vm. Negative half: the source and C1 add in series, D2 conducts, and C2 charges to 2Vm. Each diode must stand PIV = 2Vm. More sections give a tripler (3Vm) and a quadrupler (4Vm), for high-voltage, low-current loads.

Answer. 2Vm: the clamper output peaks at 2Vm, and the peak detector holds it.

Check 4

What DC voltage does a quadrupler give from a 10 V peak input, ideally?

5 · Breakdown and the Zener regulator

practice

In the regulator, Vi = 16 V, R = 1 kΩ, VZ = 10 V. Find VL, IL, IZ and PZ for (a) RL = 1.2 kΩ and (b) RL = 3 kΩ.

Guess first

In which case is the Zener actually regulating?

Breakdown is the sharp rise of reverse current at one reverse voltage. It does not destroy the diode if the current is limited.

Zener breakdown Avalanche breakdown
Doping heavy both sides light
Depletion region very thin, huge field wide
Mechanism field pulls electrons out of bonds (tunnelling) fast carriers knock out more (multiplication)
Voltage below about 5 V above about 6 V
Temperature coefficient negative (V falls) positive (V rises)

A Zener diode is built to work in breakdown; its voltage stays at VZ over a wide current range, so it is a voltage reference.

  1. Test the state. Remove the Zener: V = ViRL/(R + RL). If V ≥ VZ it is ON; if not, OFF (open).
  2. If ON: VL = VZ; IR = (Vi − VZ)/R; IL = VZ/RL; IZ = IR − IL; PZ = VZIZ < PZM.

Answer. (a) V = 16 × 1.2/2.2 = 8.73 V < 10 V: OFF, VL = 8.73 V, IZ = 0, PZ = 0. No regulation. (b) V = 12 V ≥ 10: ON. VL = 10 V, IR = 6 mA, IL = 3.33 mA, IZ = 2.67 mA, PZ = 26.7 mW. Smallest load that keeps it on: RL,min = RVZ/(Vi − VZ) = 1.67 kΩ.

Check 5

(a) A 4.7 V Zener and a 12 V Zener heat up. Whose voltage falls, and why? (b) Vi rises to 20 V with RL = 3 kΩ. Find IZ.

6 · The Schottky diode

Predict

04's rectifiers are limited at high frequency by the stored charge of 03b (reverse recovery, step 12). What junction would store no minority charge?

A Schottky diode is a metal–n-type silicon junction. Only majority carriers (electrons) take part, so there is no stored minority charge and almost no reverse recovery time: it switches very fast. Forward drop 0.2–0.4 V. The price: larger reverse leakage, lower breakdown voltage. Used in high-frequency rectifiers and fast logic.

Answer. A metal–semiconductor junction, where only majority carriers flow.

Check 6

Give two advantages and two disadvantages of a Schottky diode against a pn diode.

Exam form

Clippers. Transition voltage by KVL at the edge (id = 0, vd = 0); ON and OFF expressions; sketch input and output aligned. Series: output only while ON. Parallel: output follows input while OFF.

Clampers. Start with the ON half → VC by KVL → other half vo = vi + VC → check swing → check 5τ ≫ T/2.

Multipliers. Doubler operation, 2Vm, PIV 2Vm.

Zener. Zener vs avalanche table; regulator procedure; RL,min; PZ.

Schottky. Metal–n junction, majority carriers only, fast, 0.3 V drop.

Traps

  • Not checking the Zener is ON before writing VL = VZ.
  • Clamper: starting with the half-cycle where the diode is OFF.
  • Forgetting that the clamper output swing equals the input swing.
  • Mixing up which clipper follows the input when the diode is off.
  • Saying Zener breakdown has a positive temperature coefficient.

Self-test

  1. Compare Zener and avalanche breakdown in four points.
  2. Zener regulator: Vi = 20 V, R = 500 Ω, VZ = 8 V, RL = 1 kΩ. Find VL, IL, IZ and PZ.
  3. For Q2, find RL,min.
  4. A ±10 V square wave drives a clamper whose diode (to ground, no battery) conducts on the negative half. Find the output levels.
  5. A voltage tripler is fed a 15 V peak sine. Ideal DC output?
  6. Why must a clamper's RC be large compared with the period?

Answers

Check 1. KVL at the edge: vi − 5 = 0, transition at +5 V. ON for vi > 5 V with vo = vi − 5; peak 15 V.

Check 2. (a) It conducts when vi > 4 V and pins vo = 4 V; below 4 V, vo = vi. The top is clipped at 4 V. (b) The parallel clipper.

Check 3. (a) ON at −20 V pins vo = 0, VC = 20 V; other half 10 + 20 = 30 V. Levels 0 V and 30 V. (b) τ = 10 μs ≪ 0.5 ms: C discharges within each half-cycle, so the level droops and the shape distorts.

Check 4. 40 V.

Check 5. (a) The 4.7 V one: below about 5 V the mechanism is Zener breakdown, negative coefficient. The 12 V one is avalanche; its voltage rises. (b) IR = 10 mA, IL = 3.33 mA, IZ = 6.67 mA.

Check 6. For: very fast switching (no stored charge), low forward drop. Against: higher reverse leakage, lower breakdown voltage.

Self-test.

  1. See step 5's table.
  2. Test: 20 × 1/1.5 = 13.3 V ≥ 8: ON. VL = 8 V, IR = 12/0.5 k = 24 mA, IL = 8 mA, IZ = 16 mA, PZ = 128 mW.
  3. RL,min = 500 × 8/12 = 333 Ω.
  4. Negative half: vo = 0, VC = 10 V. Positive half: 10 + 10 = 20 V. Levels 0 and 20 V.
  5. 3 × 15 = 45 V.
  6. C must hold its charge through the OFF half-cycle; if 5τ is not much longer than T/2, it discharges through R and the output droops.
