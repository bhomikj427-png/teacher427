02a moved carriers with a field. This file adds the second way: carriers spread from where they are crowded, with no field at all. Then it asks how carriers are created and destroyed, and puts flow and creation into one bookkeeping equation, the continuity equation. 03 uses every piece: the pn junction is diffusion balanced by drift, and the diode current is diffusion of carriers that recombine within a length L.

1 · Diffusion: a crowd spreads out by itself

Predict

Put a drop of ink into still water. Nothing pushes the ink. A minute later, is it still a drop? What makes it move, and in which direction?

  • Each carrier does the random walk of 02a step 1. No carrier "knows" where the crowd is.
  • But count crossings at a plane. If one side holds more carriers, more of them happen to cross from that side:
  • So the crowd spreads, from crowded to sparse, until the concentration is the same everywhere. This net flow is diffusion (deck slide 53). No field and no force are needed.

Answer to the Predict. The ink spreads out; random motion of its molecules moves it from where it is concentrated to where it is not.

Check 1

(a) Does any single carrier move toward the sparse side on purpose? (b) When does net diffusion stop? (c) Why does a uniform n-type bar have no diffusion?

2 · Fick's law: flux is set by the slope

practice

The electron concentration falls in a straight line from 1017 to 1016 cm−3 over 1 μm. Dn = 35 cm2/s. Find the electron diffusion flux.

Guess first

A gentle slope and a steep slope of n: which gives the larger flow? Which way does it go?

Terms

Gradient dn/dx: how fast n changes with position, in cm−3 per cm = cm−4; the slope of the n(x) curve. Diffusion coefficient D: flux per unit gradient, in cm2/s; how fast that carrier spreads. Set by the same scattering that sets μ (step 6).

  • Twice the slope → twice the imbalance at each plane → twice the flux (deck slide 54):
Fn = −Dn dndx   Fp = −Dp dpdx
  • The minus sign says the flow runs downhill: where dn/dx < 0, F is positive (+x).
  • Units (deck slide 54): F in cm−2s−1, dn/dx in cm−4, so D in cm2/s.

Answer. dn/dx = (1016 − 1017) / 10−4 cm = −9 × 1020 cm−4. Fn = −35 × (−9 × 1020) = 3.15 × 1022 electrons per cm2 per second, toward +x (downhill).

Check 2

(a) Holes with dp/dx > 0: which way do they flow? (b) The slope is halved. What happens to the flux? (c) Show that D has units cm2/s.

3 · Diffusion current, and why the signs differ

Flux counts particles. Current counts charge: multiply by the charge of each carrier.

practice

In a silicon bar the electron concentration falls by 1018 cm−3 per cm (dn/dx = −1018 cm−4). Dn = 35 cm2/s. Find the electron diffusion current density and its direction.

Guess first

Electrons diffuse toward +x. Which way does their current point?

  • Electrons: Jn = (−q) × Fn = (−q)(−Dn dn/dx):
Jn,diff = +qDn dndx   Jp,diff = −qDp dpdx
  • The hole formula keeps Fick's minus sign; the electron formula loses it, because of the electron's negative charge.

Answer. Jn = 1.6 × 10−19 × 35 × (−1018) = −5.6 A/cm2. The electrons move to +x; the current flows to −x.

Check 3

(a) Holes with dp/dx = −1018 cm−4, Dp = 12.4 cm2/s: Jp and its direction? (b) In one sentence: why do electron and hole diffusion currents in the same falling profile point opposite ways?

4 · Total current = drift + diffusion

A real device often has a field and a gradient at the same place. Each carrier type then has both currents.

practice

At one point in n-type Si: n = 1016 cm−3, E = 10 V/cm along +x, dn/dx = −1018 cm−4, μn = 1350, Dn = 35. Find the drift, diffusion and total electron current densities.

Guess first

Do the two parts add or partly cancel here?

Terms

Jn, Jp: total electron and hole current densities. J: the total current density, Jn + Jp; the one an ammeter sees (per cm2).

  • Add 02a's drift to step 3's diffusion, for each carrier (deck slide 55):
Jn = qnμnE + qDndndx   Jp = qpμpE − qDpdpdx   J = Jn + Jp
Device region Which term carries the current
doped resistor (uniform) drift only: no gradient
forward-biased diode, neutral sides diffusion of minority carriers (03)
depletion region in equilibrium huge drift and huge diffusion, cancelling (step 5)
Deck slide 65

The pasted web page writes Jp = qμppE + qDpdp/dx. The diffusion term for holes is −qDpdp/dx (step 3; deck slide 55 has it right).

Answer. Drift: 1.6 × 10−19 × 1016 × 1350 × 10 = 21.6 A/cm2 (+x). Diffusion: 1.6 × 10−19 × 35 × (−1018) = −5.6 A/cm2. Total Jn = 16.0 A/cm2 (+x). They partly cancel.

(a) Write the total hole current density with both terms. (b) In the question, what gradient dn/dx would make Jn = 0?

5 · Equilibrium: drift and diffusion cancel exactly

Predict

A bar is doped unevenly: 1017 donors per cm3 at the left, falling to 1015 at the right. No battery. Electrons diffuse to the right. Does a current flow forever? If not, what stops it?

Terms

Equilibrium: nothing connected, no light, steady temperature. Built-in field: a field created inside the material by its own charges, with no battery.

  1. Electrons diffuse right. They leave behind fixed + donor ions on the left and pile up as − charge on the right.
  2. That charge makes a field pointing right (+ to −). It pushes electrons back to the left: a drift current.
  3. Diffusion builds the field until the drift exactly cancels it, at every point.
  • Each carrier's current is zero separately: Jn = 0 and Jp = 0, so Jndrift = −Jndiff (deck slide 56, detailed balance). If only the sum were zero, electrons would pile up somewhere forever.
  • On the band diagram: EF is flat (01b); EC − EF = kT ln(NC/n) grows where n falls, so the bands tilt. A tilt of the bands is the built-in field: E = (1/q) dEC/dx.
  • The potential step across the bar: kT/q × ln(1017/1015) = 0.0259 × 4.61 = 0.119 V. 03 does the same for a pn junction.

Answer to the Predict. A current flows only for an instant. The charge it moves builds a field whose drift current cancels the diffusion current. Then J = 0 everywhere.

Check 5

(a) Why must Jn and Jp each be zero, not just their sum? (b) Find EC − EF at the left end (n = 1017) and right end (n = 1015); use NC = 2.8 × 1019. Their difference? (c) Which way does the built-in field point, and why?

6 · The Einstein relation: D and μ are tied together

Deck slide 57

In a non-degenerate semiconductor in equilibrium, use Jndrift = −Jndiff to show that Dn/μn = kT/q.

Guess first

Which two facts from earlier steps do you need: one about n, one about the field?

Terms

Thermal voltage VT = kT/q: the energy kT expressed in volts. 0.0259 V at 300 K. Non-degenerate: EF more than 3kT from the band edges, so n = NCe−(EC − EF)/kT holds (01b step 6).

Answer (deck slide 57; EF constant, EC varies with x):

  1. n = NCe−(EC − EF)/kT.
  2. Drift, with the field from the band tilt E = (1/q)dEC/dx: Jndrift = qnμnE = nμn dEC/dx.
  3. Diffusion: differentiate line 1: dn/dx = n × (−1/kT) dEC/dx. So Jndiff = qDndn/dx = −(q/kT) Dn n dEC/dx.
  4. Set Jndrift = −Jndiff: nμn dEC/dx = (q/kT) Dn n dEC/dx.
  5. Cancel n dEC/dx:
Dnμn = Dpμp = kTq = VT
  • Why it makes sense: a carrier that scatters rarely (long τ) both drifts easily (large μ) and spreads fast (large D). One scattering process sets both, so their ratio depends only on temperature.
  • Units (deck slide 57): (cm2/s)/(cm2/V·s) = V. ✓
  • Numbers (300 K): Dn = 1350 × 0.0259 = 35.0 cm2/s; Dp = 480 × 0.0259 = 12.4 cm2/s.
Check 6

(a) At 400 K, VT = ? If μn = 1000 there, Dn = ? (b) Dn for μn = 1500 at 300 K? (c) A data sheet gives μ and D for a sample that do not satisfy D/μ = kT/q at its temperature. What should you suspect?

7 · Generation and recombination

Steps 1–6 moved carriers around. Now: carriers being created and destroyed.

Predict

In pure Si at 300 K, bonds break all the time, each one making an electron and a hole. Why does ni not keep growing?

Terms

EHP: electron–hole pair. Generation G: pairs created per cm3 per second (energy in: heat or light). Recombination R: pairs destroyed per cm3 per second (an electron falls into a hole; energy out).

  • The rates depend on (deck slide 58): the band structure, light of a suitable wavelength, defects and impurities, the carrier densities, the temperature.
  • R grows with how many electrons and holes there are to meet: more of both → more recombination.
  • Equilibrium: G = R. That balance is what holds n · p at ni2 (01a step 8).
  • Disturbed (light, or a diode in forward bias): extra carriers make R > G, so the excess dies away. Too few carriers (a reverse-biased junction): G > R, so pairs are made. R − G is the net rate that pulls n·p back toward ni2.

Answer to the Predict. Recombination: the more pairs exist, the faster they meet and vanish, until destruction matches creation.

Check 7

(a) In equilibrium, what is R − G? (b) Shine light on a sample: sign of R − G just after the light goes off? (c) In which device region is n·p < ni2, so G > R?

8 · Direct (band-to-band) generation and recombination

Predict

LEDs are made from GaAs, not silicon. Guess why.

Terms

Photon: a particle of light; carries energy, almost no momentum. Phonon: a quantum of lattice vibration (heat); carries momentum. Direct / indirect gap: in GaAs an electron at EC can drop straight to EV; in Si it also needs a momentum change, which only a phonon can supply.

  • Direct recombination (deck slide 59): a conduction electron meets a valence hole in one jump. The energy Eg leaves as a photon (radiative) or as phonons (non-radiative).
  • Direct generation: the reverse. A photon (photo-generation) or phonons (thermal generation) lift an electron EV → EC.
  • Both bands take part, so these are called band-to-band G–R.
  • GaAs (direct gap): band-to-band is fast and gives light. Si (indirect gap): it is rare, because a phonon must take part at the same moment.
  • Photon wavelength λ (nm) = 1240/Eg (eV): GaAs, 1240/1.42 = 873 nm (infrared); Si, 1240/1.12 = 1107 nm.

Answer to the Predict. GaAs has a direct gap, so its electrons and holes recombine by giving off light. In Si they mostly recombine through traps and give off heat (step 9).

Check 8

(a) Where does the energy go in radiative and in non-radiative recombination? (b) Why is band-to-band recombination rare in Si? (c) Which photon wavelength would a material with Eg = 1.97 eV emit?

9 · Recombination through traps (SRH)

Deck slide 61

State the Shockley–Read–Hall net recombination rate. Show that in n-type Si with a small excess of holes Δp it becomes R − G ≈ Δp/τp.

Guess first

In n-type Si, which carrier is scarce? Which one would limit how fast pairs can meet at a trap?

Terms

G–R centre (trap): a defect or impurity atom with an allowed level ET inside the gap (deck slide 60). τn, τp: electron and hole lifetimes, set by how many traps there are and how readily they capture. n1, p1: the n and p you would have if EF sat at ET (deck slide 61).

  • Two steps (figure in step 8, right): ① an electron drops from EC into the trap; ② it drops from the trap into a hole in EV. Each step needs only part of Eg, so each is easy. The energy leaves as heat. In Si this is the main route (deck slide 61).
  • Traps work best near mid-gap, ET ≈ Ei: neither half-step is too large (deck slide 60).
  • The net rate (deck slide 61):
R − G = np − ni2τp(n + n1) + τn(p + p1)   n1 = nie(ET − Ei)/kT,   p1 = nie(Ei − ET)/kT
  • Read the top: np − ni2 = how far from equilibrium. Zero in equilibrium; positive with excess carriers (R wins); negative with a shortage (G wins).

Answer (mid-gap trap, so n1 = p1 = ni):

  1. n-type: n ≈ ND, much larger than p, ni.
  2. p = p0 + Δp, with NDp0 = ni2. Top: ND(p0 + Δp) − ni2 = NDΔp.
  3. Bottom: τp(ND + ni) + τn(p + ni) ≈ τpND.
  4. So R − G ≈ Δp/τp: the rate is set by the minority carrier and its lifetime. (Checked numerically for ND = 1016, Δp = 1012: within 1 %.)
Check 9

(a) ET = Ei: what are n1 and p1? (b) Why does the minority carrier set the rate in doped Si? (c) Sign of R − G where np < ni2?

10 · Lifetime: excess carriers die away exponentially

practice

Light creates Δp = 1014 cm−3 extra holes in n-type Si with τp = 1 μs. The light goes off at t = 0. How many extra holes are left after 1 μs and after 3 μs?

Guess first

After 1 μs, none, about half, or about a third?

Terms

Excess Δp: holes above the equilibrium value p0. Minority-carrier lifetime τp: the average time an extra hole survives before it recombines.

  • From step 9: the loss rate is Δp/τp. A loss proportional to what is left gives an exponential:
dΔpdt = −Δpτp   ⇒   Δp(t) = Δp(0) e−t/τp
  • Each lifetime leaves 1/e = 0.368 of what there was.

Answer. t = 1 μs = τ: 1014 × 0.368 = 3.68 × 1013 cm−3. t = 3 μs: 1014 × e−3 = 4.98 × 1012 cm−3 (5 %).

Check 10

(a) How many lifetimes until only 1 % is left? (b) How many until half is left? (c) 03 says τ sets how fast a diode switches off. Longer τ: faster or slower switch-off?

11 · The continuity equation: bookkeeping for a small box

Steps 2–6 gave flow; steps 7–10 gave creation and loss. One equation counts both.

Deck slides 62–64

Derive the continuity equation for electrons, and write it for holes.

Guess first

List every way the number of electrons in a small box can change.

Answer (n, F vary only along x; deck slide 63):

  1. Electrons in the box: nAΔx. Its rate of change: AΔx ∂n/∂t.
  2. In per second through the left face: Fn(x)A. Out through the right: Fn(x + Δx)A.
  3. Lost inside per second: (R − G)AΔx.
  4. Balance: AΔx ∂n/∂t = Fn(x)A − Fn(x + Δx)A − (R − G)AΔx.
  5. Divide by AΔx, let Δx → 0 (deck slide 64):
∂n∂t = −∂Fn∂x − (R − G)
  1. Use Jn = −qFn and Jp = +qFp:
∂n∂t = +1q∂Jn∂x − (R − G)   ∂p∂t = −1q∂Jp∂x − (R − G)
  • In words: change in the box = (net flow in) − (net recombination). The sign before ∂J/∂x differs only because of the carrier's charge.
Deck slide 64

The slide writes Jp = +qFn. It should be Jp = +qFp (holes' own flux). The final hole equation on the slide is right.

Check 11

(a) Steady state (∂n/∂t = 0) and no recombination: what does the equation say about Jn along x? (b) A uniform sample (no gradients, no flow): reduce the hole equation and compare with step 10. (c) Why the opposite sign of ∂J/∂x for holes?

12 · Diffusion length: how far injected carriers get

The continuity equation's most-used result: holes are pushed into an n-type region at one end (as a diode does in 03). They diffuse in and recombine on the way.

practice

Holes are held at Δp(0) = 1014 cm−3 at x = 0 of a long n-type Si region; no field. Dp = 12.4 cm2/s, τp = 1 μs. Find the diffusion length Lp and Δp at x = 50 μm.

Guess first

Will the holes reach 1 mm in? 50 μm?

Terms

Steady state: nothing changes with time (∂/∂t = 0). Diffusion length L: the distance over which the excess falls to 1/e; L = √(Dτ).

  1. No field: Jp = −qDp dΔp/dx (step 3).
  2. Steady state, hole equation of step 11, with R − G = Δp/τp (step 9): 0 = Dp d2Δp/dx2 − Δp/τp.
  3. So d2Δp/dx2 = Δp/Lp2, with Lp = √(Dpτp). The solution that dies away inside the region:
Δp(x) = Δp(0) e−x/Lp

Answer. Lp = √(12.4 × 10−6) = 3.52 × 10−3 cm = 35.2 μm. Δp(50 μm) = 1014 × e−50/35.2 = 1014 × 0.242 = 2.42 × 1013 cm−3. Almost none reach 1 mm.

  • The hole current at x = 0 is qDpΔp(0)/Lp: steeper profile (short L) → more current. This is the diode current of 03.
Check 12

(a) Ln for Dn = 35 cm2/s, τn = 1 μs? (b) τ is made 4 times longer. What happens to L? (c) Find the hole current density at x = 0 in the question.

13 · Poisson's equation: charge makes field

Deck slide 65

Write Poisson's equation for a semiconductor and explain each term.

Guess first

Where in a device is the net charge not zero?

Terms

Charge density ρ(x): net charge per cm3 (not resistivity; same letter). Permittivity ε: how strongly the material weakens a field; Si: ε = 11.7ε0 = 1.04 × 10−12 F/cm. Potential V: volts; the field is its downhill slope, E = −dV/dx.

dEdx = ρε = qε(p − n + ND+ − NA−)
  • Four charges (deck slide 65): holes (+), electrons (−), ionised donors (+), ionised acceptors (−).
  • Neutral region: they balance, ρ = 0, so the field is constant (a uniform resistor).
  • Depletion region (03): carriers gone, ions left, ρ ≠ 0: the field ramps up, and the potential bends. That is the built-in voltage.

Answer. dE/dx = ρ/ε: the field changes in proportion to the net charge; ρ = q(p − n + ND+ − NA−); ε = 11.7ε0 for Si; and E = −dV/dx links the field to the potential.

Check 13

(a) 1 μm of Si with ND+ = 1016 cm−3 and no electrons. By how much does E change across it? (b) What is ρ in a uniformly doped neutral region? (c) The letter ρ means two things in this unit. Which two?

Exam form

Diffusion (deck slides 53–55). Random motion + a concentration gradient → net flow from high to low. Fick: F = −D dn/dx. Jn,diff = +qDndn/dx; Jp,diff = −qDpdp/dx. D in cm2/s.

Total current. Jn = qnμnE + qDndn/dx; Jp = qpμpE − qDpdp/dx; J = Jn + Jp. In equilibrium Jn = Jp = 0 separately: drift cancels diffusion.

Einstein relation (deck slide 57).

  1. n = NCe−(EC − EF)/kT, EF constant.
  2. Jndrift = qnμn(1/q)dEC/dx = nμndEC/dx.
  3. Jndiff = qDndn/dx = −(q/kT)Dnn dEC/dx.
  4. Equilibrium: Jdrift = −Jdiff → Dn/μn = kT/q = VT (0.0259 V at 300 K). Same for holes.

G–R (deck slides 58–61). Generation = pair created, recombination = pair destroyed; equilibrium G = R. Direct (band-to-band): photon or phonon; GaAs. Indirect via a trap at ET near mid-gap, two steps; Si. SRH: R − G = (np − ni2)/[τp(n + n1) + τn(p + p1)]. Low injection, n-type: R − G = Δp/τp; Δp(t) = Δp(0)e−t/τp.

Continuity (deck slides 62–64). Box AΔx: AΔx ∂n/∂t = Fn(x)A − Fn(x + Δx)A − (R − G)AΔx → ∂n/∂t = (1/q)∂Jn/∂x − (R − G); ∂p/∂t = −(1/q)∂Jp/∂x − (R − G).

Diffusion length. Steady state, no field: Dpd2Δp/dx2 = Δp/τp → Δp(x) = Δp(0)e−x/Lp, Lp = √(Dpτp).

Poisson (deck slide 65). dE/dx = ρ/ε = (q/ε)(p − n + ND+ − NA−); E = −dV/dx.

Traps

  • Sign of the electron diffusion current: +qDndn/dx (the minus of Fick's law cancels with −q).
  • Copying the slide-65 hole equation with +qDpdp/dx: it is −.
  • VT = 26 mV, not 26 V: Dn = 1350 × 0.0259, not × 26.
  • Saying equilibrium means "no carrier motion": both currents can be large; they cancel.
  • Thinking diffusion needs a field: it needs only a gradient.
  • Using τ of the majority carrier: lifetime in doped material is the minority carrier's.
  • Writing L = Dτ: it is √(Dτ).
