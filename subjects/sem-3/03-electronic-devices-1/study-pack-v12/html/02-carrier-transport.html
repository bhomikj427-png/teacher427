Build

1 · Drift, mobility, conductivity

Deck slide 51

(a) Calculate the conductivity and resistivity of intrinsic silicon at 300 K. (b) How would σ change if the temperature rose by 10 °C? Given μn = 1500, μp = 450 cm2/V·s.

Guess first

Bigger or smaller than 1 Ω·cm?

Terms

Field E: volts per cm. Current density J: current per unit area, A/cm2. Scattering: a carrier colliding with a vibrating atom or an ion.

A field accelerates carriers and scattering keeps stopping them, so they settle at vd = μE. Electrons are faster than holes (Si: μn ≈ 1350–1500, μp ≈ 450–480). Mobility falls as temperature or doping rise. At very high fields vd saturates near 107 cm/s. Both carriers add:

Jdrift = q(nμn + pμp)E = σE   ρ = 1/σ

Sheet resistance. IC resistors are thin doped layers of thickness t: Rs = ρ/t (Ω/□), and R = Rs × L/W. A square of any size has resistance Rs.

Answer. (a) σ = 1.6 × 10−19 × 1010 × 1950 = 3.12 × 10−6 (Ω·cm)−1; ρ = 3.2 × 105 Ω·cm. (b) ni doubles while the mobilities barely change: σ doubles. (Slide 52 dopes it with ND = 5 × 1017: σ = 32 (Ω·cm)−1, ten million times larger.)

Check 1

n-type Si, ND = 1016, μn = 1350. Find (a) σ and ρ; (b) vd at 100 V/cm; (c) Rs of a 1 μm layer; (d) R of a strip with L/W = 10.

2 · Diffusion and the Einstein relation

practice

In a silicon bar the electron concentration falls by 1018 cm−3 per cm. μn = 1350 cm2/V·s at 300 K. Find Dn and the electron diffusion current density.

Guess first

No voltage is applied. Can a current still flow?

Random thermal motion gives a net flow from crowded to sparse, proportional to the gradient:

Jn,diff = +qDn dn/dx   Jp,diff = −qDp dp/dx

The signs differ because electrons carry −q. Drift and diffusion are limited by the same scattering, so:

Dn/μn = Dp/μp = kT/q = VT

Total current: Jn = qnμnE + qDndn/dx; Jp = qpμpE − qDpdp/dx. In equilibrium Jn = 0 and Jp = 0 separately: wherever a gradient exists, a built-in field makes a drift that cancels it. That is the pn junction (03).

Answer. Yes. Dn = 1350 × 0.0259 = 34.9 cm2/s. |J| = 1.6 × 10−19 × 34.9 × 1018 = 5.59 A/cm2.

Check 2

(a) Dp for μp = 480? (b) Name the cause of drift and of diffusion in one phrase each. (c) Write the total hole current density.

3 · Generation, recombination, continuity, Poisson

Syllabus Unit 1

Write the continuity equation for electrons and state what each term means.

Guess first

List every way the number of electrons in a small box can change.

  • Generation (G) creates a pair (heat or light); recombination (R) destroys one. In equilibrium G = R, which holds n·p at ni2.
  • Direct (band-to-band) recombination gives light and dominates in GaAs. In Si the electron drops through a mid-gap trap in two steps (Shockley–Read–Hall), and the energy becomes heat.
  • Excess minority carriers decay as Δp(t) = Δp(0)e−t/τ; τ is the lifetime. It sets how fast a diode switches off (03).
∂n/∂t = (1/q)∂Jn/∂x − (R − G)   ∂p/∂t = −(1/q)∂Jp/∂x − (R − G)

Poisson links charge to field: dE/dx = ρ/ε = (q/ε)(p − n + ND − NA), with E = −dV/dx and ε = 11.7ε0 for Si. In neutral material ρ = 0 and the field is constant; in a depletion region it ramps (03).

Answer. Left: the rate of change of electron concentration at a point. First term: net flow into the slice (current in minus out). Second: net loss by recombination over generation. For holes the flow term changes sign (+q).

Check 3

(a) Why does recombination in Si mostly go through traps? (b) τ = 1 μs: what fraction of excess holes is left after 3 μs? (c) Where in a device is ρ not zero?

Exam form

Drift. vd = μE; σ = q(nμn + pμp); ρ = 1/σ; sheet resistance Rs = ρ/t, R = RsL/W.

Diffusion. J expressions with signs; Einstein D/μ = VT; total current for each carrier; equilibrium balance.

Continuity and Poisson. Both equations with every term named; G–R mechanisms; lifetime.

Traps

  • Forgetting the hole term (or the electron term) in σ for intrinsic material.
  • Sign of the diffusion current for electrons.
  • Using VT = 26 V instead of 26 mV.
  • Thinking current needs a field: diffusion flows with none.

Self-test

  1. p-type Si, NA = 1016, μp = 450. Find σ and ρ.
  2. Find Dn for μn = 1500 at 300 K.
  3. Rs = 100 Ω/□. A resistor is 200 μm long and 10 μm wide. Find R.
  4. τ = 2 μs. What fraction of an excess is left after 2 μs?
  5. Write the continuity equation for holes.
  6. Why must Jn and Jp each be zero in equilibrium?

Answers

Check 1. (a) σ = 1.6 × 10−19 × 1016 × 1350 = 2.16 (Ω·cm)−1; ρ = 0.462 Ω·cm. (b) 1.35 × 105 cm/s. (c) 0.462/10−4 = 4620 Ω/□. (d) 46.2 kΩ.

Check 2. (a) 480 × 0.0259 = 12.4 cm2/s. (b) Drift: an electric field. Diffusion: a concentration gradient. (c) Jp = qpμpE − qDpdp/dx.

Check 3. (a) Si has an indirect gap, so a direct drop is unlikely; a mid-gap trap lets it fall in two easy steps. (b) e−3 ≈ 5%. (c) The depletion (space-charge) region.

Self-test.

  1. σ = 1.6 × 10−19 × 1016 × 450 = 0.72 (Ω·cm)−1; ρ = 1.39 Ω·cm.
  2. 1500 × 0.0259 = 38.9 cm2/s.
  3. 20 squares: 2 kΩ.
  4. e−1 = 0.368.
  5. ∂p/∂t = −(1/q)∂Jp/∂x − (R − G).
  6. With no applied bias, any net flow of either carrier would build up charge somewhere forever; the built-in field adjusts until drift exactly cancels diffusion for each carrier.
