You have met feedback twice without the name: the unbypassed source resistor (07 §6) and the emitter resistor (11 §3) both sent part of the output back to oppose the input. This file gives the general theory: one formula, four connections, and what each does.

Build

1 · The gain with feedback

Deck slide 32 "Stabilization of Gain"

An amplifier has feedback factor β = 0.01. Find the gain with feedback when the open-loop gain is 1000, and again when it is 10 000. What do you notice?

Guess first

The open-loop gain grows ten times. Does the gain with feedback grow ten times?

Terms

Open-loop gain A: the amplifier alone. Feedback factor β: the fraction of the output returned to the input (not the transistor's β). Closed-loop gain Af: the whole circuit with feedback.

In negative feedback the returned signal is subtracted; in positive feedback it adds (14). The amplifier sees Vi = Vs − βVo:

Vo = A(Vs − βVo)   ⟹   Af = Vo/Vs = A/(1 + βA)
Name Expression Meaning
Loop gain βA gain once round the loop
Amount of feedback (desensitivity) D 1 + βA gain falls by this factor; everything else improves by it
Feedback in dB 20 log(1 + βA)

The key result. If βA ≫ 1, Af ≈ 1/β: the gain depends only on the feedback network, made of stable resistors. The four basic amplifiers: voltage (V in, V out; wants high Ri, low Ro), current (I in, I out; low Ri, high Ro), transconductance (V in, I out; high Ri, high Ro), transresistance (I in, V out; low Ri, low Ro).

Answer. A = 1000: Af = 1000/11 = 90.9. A = 10 000: 10 000/101 = 99.0. A changed by 900%, Af by only 9%, and both are close to 1/β = 100. Feedback trades gain for stability.

Slip in the slide

Slide 32 prints 98.3 for A = 5000. The formula gives 5000/51 = 98.0. The other entries (90.9, 99.0, 99.5) are correct.

2 · The four topologies

practice · deck slides 16–27, 41–45

A CE amplifier has an unbypassed emitter resistor RE. Identify the feedback topology and say what it does to the input and output resistance.

Guess first

Is the voltage across RE proportional to the output voltage or to the output current?

Terms

Sampling: how the feedback senses the output: its voltage (across the load) or its current (in series with it). Mixing: how it is combined at the input: a voltage in series, or a current in shunt.

Voltage-series Voltage-shunt Current-series Current-shunt
Samples · mixes Vo · series V Vo · shunt I Io · series V Io · shunt I
Improves a voltage amp transresistance amp transconductance amp current amp
β Vf/Vo If/Vo (S) Vf/Io (Ω) If/Io
Zif Zi(1 + βA) ↑ Zi/(1 + βA) ↓ Zi(1 + βA) ↑ Zi/(1 + βA) ↓
Zof Zo/(1 + βA) ↓ Zo/(1 + βA) ↓ Zo(1 + βA) ↑ Zo(1 + βA) ↑
Example emitter/source follower collector-to-base resistor CE with unbypassed RE two stages, 2nd emitter → 1st base

Two rules cover the table (slide 40): a series connection at a port raises its resistance by (1 + βA); a shunt connection lowers it. At the output, voltage sampling is a shunt connection (Zo falls) and current sampling is series (Zo rises).

Answer. Vf = IoRE is proportional to the output current and sits in series with the input (vbe = vs − vf): current-series, β = −RE. Both Zi and Zo rise by (1 + βA). With heavy feedback the gain becomes about −RC/RE.

3 · What negative feedback does

practice · deck slides 28–40

A = 1000, fH = 20 kHz, Zi = 2 kΩ, Zo = 20 kΩ, 10% distortion. Voltage-series feedback with β = 0.1 is applied. Find Af, the new fH, Zif, Zof and the distortion.

Guess first

One number, 1 + βA, appears in every answer. What is it here?

Effect Formula Why
Gain reduced Af = A/(1 + βA) the price
Gain stabilised dAf/Af = (dA/A)/(1 + βA) a change in A shrinks by (1 + βA)
Bandwidth widened fHf = fH(1 + βA), fLf = fL/(1 + βA) where A falls, feedback falls with it
Distortion, noise reduced Df = D/(1 + βA) inside-loop distortion is fed back inverted
Impedances modified × or ÷ (1 + βA) per step 2

Disadvantages. Lower gain, so more stages; and if phase shifts add to 180° at some frequency, the feedback turns positive and the amplifier can oscillate (14 does it on purpose).

Answer. 1 + βA = 101. Af = 9.90. fHf = 2.02 MHz. Zif = 2 k × 101 = 202 kΩ, Zof = 20 k/101 = 198 Ω. Distortion 0.099%. Check: gain × bandwidth = 20 MHz before and after.

Check 3

Same amplifier: (a) A drops by 10% as it warms. By what percentage does Af change? (b) fL = 100 Hz. Find fLf. (c) List four advantages and one disadvantage of negative feedback.

Exam form

Concept. Block diagram; derive Af = A/(1 + βA); loop gain, desensitivity; Af ≈ 1/β.

Topologies. The 4-column table with Zif, Zof and an example; the two rules (series raises, shunt lowers).

Effects. Table: gain, stability (derive dAf/Af by differentiating Af), bandwidth, distortion, impedances; disadvantages.

Traps

  • Confusing the feedback β with the transistor's β.
  • Applying "Zo falls" to current sampling: current sampling raises Zo.
  • Forgetting that the lower cutoff divides by (1 + βA) while the upper multiplies.
  • Writing 98.3 from slide 32.

Self-test

  1. Derive dAf/Af = (dA/A)/(1 + βA).
  2. A = 500, β = 0.02. Find Af.
  3. An amplifier with A = 2000 must have Af = 50. Find β.
  4. A resistor from collector to base of a CE stage: which topology, and what happens to Zi?
  5. A = 200, fH = 50 kHz, β = 0.05. Find the new fH.
  6. Why can too much negative feedback make an amplifier oscillate?

Answers

Check 1. (a) Vi = Vs − βVo; Vo = A(Vs − βVo); Vo(1 + βA) = AVs. (b) 20 000/201 = 99.5. (c) ≈ 1/β: the gain depends only on stable resistors, not on the transistor.

Check 2. (a) Voltage-series. (b) Voltage-shunt. (c) All of it: Vf = Vo, β = 1; voltage-series.

Check 3. (a) About 10%/101 ≈ 0.1% (exactly: A = 900 gives 9.89, a fall of 0.11%). (b) 100/101 = 0.99 Hz. (c) Stable gain, wider bandwidth, less distortion and noise, impedances raised or lowered as needed. Against: reduced gain (and possible instability).

Self-test.

  1. Af = A/(1 + βA); dAf/dA = 1/(1 + βA)2; divide by Af: dAf/Af = (dA/A)/(1 + βA).
  2. 1 + βA = 11: Af = 45.5.
  3. 1 + βA = 2000/50 = 40, so β = 39/2000 = 0.0195.
  4. Voltage-shunt: Zi falls by (1 + βA).
  5. 1 + βA = 11: fHf = 550 kHz.
  6. At high frequency the amplifier's own phase shift can reach 180°; the subtracted signal then adds, and if the loop gain is ≥ 1 there, the circuit sustains its own output.
