1 · The Barkhausen criterion

Syllabus Unit 7

State the Barkhausen criterion for sustained oscillations.

Guess first

With positive feedback, Af = A/(1 − βA). What value of βA makes the gain infinite?

Terms

Oscillator: produces a repeating output with no input signal; converts DC power into AC. Positive feedback: the returned signal is in phase with the input and adds to it.

When βA = 1 the denominator is zero: a finite output with zero input. A signal that goes once round the loop and arrives back with the same size and phase sustains itself.

Answer. Sustained sinusoidal oscillation occurs at the frequency where |βA| = 1 and the total loop phase shift is 0° or 360°. If |βA| < 1 the oscillation dies; if > 1 it grows until the amplifier clips. Start-up: circuit noise contains every frequency; the frequency-selective network lets only one meet the phase condition. In practice |βA| is set slightly above 1, and the amplifier's non-linearity brings it back to 1.

Type Feedback network Range Examples
RC resistors, capacitors a few Hz to about 1 MHz (audio) phase-shift, Wien bridge
LC resonant tank 100 kHz to 100 MHz (radio) Hartley, Colpitts
Crystal quartz crystal 10 kHz to 30 MHz, very stable clocks
Relaxation RC timing + switch — square, triangle, sawtooth

2 · The RC phase-shift oscillator

Deck design example

Design a phase-shift oscillator for a frequency of 800 Hz. The capacitors are to be 10 nF.

Guess first

One RC section shifts the phase by less than 90°. How many sections are needed for 180°?

The inverting amplifier gives 180°; three identical RC sections give the other 180° at exactly one frequency:

f0 = 1/(2πRC√6)   β = 1/29 at f0   so |A| ≥ 29, Rf = 29R

The third R is the op-amp's input resistor (the inverting input is a virtual ground). FET version: same f0, gmRL ≥ 29. BJT version: f0 = 1/(2πRC√(6 + 4RC/R)), hfe ≥ 23 + 29(R/RC) + 4(RC/R). Tuning needs all three R or C changed together, so it suits fixed audio frequencies.

Answer. R = 1/(2π × 800 × 10−8 × 2.449) = 8.12 kΩ; use 8.2 kΩ. Rf = 29 × 8.2 k = 238 kΩ (the deck's value). With 8.2 kΩ the frequency is 792 Hz.

Check 2

(a) Why three sections, not two? (b) R = 10 kΩ, C = 0.01 μF: find f0 and Rf.

3 · The Wien-bridge oscillator

practice · Wien-bridge slides

A Wien-bridge oscillator uses R = 10 kΩ and C = 10 nF. Find f0. If R1 = 10 kΩ, what should R2 be?

Guess first

This amplifier is non-inverting. How much phase shift may the feedback network add?

The lead-lag network: at low f the series C blocks; at high f the parallel C shorts. At one frequency the output is largest and in phase:

f0 = 1/(2πRC)   β = 1/3, 0°   1 + R2/R1 = 3   ⟹   R2 = 2R1

To start, R2/R1 is made slightly more than 2. The two RC arms with R1, R2 form a bridge. Clean sine, easy tuning (gang the two Cs): the standard audio oscillator.

RC phase-shift Wien bridge
Amplifier inverting (180°) non-inverting (0°)
Network three RC sections, 180° lead-lag, 0°
f0 1/(2πRC√6) 1/(2πRC)
β · gain needed 1/29 · 29 1/3 · 3
Tuning awkward easy

Answer. f0 = 1/(2π × 104 × 10−8) = 1.59 kHz. R2 = 2R1 = 20 kΩ (slightly more, to start).

(a) To double the frequency, what do you do to C? (b) Why is the amplifier non-inverting here but inverting in the phase-shift oscillator?

4 · LC oscillators: Hartley and Colpitts

practice

A Colpitts oscillator has C1 = C2 = 200 pF and L = 1 mH. Find the frequency of oscillation.

Guess first

The two capacitors are in series round the tank. Is their combined value more or less than 200 pF?

Terms

Tank circuit: L and C in a loop; energy swings between them at f0 = 1/(2π√(LC)).

A tank rings and dies away; the amplifier tops up the lost energy each cycle. The grounded tap puts the tank's two ends 180° apart, which with an inverting amplifier makes 360°.

Hartley Colpitts
Tapped element inductor: L1, L2 capacitor: C1, C2 in series
Equivalent Leq = L1 + L2 (+ 2M if coupled) Ceq = C1C2/(C1 + C2)
f0 1/(2π√(LeqC)) 1/(2π√(LCeq))
Notes one variable C tunes it better stability at high f

Memory hook: Colpitts splits the Capacitor.

Answer. Ceq = 100 pF. f0 = 1/(2π√(10−3 × 10−10)) = 503 kHz.

5 · The crystal oscillator

Deck "crystal"

A quartz crystal has Ls = 1 H, Cs = 0.01 pF, Rs = 1 kΩ and Cp = 10 pF. Find its series resonant frequency, how far above it the parallel resonance lies, and its Q.

Guess first

An ordinary LC tank has a Q of about 100. Will the crystal's be similar?

Terms

Piezoelectric effect: squeezing quartz produces a voltage; the inverse effect: a voltage makes it change shape. Q: how sharply a resonator selects one frequency, Q = ωsL/R.

An AC voltage makes the quartz vibrate; it vibrates strongly only at its mechanical frequency (set by cut and thickness), and the vibration produces a voltage back. Electrically: Rs, Ls, Cs (friction, mass, stiffness) in parallel with Cp (plates and holder).

fs = 1/(2π√(LsCs))   fp = fs√(1 + Cs/Cp)

At fs the impedance is lowest (Rs); at fp, just above, it is very large; between them the crystal is inductive. In an oscillator it replaces the tank or the inductor of a Colpitts circuit.

Answer. fs = 1/(2π√(10−14)) = 1.592 MHz. fp = fs × 1.0005, 795 Hz above. Q = 2π × 1.592 × 106 × 1/1000 = 10 000, about 100 times an LC tank. So the frequency hardly moves with temperature, supply or ageing; it cannot be tuned over a range.

Check 5

(a) Draw the equivalent circuit from memory and name each element. (b) Why is a crystal oscillator more stable than an LC oscillator?

Exam form

Barkhausen. |βA| = 1, loop phase 0°/360°; start-up from noise with |βA| slightly > 1.

Phase-shift. Circuit; three sections, 180°; f0 = 1/(2πRC√6); β = 1/29, A ≥ 29.

Wien bridge. Circuit; lead-lag 0°; f0 = 1/(2πRC); β = 1/3, A = 3, R2 = 2R1. Comparison table.

LC. Hartley and Colpitts circuits, Leq / Ceq, f0; comparison.

Crystal. Piezoelectric effect; equivalent circuit; fs, fp; Q 104–106; why stable.

Traps

  • Writing the Wien gain as 29 or the phase-shift gain as 3: swap check with β.
  • Adding C1 + C2 for Colpitts: they are in series.
  • Forgetting √6 in the phase-shift formula.
  • Writing f0 = 1/(2πLC) without the square root.

Answers

Check 1. (a) 0.9: dies out. 1.5: grows until the amplifier saturates; clipped, distorted. (b) Another 180°.

Check 2. (a) One section gives less than 90°, so two give less than 180°; three is the fewest that can reach 180°. (b) f0 = 1/(2π × 104 × 10−8 × 2.449) = 650 Hz; Rf = 290 kΩ.

Check 3. (a) Halve both capacitors. (b) The Wien network adds 0°, so the amplifier must add 0°; the phase-shift network adds 180°, so the amplifier adds the other 180°.

Check 4. (a) Leq = 2 mH: f0 = 1/(2π√(2 × 10−13)) = 356 kHz. (b) Low f would need very large, heavy, costly L and C.

Check 5. (a) Rs, Ls, Cs in series (friction loss, vibrating mass, stiffness), all in parallel with Cp (plates and holder). (b) Its Q is far higher (104–106), so the resonance is very sharp and barely drifts.
