The FETs (07, 08) use a voltage to control a channel. The bipolar junction transistor is two pn junctions back to back, and a small current into the thin middle layer controls a large current through the device.

Build

1 · Construction and operation

Deck L20

Why is the base of a transistor made very thin and lightly doped, while the emitter is heavily doped?

Guess first

What should happen to the electrons the emitter sends into the base?

Terms

Emitter (E): supplies (emits) the carriers. Base (B): the thin middle layer that controls them. Collector (C): collects them.

  • Three layers, n–p–n or p–n–p, so two junctions: emitter–base and collector–base. "Bipolar": both electrons and holes take part.
  • Active region (npn): EB forward biased, so the emitter injects electrons into the base. Only about 1% recombine there; that needs a small IB. The other 99% diffuse across and are swept into the collector by the reverse-biased CB junction: IC.
  • A small IB controls a large IC: the BJT is current-controlled. For pnp, swap n and p and reverse everything.
IE = IC + IB
Region EB CB Behaviour · use
Active forward reverse IC = βIB, VBE ≈ 0.7 V. Amplifier
Saturation forward forward IC < βIB, set by the circuit; VCE(sat) ≈ 0.2 V. Closed switch
Cutoff reverse reverse IC ≈ 0. Open switch

Answer. Almost every carrier from the emitter should reach the collector. A thin, lightly doped base has few holes and gives electrons little time to meet one, so few recombine: IB is small and IC/IB large. The heavy emitter doping makes the EB current almost all electrons into the base, not holes back into the emitter. The collector is the largest region because it dissipates the most heat.

2 · The three connections and α, β, γ

practice · decks L22–L25

A transistor has β = 100 and ICBO = 20 nA. Find α and ICEO.

Guess first

Is α slightly below 1 or slightly above?

An amplifier needs two input and two output wires, so one terminal is shared ("common"):

Common base (CB) Common emitter (CE) Common collector (CC)
In · out emitter · collector base · collector base · emitter
Current gain α = IC/IE, 0.95–0.995 β = IC/IB, 50–400 γ = IE/IB = 1 + β
Voltage gain high high just under 1
Rin very low (tens of Ω) medium (≈ 1 kΩ) very high
Rout very high medium–high very low
Phase 0° 180° 0°
Use high frequency; low-R source general: highest power gain buffer, matching

Relations. Substitute IE = IC + IB into the definitions:

β = α/(1 − α)   α = β/(1 + β)   γ = 1 + β = 1/(1 − α)

Leakage. ICBO (ICO) is the CB leakage with the emitter open. In CE the transistor amplifies its own leakage: ICEO = (1 + β)ICBO, the collector current with the base open. Complete expressions: IC = αIE + ICBO; IC = βIB + (1 + β)ICBO.

Characteristics. Input curves (IB against VBE) look like a forward diode. Output curves (IC against VCE, one per IB) are nearly flat in the active region (11 §1).

Answer. α = 100/101 = 0.990. ICEO = 101 × 20 nA = 2.02 μA.

Check 3

(a) Two reasons MOSFETs replaced BJTs in digital ICs. (b) Which gives more voltage gain per stage, and which parameter explains it? (c) Why is a MOSFET thermally more stable?

Exam form

Operation. Sketch npn with biases; electron flow: injection, ~1% recombination (IB), collection (IC); IE = IC + IB; doping and width reasons. Region table.

Connections. The CB/CE/CC table. Derive β = α/(1 − α), γ = 1 + β. ICEO = (1 + β)ICBO.

Comparison. BJT vs MOSFET table, at least eight rows.

Traps

  • Writing VBE ≈ 0.7 V in cutoff: it is below that.
  • Swapping α and β: α < 1 is CB; β ≫ 1 is CE.
  • Forgetting the (1 + β) when leakage flows in CE.
  • Saying CC has voltage gain: it has current gain only (γ), voltage gain just under 1.

Self-test

  1. IE = 10 mA, α = 0.99. Find IC and IB.
  2. β = 150. Find α.
  3. ICBO = 10 nA, β = 100. Find ICEO.
  4. Draw the CB/CE/CC table from memory (gains, resistances, phase).
  5. Why is CE the most used connection?
  6. Explain why the collector current hardly depends on VCE in the active region.

Answers

Check 1. (a) EB forward, CB reverse. (b) IC = 5 − 0.05 = 4.95 mA. (c) Cutoff (open) and saturation (closed).

Check 2. (a) β = 0.98/0.02 = 49; γ = 50. (b) β = IC/IB = IC/(IE − IC); divide by IE: α/(1 − α). (c) Common collector: high Rin, very low Rout, so it drives a low-resistance load without loading the stage before.

Check 3. (a) Near-zero static power in CMOS; far smaller, so higher density (also simpler fabrication). (b) The BJT: gm = IC/VT is higher at the same current. (c) Its current falls as temperature rises, because mobility falls.

Self-test.

  1. IC = 9.9 mA, IB = 0.1 mA.
  2. α = 150/151 = 0.9934.
  3. 101 × 10 nA = 1.01 μA.
  4. See step 2.
  5. It is the only connection with both current gain and voltage gain, so it has the highest power gain; its Rin and Rout are moderate.
  6. The CB junction is reverse biased; its field sweeps across every electron that reaches it. IC is set by how many electrons the emitter injects (by VBE, IB), not by how hard they are pulled.
