Part A · 10 × 2 = 20 marks

  1. Define mobility and give its unit.
  2. State the mass-action law.
  3. Give two reasons why silicon is preferred over germanium.
  4. Define peak inverse voltage. What is the PIV of each diode in a bridge rectifier?
  5. Give two differences between Zener and avalanche breakdown.
  6. Define the pinch-off voltage of a JFET.
  7. Sheet Q6 A reverse gate voltage of 15 V gives a gate current of 10−3 μA. Find the gate–source resistance.
  8. Why is an enhancement MOSFET called "normally off"?
  9. Write the relation between α and β. Find β when α = 0.99.
  10. State the Barkhausen criterion.

Part B · 6 × 6 = 36 marks

  1. Derive the conductivity of a semiconductor. Find the resistivity of intrinsic Ge at 300 K: ni = 2.5 × 1013 cm−3, μn = 3800, μp = 1800 cm2/V·s.
  2. Draw a centre-tapped full-wave rectifier and explain it with waveforms. Secondary 9 V–0–9 V rms, load 1 kΩ, ideal diodes: find Vdc, Idc and the PIV.
  3. Sheet Q29 A D-MOSFET amplifier: VDD = +15 V, RD = 620 Ω, gate to ground through RG = 10 MΩ (VGS = 0), source grounded, load RL = 8.2 kΩ capacitor-coupled to the drain. IDSS = 12 mA, gm = 3.2 mS. Find (i) VDS and (ii) the AC output voltage for vin = 500 mV.
  4. Divider bias: VCC = 16 V, R1 = 62 kΩ, R2 = 9.1 kΩ, RC = 3.9 kΩ, RE = 0.68 kΩ, β = 150. Approximate method: VB, IC, VCE. Then S.
  5. Book P3 hic = 1.1 k, hrc = 1, hfc = −51, hoc = 25 μA/V. Find AI, AV, AVS, Ri and Ro for CC with RS = RL = 10 k.
  6. Draw the block diagram of a negative-feedback amplifier and derive its gain. A = 60 dB, Zo = 10 kΩ, voltage-series feedback with β = 0.05: find Af (number and dB) and Zof.

Part C · 2 × 8 = 16 marks

  1. Define the four characteristic parameters of a JFET, stating what is held constant. Derive μ = gmrd. From Shockley, show gm = gm0(1 − VGS/VGS(off)).
  2. Explain the RC phase-shift oscillator with a circuit and state the conditions for oscillation. Design one for 1 kHz with 0.01 μF capacitors.

Part D · 1 × 8 = 8 marks

  1. (a) Sheet Q31 An E-MOSFET is biased by a divider: VDD = 24 V, R1 = 100 kΩ (top), R2 = 15 kΩ, RD = 470 Ω, source grounded. ID(on) = 500 mA at VGS = 10 V, VGS(th) = 1 V. Find VGS and VDS. (b) Draw a two-input CMOS NAND and explain it with a truth table.

Key

Part A.

  1. Drift velocity per unit field, μ = vd/E; cm2/V·s. (02)
  2. In equilibrium n·p = ni2, whatever the doping. (01)
  3. Any two: larger gap, so far smaller leakage; works at higher temperature (≈ 150 °C against 75 °C); higher PIV; cheap, forms a good oxide. (01a, 03b)
  4. The largest reverse voltage across a diode while it blocks. Bridge: Vm. (04)
  5. Zener: heavy doping, thin region, field pulls electrons from bonds, below ≈ 5 V, negative coefficient. Avalanche: light doping, wide region, multiplication by collision, above ≈ 6 V, positive coefficient. (05)
  6. The VDS, at VGS = 0, at which the channel pinches off and ID levels at IDSS; equal to |VGS(off)|. (07)
  7. 15 V/10−9 A = 1.5 × 1010 Ω.
  8. It has no channel at VGS = 0, so no current flows until VGS > VT. (08)
  9. β = α/(1 − α) = 0.99/0.01 = 99. (10)
  10. |βA| = 1 and loop phase 0° or 360°. (14)
