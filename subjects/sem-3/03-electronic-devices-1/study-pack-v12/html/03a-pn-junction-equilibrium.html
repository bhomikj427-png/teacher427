Build

1 · The diode: p-type next to n-type

Predict

A piece of silicon is p-type on its left half and n-type on its right half. You connect a battery one way, then the other way. Will the current be the same both ways?

Terms

pn junction: the boundary where p-type silicon meets n-type silicon inside one crystal. Diode: a device made of one pn junction with a metal contact on each end. Anode / cathode: the p-side terminal / the n-side terminal.

  • The junction is not two pieces glued together. It is one crystal whose doping changes from acceptors to donors (step 2).
  • A diode lets current through in one direction only: from anode to cathode. The other way it blocks. This is called rectifying (deck slide 75).
  • Uses (deck slide 66): on its own as a diode; as a building block of every transistor (07–12); and to isolate devices from each other on a chip.

Answer to the Predict. No. One direction gives a large current, the other almost none. Steps 4–12 find out why.

Check 1

(a) Which terminal of a diode is the anode, and which side of the junction is it on? (b) In the symbol, which way does the triangle point? (c) Is a pn junction two crystals pressed together?

2 · How a junction is made

The deck shows the steps before the physics (slides 67–69). Each step only adds or removes material from one region.

Predict

You want boron (an acceptor) in one small patch of an n-type wafer and nowhere else. How could you keep it out of the rest of the surface?

Terms

Epitaxial (epi) layer: a thin, lightly doped layer of crystal grown on top of the wafer. Oxide (SiO2): glass grown on the silicon; boron cannot get through it. Photoresist and mask: a light-sensitive coating; UV light through a patterned plate (the mask) marks where the oxide will be removed.

  • The oxide is the stencil: boron enters only where the window is.
  • The boron diffuses in from the surface, so its concentration fades with depth. Step 3 replaces this gradual profile with a sharp step.
  • The n⁺ wafer underneath (very heavily doped) is a good conductor that carries current to the bottom contact.

Answer to the Predict. Cover the surface with oxide, open a window only where you want the p region, and diffuse boron through the window.

Check 2

(a) What does the oxide do during the boron step? (b) Which step decides the size of the p region? (c) Why is the bottom layer n⁺ and not lightly doped?

3 · The abrupt-junction model

Real boron fades out with depth (step 2). To calculate anything, the deck replaces the real profile with a simpler one (slide 70).

Predict

Which is easier to calculate with: doping that changes smoothly over 0.1 μm, or doping that jumps at one point?

Terms

Abrupt (step) junction: doping NA everywhere left of a plane and ND everywhere right of it. Junction plane x = 0 (deck: xj): where the doping switches. One-sided junction: one side much more heavily doped than the other, written p⁺n or n⁺p.

  • Left of x = 0: p-type, NA = 1017 acceptors per cm3. Right: n-type, ND = 1016 donors per cm3.
  • Far from the junction each side is just the uniform material of 01a: p ≈ NA on the p side, n ≈ ND on the n side.
  • The model gives formulas you can use by hand; the real profile only changes the numbers a little.

Answer to the Predict. The jump. With a step, each side has one constant doping, so each side can be solved on its own and the two answers joined at x = 0.

Check 3

(a) In the running example, which side is more heavily doped, and by what factor? (b) What do "p⁺n" and the ⁺ mean? (c) Far from the junction on the n side, about how many electrons per cm3 are there?

4 · At contact, carriers cross

Now let the two sides meet. Use 01a's mass-action law (n·p = ni2) to count both carriers on each side first.

practice

Before any carriers move: how many holes and how many electrons per cm3 are on each side of the running junction? By what factor do the hole counts on the two sides differ?

Guess first

Holes are packed on one side and scarce on the other. Which way will they move, and what makes them move? (02b step 1)

Terms

Majority carrier: the carrier the doping provides (holes in p, electrons in n). Minority carrier: the other one, made only by heat (01a step 8).

Side Majority Minority = ni2/majority
p side (NA = 1017) holes p = 1017 electrons n = 1020/1017 = 103
n side (ND = 1016) electrons n = 1016 holes p = 1020/1016 = 104
  • Holes: 1017 on the left, 104 on the right. A steep slope in a crowd means diffusion (02b steps 1–2): holes flow p → n.
  • Electrons: 1016 on the right, 103 on the left: electrons diffuse n → p.
  • A hole that crosses lands among many electrons and soon recombines (02b step 7); so does an electron that crosses.

Answer. p side: 1017 holes, 103 electrons. n side: 1016 electrons, 104 holes. The hole counts differ by 1017/104 = 1013; so do the electron counts (1016/103). That huge ratio drives the diffusion, and step 8 turns it into a voltage.

(a) Redo the table for NA = 1016, ND = 1018. (b) In which direction do electrons diffuse, and why? (c) What happens to a hole after it crosses into the n side?

5 · The bare ions: the depletion region

Predict

A hole leaves the p side. The acceptor atom it came from stays fixed in the crystal. Is that region now neutral, positive or negative?

Terms

Ion: a dopant atom whose carrier has left: an acceptor ion is −, a donor ion is + (01a steps 6–7). Ions are part of the crystal and cannot move. Depletion region (space-charge region): the strip around the junction emptied of mobile carriers, holding only fixed ions. Depletion approximation: treat that strip as having no carriers at all and sharp edges; outside it, everything is neutral (deck slide 72).

  • Before contact each side was neutral: on the p side, each − acceptor ion had its + hole beside it.
  • Holes leave the p side near the junction → the − acceptor ions there are uncovered. Electrons leave the n side → the + donor ions are uncovered.
  • So the depletion region has negative charge on its p side and positive charge on its n side. That is why the deck also calls it the space-charge region (slide 72).
  • Outside the strip, carriers still match the ions: those neutral regions carry no charge.

Answer to the Predict. Negative. The acceptor ion keeps its extra electron and lost the hole that balanced it.

Check 5

(a) What charge is left on the n side of the depletion region, and from which atoms? (b) Why can the ions not move away too? (c) State the depletion approximation in one line.

6 · The built-in field stops the crossing

The uncovered charges of step 5 make a field, and a field pushes carriers (02a). This is what stops the crossing.

Predict

The n side holds + ions, the p side − ions. Which way does the field between them point? Which way does it push a hole?

Terms

Built-in field: the field set up by the ions of the depletion region, with no battery connected. Equilibrium: nothing connected and nothing changing; every current is zero (02b step 5).

  • A field points from + charge to − charge: here from the n side to the p side.
  • Holes drift along E: n → p, opposite to their diffusion (p → n).
  • Electrons drift against E: p → n, opposite to their diffusion (n → p).
  • Crossing continues, more ions are bared, the field grows, until for each carrier drift exactly cancels diffusion (deck slide 74):
Jndiff = −Jndrift   Jpdiff = −Jpdrift
  • Both currents are still flowing; they just cancel. This is 02b step 5's balance, now at a junction.

Answer to the Predict. From n to p. It pushes holes from n back toward p, against their diffusion.

Check 6

(a) Which way do electrons drift in the built-in field? (b) In equilibrium is the hole diffusion current zero? (c) Why does the crossing stop instead of continuing until both sides are mixed?

7 · The band picture: one flat Fermi level

01b step 10 put EF near EV in p-type silicon and near EC in n-type. Now both are in one crystal.

Predict

In equilibrium the Fermi level is the same everywhere (01b: EF is a single level for the whole system). On the p side it sits near EV, on the n side near EC. What must the bands do?

Terms

Band bending: EC and EV shifting up or down with position because of a voltage there. An electron's energy changes by −q × (change in potential). Barrier: the energy step an electron must climb to go from n side to p side.

  • EF is drawn flat: nothing flows, so the Fermi level is one value everywhere (deck slide 71).
  • Far on the p side EF is just above EV; far on the n side it is just below EC. To keep EF flat the bands on the p side sit higher.
  • The difference in height is qVbi: the built-in voltage Vbi times the electron charge.
  • For an electron on the n side, this is a hill: only those with enough energy can climb to the p side (03b step 2 uses this).

Answer to the Predict. They bend: EC and EV rise from the n side to the p side by the same amount, qVbi, so that one flat EF fits both sides.

Check 7

(a) Which side's bands sit higher? (b) In which region do the bands bend, and why there? (c) Why is EF flat in equilibrium?

8 · The built-in voltage Vbi

Now combine three things you already have: the hole count on each side (step 4), the Boltzmann count (01b step 6), and the voltage step of step 7.

practice · deck slide 73

Find Vbi for the running junction: NA = 1017, ND = 1016 cm−3, ni = 1010 cm−3, VT = 25.9 mV.

Guess first

More doping makes the crowds bigger. Does Vbi grow or shrink with doping?

Terms

Built-in voltage Vbi: the voltage across the depletion region in equilibrium, n side higher. Also called the contact potential or barrier voltage. Thermal voltage VT = kT/q: k is Boltzmann's constant (1.38 × 10−23 J/K), T the temperature in kelvin, q = 1.6 × 10−19 C. VT = 25.9 mV at 300 K.

The pieces (each line follows from the one before):

  1. Holes on the p side: pp = NA. Holes on the n side: pn = ni2/ND (step 4).
  2. Boltzmann (01b step 6): the number of holes at a place falls by e−1 for every VT of potential they would have to climb. Across the junction they climb Vbi, so pn = pp·e−Vbi/VT.
  3. Rearrange: eVbi/VT = pp/pn = NAND/ni2. Take the log:
Vbi = VT lnNANDni2
  • NA, ND inside the log: more doping → bigger ratio → larger Vbi, but only logarithmically.
  • ni2 below: a wider-gap material (smaller ni) gives a larger Vbi. Si: 0.6–0.9 V.

Answer. NAND/ni2 = 1033/1020 = 1013; ln(1013) = 29.93. Vbi = 0.0259 × 29.93 = 0.775 V.

Why a voltmeter reads 0 V across a diode

The diode's metal contacts also have built-in voltages. Around the closed loop they cancel Vbi exactly (deck slide 76, equation (1)). Vbi does no work, so no meter can read it.

9 · The charge in the depletion region: ρ(x)

Steps 9–11 find how wide the depletion region is. They follow 02b step 13's chain: charge → field → potential.

Predict

The p side is 10 times more heavily doped than the n side. The two sides of the depletion region hold equal and opposite charge. Which side's part of the strip is wider?

Terms

Charge density ρ(x): net charge per cm3 at position x (02b step 13). xp, xn: how far the depletion region reaches into the p side and into the n side. W = xp + xn: the total width.

  • Under the depletion approximation (step 5): ρ = −qNA from −xp to 0 (bare acceptors), ρ = +qND from 0 to xn (bare donors), ρ = 0 outside.
  • The whole crystal stays neutral, so the − charge equals the + charge. For a cross-section of area A: qNAxpA = qNDxnA, so
NAxp = NDxn
  • In words: the two shaded areas are equal. The lightly doped side needs a wider strip to bare the same charge.

Answer to the Predict. The n side (the lightly doped one). With NA = 10 ND, xn = 10 xp.

Check 9

(a) NA = 1018, ND = 1015. Find xn/xp. (b) Why is ρ zero outside the depletion region? (c) For a p⁺n junction, where is almost all of W?

10 · The field E(x): a triangle

Predict

02b step 13: the slope of E equals ρ/ε. On the p side ρ is a large negative constant; on the n side a small positive constant. What shape is E(x)?

Terms

Peak field Emax: the largest field strength, at the junction plane x = 0. Slope of E = ρ/ε: the rule from 02b step 13 (Poisson's equation).

  • Start at −xp with E = 0 (neutral region). Slope = −qNA/ε: E falls in a straight line to its most negative value at x = 0.
  • From 0 to xn the slope is +qND/ε: E rises in a straight line back to 0 at xn.
  • So E(x) is a triangle; its tip is
|Emax| = qNDxnε = qNAxpε
  • The two forms are equal because NAxp = NDxn (step 9).

Answer to the Predict. A triangle: zero at both edges, sloping straight down on the p side (steep) and straight back up on the n side (gentle), peak at the junction. For the running example (xn from step 11): |Emax| = 1.6 × 10−19 × 1016 × 3.03 × 10−5 / 1.04 × 10−12 = 4.66 × 104 V/cm.

Check 10

(a) Why is the p-side slope steeper? (b) Where is the field largest? (c) Find |Emax| from the p-side form, with xp = 3.03 × 10−6 cm.

11 · The potential, and the width W

Now combine steps 8, 9 and 10. The potential across the strip must add up to Vbi.

practice

For the running junction (Vbi = 0.775 V) find W, xn and xp.

Guess first

Is W closer to 1 nm, 0.3 μm or 1 mm?

Terms

Potential ψ(x): the voltage at position x, with the p side taken as 0. Its slope is −E (02b step 13), so the area under the E(x) triangle is the total voltage across it.

Combining (each line follows from the one before):

  1. Area of the field triangle = ½ × base × height: Vbi = ½|Emax|W (step 10's triangle, base W).
  2. Put in |Emax| = qNDxn/ε and xn = W·NA/(NA + ND) (from step 9).
  3. Solve for W:
W = √(2εq (1NA + 1ND) Vbi)
Symbol What it is Its effect on W
ε permittivity: a weaker-shielding material needs more width W ∝ √ε
1/NA + 1/ND the lighter doping dominates the sum lighter doping → wider
Vbi the voltage the strip must hold W ∝ √Vbi

Answer. 2ε/q = 2 × 1.04 × 10−12/1.6 × 10−19 = 1.30 × 107. (1/1017 + 1/1016) = 1.1 × 10−16. W = √(1.30 × 107 × 1.1 × 10−16 × 0.775) = 3.33 × 10−5 cm = 0.333 μm. xn = W × 10/11 = 0.303 μm; xp = W × 1/11 = 0.030 μm.

Check by a second route: ½ × 4.66 × 104 × 3.33 × 10−5 = 0.776 V ≈ Vbi. ✓

12 · Applying a voltage: Vj = Vbi − Va

A battery Va is connected, + to the p side for forward bias, + to the n side for reverse bias (Va negative).

Deck slides 77–78

For an abrupt silicon pn junction, Vbi = 0.85 V. Let W0 and W1 denote the depletion widths for Va = 0 V and Va = 0.6 V. What is W1/W0? Repeat for Va = −2 V (a reverse bias of 2 V).

Guess first

Forward bias: does the depletion region get wider or narrower?

Terms

Applied voltage Va: the battery voltage, p side minus n side. Junction voltage Vj: the voltage across the depletion region alone (deck slide 76).

  • In equilibrium the junction holds Vbi. Forward bias opposes the built-in voltage (deck slide 76):
Vj = Vbi − Va
  • Forward (Va > 0): smaller barrier, narrower W. Majority carriers can climb it: large current (03b).
  • Reverse (Va < 0): bigger barrier Vbi + |Va|, wider W. Almost no current.
  • Step 11's formula with Vbi replaced by Vbi − Va gives W ∝ √(Vbi − Va). The ratio method of the deck: Vj = kW2, so W1/W0 = √(Vj1/Vj0).

Answer. Forward 0.6 V: W1/W0 = √((0.85 − 0.6)/0.85) = √0.294 = 0.54: the region shrinks. Reverse 2 V: √((0.85 + 2)/0.85) = √3.35 = 1.83: it expands.

Running example: +0.5 V gives W = 0.198 μm; −5 V gives 0.909 μm (2.73 × W0).

Check 12

(a) Vbi = 0.7 V, forward 0.4 V: W1/W0? (b) Same junction: what reverse bias makes W 3.16 times W0? (c) Why does almost all of Va appear across the depletion region and not the neutral regions?

Exam form

Junction formation (deck slides 70–74). Abrupt junction: NA for x < 0, ND for x > 0. At contact, holes diffuse p → n and electrons n → p and recombine. Fixed ions are left: − acceptors on the p side, + donors on the n side: the depletion (space-charge) region. Their field points n → p. Equilibrium: for each carrier, drift = −diffusion; EF flat; bands bend by qVbi.

Sketch (deck slide 73): ρ(x) two rectangles (−qNA, +qND, equal areas); E(x) a triangle with its peak at x = 0; ψ(x) two parabolas rising by Vbi.

Vbi derivation.

  1. pp = NA, pn = ni2/ND.
  2. Boltzmann: pn = ppe−Vbi/VT.
  3. Vbi = VTln(NAND/ni2).

Width. NAxp = NDxn; |Emax| = qNDxn/ε; Vbi = ½|Emax|W; W = √[(2ε/q)(1/NA + 1/ND)(Vbi − Va)]; xn = W·NA/(NA + ND).

Bias. Vj = Vbi − Va; W ∝ √(Vbi − Va); W1/W0 = √[(Vbi − Va1)/(Vbi − Va0)].

Traps

  • W ∝ √(Vbi − Va), not √Va: forgetting Vbi gives W = 0 at zero bias.
  • Reverse bias is a negative Va: Vbi − (−2) = Vbi + 2.
  • The depletion region reaches further into the lightly doped side, not the heavy one.
  • The built-in field points n → p (from + ions to − ions).
  • Units: ε in F/cm with N in cm−3 gives W in cm; 1 μm = 10−4 cm.
  • Vbi cannot be read by a voltmeter.
  • In equilibrium the currents are not zero one by one: each drift cancels a diffusion.
