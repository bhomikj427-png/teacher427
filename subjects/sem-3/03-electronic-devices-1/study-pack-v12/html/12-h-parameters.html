Build

1 · The h-parameter model

Predict

A two-port has four quantities: vin, iin, vout, iout. If you pick two as inputs to the equations, how many coefficients describe the box?

Terms

Two-port: a box with an input pair and an output pair of terminals. Small-signal quantities (lower case: ib, vce): the AC parts only. mho (℧) = siemens, 1/Ω.

vbe = hieib + hrevce   ic = hfeib + hoevce
Parameter Name Measured with Unit Typical (CE)
hie = vbe/ib input resistance output shorted (vce = 0) Ω 1.1 kΩ
hre = vbe/vce reverse voltage ratio input open (ib = 0) none 2.5 × 10−4
hfe = ic/ib forward current gain (≈ β) output shorted none 50
hoe = ic/vce output admittance input open S 25 μS

The second subscript names the connection: e, b or c (hib, hfc, …).

Answer. Four: two per equation. Their units are mixed (an impedance, an admittance, two ratios), so they are hybrid parameters.

(a) Which h-parameter is closest to β? (b) Why is hie measured with the output shorted?

2 · CE amplifier analysis

Book P9

A CE amplifier is driven by a source of internal resistance Rs = 800 Ω. The load is 2 kΩ. hie = 1.1 k, hfe = 50, hoe = 25 μmho, hre = 2.5 × 10−4. Compute Ai, Ri, Av, Ro, Rot and Ap, by (a) exact and (b) approximate analysis. Compute the % error in Ai.

Guess first

hfe is 50. Will the current gain into a real load be exactly 50, more, or less?

Quantity Exact Approximate (hoeRL < 0.1)
Ai = iL/ib −hfe/(1 + hoeRL) −hfe
Ri hie + hreAiRL hie
Av AiRL/Ri −hfeRL/hie
Yo (Ro = 1/Yo) hoe − hfehre/(hie + Rs) 0, Ro → ∞
Also Avs = AvRi/(Ri + Rs) · Ap = AiAv · Rot = Ro ∥ RL

Always in the order Ai → Ri → Av → Yo: each uses the one before. The minus signs are the CE phase reversal. The same formulas serve CB and CC with that connection's parameter set.

Answer (a), exact. Ai = −50/(1 + 0.05) = −47.6. Ri = 1100 + (2.5 × 10−4)(−47.6)(2000) = 1076.2 Ω. Av = (−47.6)(2000)/1076.2 = −88.5. Avs = −88.5 × 1076.2/1876.2 = −50.75. Yo = 25 μS − (50)(2.5 × 10−4)/1900 = 18.42 μS, Ro = 54.28 kΩ. Rot = 54.28 k ∥ 2 k = 1.93 kΩ. Ap = (−47.6)(−88.5) = 4214 (the book rounds earlier: 4210.6).

(b), approximate (hoeRL = 0.05 ✓). Ai = −50, Ri = 1100 Ω, Av = −50 × 2000/1100 = −90.9. Error in Ai = (50 − 47.6)/47.6 = 5%.

(a) hie = 1 kΩ, hfe = 100, RL = 1 kΩ: approximate Ai, Ri, Av. (b) What condition allows the approximate model?

3 · Cascading stages

Deck

Two amplifier stages with voltage gains 40 and 60 are cascaded. Find the overall gain as a number and in dB.

Guess first

Do the gains add or multiply?

The overall gain is the product of the stage gains, where each stage's gain is found with the next stage connected as its load (Ri of stage 2 loads stage 1). In decibels, Av(dB) = 20 log10|Av|, and the stage values add.

Coupling How For · against
RC a capacitor between stages cheap, small, good at audio; blocks DC so each stage biases separately; gain falls at low f
Transformer a transformer matches impedances for maximum power; bulky, costly, poor frequency response
Direct a wire amplifies down to DC; bias of one stage disturbs the next; drift is amplified

Answer. Av = 40 × 60 = 2400. 20 log 40 + 20 log 60 = 32.0 + 35.6 = 67.6 dB.

Check 3

(a) Why is the gain of stage 1 lower once stage 2 is connected? (b) Which coupling can amplify a DC signal?

4 · The Darlington pair

Deck

Derive the current gain of a Darlington pair.

Guess first

Q1's emitter current is Q2's base current. Roughly what is the overall β?

IE1 = (1 + β1)IB, and IE2 = (1 + β2)IE1 = (1 + β1)(1 + β2)IB. The collector current is IE2 − IB = (β1β2 + β1 + β2)IB:

βD = β1β2 + β1 + β2 ≈ β1β2

Two base–emitter drops in series: VBE ≈ 1.4 V. Very high input resistance. Used as an emitter follower to drive heavy loads. DC bias with RB and RE: IB = (VCC − VBE)/(RB + βDRE).

Answer. βD = β1β2 + β1 + β2, about β1β2: a "super-β" transistor.

Check 4

Two transistors with β = 100 each form a Darlington pair. Find βD exactly.

Exam form

h-parameters. Two equations; the table of four with name, test condition and unit; why "hybrid"; draw the model.

CE analysis. Ai = −hfe/(1 + hoeRL) → Ri = hie + hreAiRL → Av = AiRL/Ri → Yo = hoe − hfehre/(hie + Rs); Avs, Ap, Rot. Approximate when hoeRL < 0.1. Percentage error.

Multistage. Product of gains (loaded), dB add; RC, transformer, direct coupling compared.

Darlington. Derivation of βD; VBE ≈ 1.4 V; high Ri.

Traps

  • Computing Ri before Ai: Ri needs Ai.
  • Dropping the minus sign on Ai and Av.
  • Using μmho as mho: 25 μmho = 25 × 10−6 S.
  • Multiplying unloaded stage gains.
  • Forgetting Rs in Yo.

Self-test

  1. Define the four CE h-parameters with their test conditions.
  2. hie = 2 kΩ, hfe = 80, hoe = 10 μS, RL = 4 kΩ. Is the approximate model allowed? Find approximate Ai, Ri, Av.
  3. For Q2, find the exact Ai.
  4. Three stages have gains 10, 20 and 30. Overall gain, and in dB?
  5. A Darlington pair has β1 = 50, β2 = 80. Find βD.
  6. Compare RC and direct coupling in three points.

Answers

Check 1. (a) hfe. (b) Shorting the output sets vce = 0, so the hrevce term vanishes and vbe/ib is hie alone.

Check 2. (a) Ai = −100, Ri = 1 kΩ, Av = −100 × 1/1 = −100. (b) hoeRL < 0.1.

Check 3. (a) Stage 2's input resistance is in parallel with stage 1's collector resistor, lowering its effective load. (b) Direct coupling.

Check 4. 100 × 100 + 100 + 100 = 10 200.

Self-test.

  1. See step 1's table.
  2. hoeRL = 0.04 < 0.1 ✓. Ai = −80, Ri = 2 kΩ, Av = −80 × 4/2 = −160.
  3. Ai = −80/1.04 = −76.9.
  4. 10 × 20 × 30 = 6000; 20 log 6000 = 75.6 dB.
  5. 4000 + 50 + 80 = 4130.
  6. RC blocks DC (separate biasing, no drift passed on), cheap, low-frequency roll-off. Direct passes DC (amplifies DC signals), stages' biases interact, drift is amplified.
