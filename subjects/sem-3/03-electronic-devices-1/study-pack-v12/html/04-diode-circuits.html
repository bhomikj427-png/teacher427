Build

1 · The load line

Deck L08 slide 4

For the given diode configuration and diode characteristics, determine VDQ, IDQ and VR. (E = 10 V, R = 0.5 kΩ, silicon diode.)

Guess first

Roughly how much of the 10 V lands on the resistor?

The diode and resistor carry the same current, and KVL gives E = VD + IDR. Rearranged, ID = (E − VD)/R is a straight line on the diode's own axes. Its ends: VD = 0 gives ID = E/R; ID = 0 gives VD = E. The circuit must sit on the line and on the diode curve at once: the crossing is the Q-point.

Answer. Ends 20 mA and 10 V. The crossing reads VDQ ≈ 0.78 V, IDQ ≈ 18.5 mA. VR = 18.5 mA × 0.5 kΩ = 9.25 V (check: E − VDQ = 9.22 V).

Slip in the slide

Slide 5 writes VR = (18.5 mA)(1 K) = 18.5 V. R is 0.5 kΩ, and 18.5 V cannot come out of a 10 V source. Use 9.25 V.

Check 1

If R is doubled to 1 kΩ, where do the line's ends move, and does IDQ rise or fall?

2 · Without the graph: the three-step method

Deck L08 slide 14

Determine I, V1, V2 and Vo. (E1 = 10 V, Si diode, R1 = 4.7 kΩ, R2 = 2.2 kΩ, E2 = 5 V, as drawn.)

Guess first

Is the diode on? Which way do the two sources push?

  1. Decide the state. Remove the diode. If the voltage across the gap pushes current along the arrow and exceeds 0.7 V (Si), it is ON; otherwise OFF.
  2. Substitute. ON → a 0.7 V drop (0.3 V Ge, 0 ideal). OFF → open circuit, I = 0.
  3. Solve with KVL and Ohm's law.

Combinations: diodes in series add their drops (Si + red LED = 0.7 + 1.8 V); a reversed diode anywhere in a series path opens it. Diodes in parallel share one 0.7 V drop, and identical ones split the current equally.

Answer. The sources total 15 V along the arrow: ON. I = (10 + 5 − 0.7)/(4.7 k + 2.2 k) = 2.07 mA. V1 = 9.74 V, V2 = 4.56 V, Vo = V2 − E2 = −0.44 V.

3 · The half-wave rectifier

Deck L09 slide 9

Sketch the output vo and determine its DC level. Repeat if the ideal diode is replaced by a silicon diode. (Input: 20 V peak sine; the diode points toward the source; R = 2 kΩ across the output.)

Guess first

Which half-cycle reaches the output, and with what sign?

Terms

Rectifier: turns AC into one-direction (pulsating DC) voltage. Vm: peak of the input sine; Vrms = Vm/√2. Vdc: the average of the output over one period, what a DC meter reads. PIV: the largest reverse voltage the diode must survive while blocking.

One diode conducts on one half-cycle and blocks the other. Averaging one half-sine over a full period:

Vdc = Vm/π = 0.318Vm   Si: 0.318(Vm − 0.7)   PIV = Vm

Answer. The diode points toward the source, so it conducts only when vi is negative: the output is the negative half-cycles. Ideal: Vdc = −0.318 × 20 = −6.36 V. Si: −0.318 × 19.3 = −6.14 V. PIV = 20 V.

Check 3

Derive the ripple factor 1.21 from Vrms = Vm/2 and Vdc = Vm/π, using r = √[(Vrms/Vdc)2 − 1].

4 · Full-wave rectifiers

practice

A bridge rectifier with Si diodes is fed 12 V rms. Find Vm, Vdc and the PIV per diode.

Guess first

Two diodes conduct at a time in a bridge. How does that change the 0.7 V correction?

Both half-cycles push current through R in the same direction, so Vdc doubles. In the centre-tapped circuit Vm is the peak from the tap to one end, and the off diode sees both halves: PIV = 2Vm.

Efficiency η = (Vdc/Vrms)2 for ideal diodes. A capacitor across R (a filter) holds the voltage up between peaks and cuts the ripple.

Answer. Vm = 12√2 = 17.0 V. Vdc = 0.636 × (17.0 − 1.4) = 9.92 V. PIV ≈ 17 V.

Check 4

Why is a bridge usually preferred over the centre-tapped circuit? Two reasons.

Exam form

Load line. KVL: E = VD + IDR; ends (0, E/R) and (E, 0); Q-point at the crossing; VR = IDQR.

Three-step method. State → substitute (0.7 V / open) → KVL. Series drops add; parallel diodes share one drop.

Rectifiers. Draw each circuit with its waveforms. The comparison table (Vdc, Vrms, PIV, r, η, ripple f). Derive r = 1.21 and 0.482 and η = 40.6% and 81.2%.

Traps

  • Reading VR with the wrong R (the slide-5 slip).
  • Assuming a diode is on without the state test.
  • Subtracting only 0.7 V for a bridge: two diodes conduct, so 1.4 V.
  • PIV of the centre-tapped circuit is 2Vm, not Vm.
  • Using Vrms of the input (Vm/√2) for a half-wave output (Vm/2).

Self-test

  1. State the three-step method for a diode circuit.
  2. E = 12 V, Si diode, R = 1 kΩ in series. Find ID and VR.
  3. A half-wave rectifier with a Si diode has Vm = 10 V. Find Vdc and PIV.
  4. A centre-tapped rectifier has 15 V peak from the tap to each end, ideal diodes. Find Vdc and PIV.
  5. Derive the full-wave ripple factor.
  6. Why is the ripple frequency of a full-wave rectifier 2f?

Answers

Check 1. E/R falls to 10 mA; the VD end stays at 10 V. The line is flatter, so IDQ falls (to about 9.3 mA).

Check 2. (a) Forward: VD = 0.7 V, VR = 7.3 V, ID = 3.32 mA. Reversed: ID = 0, VR = 0, VD = 8 V. (b) Vo = 12 − 0.7 − 1.8 = 9.5 V; ID = 13.97 mA. (c) Vo = 0.7 V; I1 = 9.3/0.33 k = 28.18 mA; 14.09 mA each. (d) I1 = 0.7/3.3 k = 0.212 mA; I2 = (20 − 1.4)/5.6 k = 3.32 mA; ID2 = 3.11 mA.

Check 3. Vrms/Vdc = (Vm/2)/(Vm/π) = π/2 = 1.571; r = √(1.5712 − 1) = 1.21.

Check 4. No centre-tapped transformer is needed, and each diode needs only half the PIV (Vm, not 2Vm).

Self-test.

  1. See step 2.
  2. ID = 11.3/1 k = 11.3 mA; VR = 11.3 V.
  3. Vdc = 0.318 × 9.3 = 2.96 V; PIV = 10 V.
  4. Vdc = 0.636 × 15 = 9.54 V; PIV = 30 V.
  5. Vrms/Vdc = (Vm/√2)/(2Vm/π) = π/(2√2) = 1.111; r = √(1.234 − 1) = 0.482.
  6. Both half-cycles give an output pulse, so the output repeats twice per input period.
