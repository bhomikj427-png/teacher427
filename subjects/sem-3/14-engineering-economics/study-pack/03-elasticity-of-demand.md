# 03 — Elasticity of demand (L6–L7)

**All 3 deck numericals are answered here.** Every elasticity uses the same formula shape. Learn it once and
change the variable.

## Map

```
 elasticity = % change in quantity demanded / % change in <something>
    ├─ price elasticity Eₚ ──── 5 degrees: 0, <1, =1, >1, ∞
    ├─ income elasticity Eᵧ ─── sign/size tells you luxury / necessity / inferior
    ├─ cross elasticity Eₓᵧ ─── sign tells you substitutes / complements / unrelated
    └─ promotional elasticity Eₐ ── is advertising worth it?
```

---

## 1. The one formula

**Q ▸** (L6) The price rises by 10%. We know demand falls, but by more than 10% or by less?

That depends on the good, and measuring it is exactly what **elasticity** does. It is the responsiveness of quantity demanded to
a change in price, income or the price of a related good.

```
          % change in Q         ΔQ/Q          ΔQ     X
 E  =  ─────────────────── = ──────── = ──── × ───
          % change in X        ΔX/X          ΔX     Q

 ΔQ = new − original quantity     Q = ORIGINAL quantity
 ΔX = new − original X            X = ORIGINAL price / income / ad spend
```

**Use the ORIGINAL values** in X/Q. The deck does this in every problem.

## 2. The four elasticities

| Elasticity | X | Reading the answer |
|---|---|---|
| **Price** Eₚ | own price P | normally negative. Judge its **size** |Eₚ| |
| **Income** Eᵧ | income Y (or M) | the sign and size classify the good |
| **Cross** Eₓᵧ | **price of another good Y** (quantity of X) | the sign classifies the relationship |
| **Promotional** Eₐ | advertising spend A | positive → the advertising expands demand |

**Degrees of price elasticity (draw these curves if asked):**

| | Value | Curve |
|---|---|---|
| Perfectly elastic | E = ∞ | horizontal line |
| Perfectly inelastic | E = 0 | vertical line |
| Unitary elastic | E = 1 | rectangular hyperbola (P × Q constant) |
| Relatively elastic | E > 1 | flatter |
| Relatively inelastic | E < 1 | steeper |

**Income elasticity types (the deck's labels):**

| Eᵧ | Good |
|---|---|
| > 1 | luxury / superior |
| = 1 | "semi-luxury" (deck's term. Standard name: unit income elastic) |
| 0 < Eᵧ < 1 | necessity (a normal good) |
| = 0 | income doesn't change demand (cheap necessities, e.g. salt) |
| < 0 | **inferior** good |

**Cross elasticity:** > 0 **substitutes** (tea and coffee: Y's price ↑ → X's demand ↑). < 0 **complements** (car and
petrol: Y's price ↑ → X's demand ↓). = 0 **unrelated**.
*(The L6 slide explains the negative case using "income". The variable is the **price of Y**.)*

## 3. Price elasticity `[L7 P1]`

> Price reduced from ₹50 to ₹48 per unit to attract more customers. Demand increased from 100 to 110 units.
> Calculate the price elasticity of demand.

**Q ▸** Write ΔQ, ΔP and the originals before substituting.

ΔQ = 110 − 100 = 10 · ΔP = 48 − 50 = −2 · P = 50, Q = 100

Eₚ = (ΔQ/ΔP) × (P/Q) = (10 / −2) × (50/100) = **−2.5**

|Eₚ| = 2.5 > 1 → **relatively elastic**. A 4% price cut raised quantity by 10%. *(For an elastic good, a price cut
**raises total revenue**: 50 × 100 = 5000 → 48 × 110 = 5280.)*

## 4. Income elasticity `[L7 P2]`

> Income rises from ₹100 to ₹200. The quantity purchased rises from 25 to 30 units. Find the income elasticity of demand.

ΔQ = 5 · ΔY = 100 · Y = 100, Q = 25
Eᵧ = (5/100) × (100/25) = **0.2**

0 < 0.2 < 1 → a **necessity (normal good)**. Income doubled (+100%) but quantity rose only 20%.

## 5. Promotional elasticity `[L7 P3]`

> Quantity demanded increases from 8000 to 10,000 units because advertisement expenditure rises from ₹6000
> to ₹12000. Find the promotional elasticity of demand.

ΔQ = 2000 · ΔA = 6000 · A = 6000, Q = 8000
Eₐ = (2000/6000) × (6000/8000) = **0.25**

Positive → the advertising **does** expand demand, so the promotional spend is beneficial (the deck's conclusion). It is
**< 1**, though: doubling the ad spend raised quantity by only 25%.

## Traps

- **Original** values go in X/Q, not the new ones.
- Keep the **sign** for income and cross elasticity, because the sign *is* the answer. For price, report −2.5 and
  interpret it using |Eₚ|.
- Cross elasticity: the numerator is **X's** quantity and the denominator is **Y's** price.

## Self-test

1. Price rises from ₹10 to ₹12 and quantity falls from 200 to 180. Find Eₚ and classify it.
2. Income rises from ₹20,000 to ₹25,000 and quantity falls from 10 to 9. Find Eᵧ. What kind of good is it?
3. The price of Y rises from ₹40 to ₹50 and the demand for X rises from 100 to 120. Find Eₓᵧ. How are X and Y related?
4. Draw (in words) the demand curve for E = 0 and for E = ∞.

---

## Answers

1. (−20/2) × (10/200) = **−0.5** → |E| < 1, **relatively inelastic**.
2. (−1/5000) × (20000/10) = **−0.4** → negative, so an **inferior good**.
3. (20/10) × (40/100) = **+0.8** → positive, so **substitutes**.
4. E = 0: a **vertical** line (the same quantity at every price). E = ∞: a **horizontal** line (any price rise kills demand).
