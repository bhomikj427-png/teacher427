4-bit adder / 2's-complement subtractor (Fig 2.6)

  • Feed each B bit through an XOR whose other input is the control M (7486), and also send M to Cin (pin 13).
  • M = 0: B passes unchanged, Cin = 0, so the 7483 computes A + B.
  • M = 1: each B bit is inverted (1's complement) and Cin = 1 adds the 1, giving 2's complement. So the 7483 computes A − B.

BCD adder (Fig 2.7)

  • Add the two BCD digits in 7483 #1.
  • If the sum is more than 9, or produced a carry, add 6 (0110) in 7483 #2.

The correction signal is:

K = C4 + S3S2 + S3S1

Make S3S2 and S3S1 with two 7408 gates, then OR all three with two 7432 gates. Feed 0 K K 0 into B3 B2 B1 B0 of 7483 #2, with the first sum on its A inputs and Cin = 0. K is the decimal carry.

Example: 7 + 5. 7483 #1 gives 1100 (12 > 9), so K = 1. Then 1100 + 0110 = (1)0010. Output: carry 1, digit 0010, which reads as 12 ✓.

Write in your record

Result: the truth tables of the half/full adder and half/full subtractor were verified. The 7483 added two 4-bit numbers; with the XOR/M arrangement it subtracted in 2's complement (7 − 5 = 0010, Cout = 1; 5 − 7 = 1110, Cout = 0). The BCD adder gave 7 + 5 = 1 0010.

Theory for the record: a half adder adds two bits (S = A ⊕ B, C = AB). A full adder adds three, including the carry-in (S = A ⊕ B ⊕ Cin, Cout = AB + BCin + ACin), and can be built from two half adders and an OR. Subtractors produce a difference and a borrow (half: D = A ⊕ B, Bo = A'B). The 7483 is a 4-bit binary adder with fast carry. Subtraction uses A − B = A + B' + 1, made by XOR-ing B with a control M and feeding M to Cin. For BCD addition, 6 is added when the binary sum exceeds 9 or produces a carry.

Check 1

(a) In the subtractor, why does Cout = 1 mean "no borrow"? (b) BCD 8 + 9: give 7483 #1's output, K, and the final output. (c) A student powers the 7483 on pins 16 and 8. What happens?

Answers

Opening question. XOR each B bit with M (M = 1 inverts it) and feed M into Cin (+1). Together that adds the 2's complement of B, so the adder subtracts. After a subtraction, Cout = 1 means A ≥ B (positive result); Cout = 0 means the result is negative, in 2's-complement form.

Check 1. (a) A + (2⁴ − B) = 2⁴ + (A − B). When A ≥ B this overflows 4 bits, so carry = 1. When A < B it doesn't. (b) 8 + 9 = 17: #1 gives C4 = 1, S = 0001, so K = 1. 0001 + 0110 = 0111, carry 1, i.e. 1 0111 = 17 ✓. (c) Pin 16 is B3 and pin 8 is A1, so the chip gets no power. If the real VCC/GND pins (5, 12) are left floating while inputs are driven, it misbehaves and can be damaged. The outputs are garbage.
