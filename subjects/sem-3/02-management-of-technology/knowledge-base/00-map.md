# MBB2101 Management of Technology — Map (Stage 1)

> Big-picture-first (learner pref §3): the whole terrain before any one piece. This is the
> **full-depth** overview — branches *and* sub-branches, each one-lined. Scope = the official flat
> topic list (`course-info.md`), grouped into 5 logical units. Status `stage-1` (in progress).

## What this subject actually is (one line)

A **survey of entrepreneurship + the core business functions + IPR**, framed for engineers — *not*
the classical "Management of Technology." Low math, high terminology; exam is concept-recall +
short frameworks + small financial sums. The unifying thread: **turning a technical
invention into a viable, legally-protected, financed, staffed, marketed venture.**

## Full-depth concept tree

- **Unit 1 — Innovation & technology foundations**
  - *Invention vs Innovation* — invention = new idea/device; innovation = invention **commercialized
    / put to use**. (The course's whole premise: engineers invent, this course is the bridge to
    innovation.) `settled`
  - *Types of innovation* — product vs process; incremental vs radical/disruptive. `settled`
  - *Technology transition* — how a technology matures and is adopted: **technology life cycle**,
    the **S-curve** (slow→rapid→saturation), **diffusion of innovations** (Rogers: innovators →
    early adopters → early majority → late majority → laggards), the chasm. `settled` (frameworks);
    exact emphasis `uncertain` (no PYQ)
- **Unit 2 — Market analysis & marketing**
  - *Market analysis* — sizing/segmenting a market; demand assessment; competition. `settled`
  - *Market survey technique* — primary vs secondary data; sampling; questionnaire design;
    qualitative (interview/focus group) vs quantitative (survey) methods. `settled`
  - *Analysing marketing opportunities* — STP (segmentation, targeting, positioning); marketing
    mix (4 Ps); environmental scan (PESTEL); SWOT. `settled`
  - *Ansoff Matrix* — growth grid (existing/new × products/markets): **market penetration, market
    development, product development, diversification**. `settled` (verified)
- **Unit 3 — Entrepreneurship & project formulation**
  - *Entrepreneur & entrepreneurship* — risk-bearing innovator; types; the entrepreneurial process
    (idea → opportunity → plan → resources → launch → harvest). `settled`
  - *Project Formulation (phases 1/2/3)* — the scheme's "Project Formulations (1,2,3) based on market
    survey": staged project report build — **feasibility (technical/market/financial) →
    detailed project report (DPR) → appraisal/financing**. Exact 3-phase definition `uncertain`
    (course-specific framing; reconcile with PYQ/handout). Mechanism `settled`.
  - *Commercialization* — moving from prototype to market: tech readiness, business model, scaling,
    go-to-market. `settled`
- **Unit 4 — Functional management**
  - *Financial Management* — objective (maximize shareholder/firm value); sources of finance
    (equity/debt); time value of money; capital budgeting (payback, NPV, IRR); working capital;
    break-even. `settled` (core finance); which sub-tools the exam wants `uncertain`
  - *Human Resource Management* — HR functions: planning, recruitment & selection, training &
    development, performance appraisal, compensation, industrial relations. `settled`
  - *Leadership* — manager vs leader; trait/behavioural/contingency theories; styles
    (autocratic/democratic/laissez-faire); motivation links (Maslow, Herzberg, McGregor X/Y).
    `settled`
- **Unit 5 — Intellectual Property Rights**
  - *IP families (India)* — **Patent, Copyright, Trademark, Industrial Design, Geographical
    Indication**, plus trade secrets, semiconductor layout-designs, plant varieties. `settled`
  - *Terms (India, verified)* — Patent **20 yr** from filing (no renewal); Copyright **life + 60
    yr**; Trademark **10 yr, renewable indefinitely**; Design **10 + 5 = 15 yr** max; GI **10 yr,
    renewable**. `settled` (web-verified 2026-06-24; GI term `likely`)
  - *Indian acts/offices* — Patents Act 1970; Copyright Act 1957; Trade Marks Act 1999; Designs Act
    2000; GI Act 1999; CGPDTM office. `settled`
  - *Process & rationale* — what's patentable (novel, non-obvious, useful, not excluded); why IP
    exists (incentive vs disclosure trade-off). `settled`

## Threshold concepts (the "you don't get the course until you get these")

1. **Innovation ≠ invention** — value comes from commercialization, not the idea. The spine of the
   whole course.
2. **Ansoff = risk ladder** — penetration (lowest risk) → diversification (highest); the grid *is* a
   risk/growth trade-off, not just a 2×2.
3. **IP is a bargain, not a reward** — a time-limited monopoly *in exchange for* public disclosure;
   explains every term length and exclusion.
4. **TVM underlies all of financial appraisal** — NPV/IRR/payback only make sense once "money now >
   money later" clicks.

## ⭐ Stage-2 deserves-analysis (the explicit ask) — recommendation: **NO full Stage 2**

The learner asked, before any Stage-2 work: *does this subject even deserve a Stage 2?* Worked
honestly against `two-stage-depth.md`'s definition of Stage 2.

**What Stage 2 *is* (the bar):** IIT/Ivy depth — *first-principles derivations, assumptions & limits
of every model, where the UG textbook lies, cross-topic unification, the contested research
frontier, grad-qualifier problems.* That bar was written for **engineering/science** subjects where
a generative deep structure exists beneath the textbook (derive the diode equation, prove
convolution = LTI output).

**Why it largely doesn't fit MBB2101:**
1. **No first-principles substrate.** Ansoff, SWOT, the 4 Ps, Maslow, X/Y — these are *organizing
   frameworks*, not theorems. There is nothing to "derive." Stage 2's flagship deliverable
   (first-principles derivations) has almost no target here.
2. **Non-core, non-major, low-stakes.** It's a 3-credit management course for an ECE student. There
   is no grad qualifier on it, no downstream subject depends on it, and the teaching payoff of
   grad-level depth is low. Effort is better spent on the core engineering subjects still at
   `stage-0` (04, 05, 07, etc.).
3. **The "frontier" is real but a different subject.** Genuine MOT/innovation research (real options
   for R&D, disruption theory critiques, diffusion modelling, patent-strategy economics) *does*
   exist — but it's a graduate management course, **not** what MBB2101 tests. Building it would be
   research surplus aimed at the wrong target (violates scoring-over-depth).

**Where a *little* extra depth genuinely pays (a targeted "Stage 1.5," not a full Stage 2):**
- **IPR** — the one topic with real, examinable depth and correctness stakes: exact Indian acts,
  terms, patentability tests, recent amendments. Worth verifying to primary (the Acts), since wrong
  numbers here are a real liability. *Already mostly done in unit 05.*
- **Financial appraisal** — NPV/IRR/payback worked-problem patterns, *if* a PYQ shows the exam
  computes them. Procedural, not "deep."
- **Innovation/diffusion theory** — S-curve, Rogers, Christensen done one level past the textbook
  caricature, so teaching isn't shallow. Modest.

**Recommendation:** Drive this subject to **`stage-1✓`** (exam-ready, complete topic coverage) and
**stop there** for now. Do **not** build a full Stage 2. Instead, when teaching is activated, fold
the three "Stage 1.5" items above into Stage 1 (verify IPR to the Acts; add finance worked-problems
if a PYQ demands; deepen innovation theory one notch). **Teaching gate caveat:** the engine's hard
rule is *teach only at `stage-2✓`*. This subject is the legitimate exception to *formalize with the
learner* — for a framework-based survey, `stage-1✓` + the IPR/finance hardening **is** a complete
base; a ritual Stage-2 folder would be padding.
