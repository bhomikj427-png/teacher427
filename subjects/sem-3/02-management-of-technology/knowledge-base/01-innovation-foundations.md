## 1. Invention vs Innovation (the spine of the course)

- **Invention** = creation of a **new idea, device, method, or process** — the *technical* act. May
  never leave the lab.
- **Innovation** = **invention put to commercial/practical use** — invention + exploitation. "The
  successful commercial introduction of a new product/process." (Mechanism: value is created only
  when the invention reaches users at scale.)
- **One-line discriminator (exam):** *Invention creates; innovation captures value.* An idea on
  paper is invention; the same idea sold profitably is innovation.
- Why it matters here: engineers are trained to **invent**; this course is the bridge to
  **innovate** — finance it, protect it, market it, staff it.

**Types of innovation (recurring exam list):**
- **Product** (new/improved offering) vs **Process** (new/improved way to make/deliver it).
- **Incremental** (small improvements on existing) vs **Radical/breakthrough** (new-to-the-world).
- **Disruptive** (Christensen): starts cheaper/simpler, serves an overlooked segment, then moves
  upmarket and displaces incumbents. `settled` (concept); deeper critique is Stage-1.5/2 material.

## 2. Technology transition (how a technology matures & spreads)

Three standard lenses — expect "explain with diagram" questions.

### (a) Technology Life Cycle / S-curve
Performance vs effort(time) traces an **S**: slow start (immature) → rapid improvement (growth) →
**saturation** (limits of the approach). A new technology starts a *new* S-curve; the **discontinuity**
between curves is where incumbents get displaced.
- **Mechanism:** early gains are hard (learning), middle gains are fast (refinement), late gains
  shrink (physical/diminishing limits).
- → render the S-curve and curve-jump to the lesson surface (primitive 4) when teaching.

### (b) Diffusion of Innovations (Rogers) — adopter categories
The cumulative-adoption curve is S-shaped; the **adopter bell-curve** splits the population:

| Category | ~Share | Trait |
|----------|--------|-------|
| Innovators | ~2.5% | venturesome, risk-tolerant |
| Early adopters | ~13.5% | opinion leaders |
| Early majority | ~34% | deliberate, follow leaders |
| Late majority | ~34% | sceptical, adopt under pressure |
| Laggards | ~16% | tradition-bound, last |

Percentages are Rogers' textbook figures (normal-curve partitions) — `settled` as the standard, but
they're a model, not a measurement; quote as "approximately."
- **The chasm** (Moore): the gap between early adopters and early majority where many tech products
  die. `settled`.
- **5 factors driving adoption rate:** relative advantage, compatibility, complexity (−), trialability,
  observability.

### (c) "Technology transition" as substitution
Old → new technology handover (e.g. film→digital). Driven when the new S-curve's trajectory exceeds
the old's ceiling. Tie to commercialization timing (Unit 3).
