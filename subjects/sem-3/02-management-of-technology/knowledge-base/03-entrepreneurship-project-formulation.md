## 1. Entrepreneurship (framing context)
- **Entrepreneur** = one who organizes, manages, and **assumes the risk** of a venture to exploit an
  opportunity through innovation. (Schumpeter: the entrepreneur drives "creative destruction.")
- **Entrepreneurial process:** opportunity identification → evaluation → business plan → resource
  marshalling → launch & manage → growth/harvest. `settled`
- **Types:** innovative, imitative, social, technopreneur, intrapreneur (inside a firm). `settled`

## 2. Project Formulation — phases 1, 2, 3 (`uncertain` exact framing)

> The scheme says "Project Formulations (1, 2 and 3) **based on market survey technique**." The
> numbered phasing is course-specific; the mapping below is a **reasoned reconstruction** from
> standard project-management / DPR practice (Chandra, *Projects*), tied back to the market survey of
> Unit 2. **Flagged `uncertain`** — reconcile with a PYQ or the MBB2101 handout.

Project formulation = turning an idea (validated by the **market survey**, Unit 2) into a structured,
financeable project. Standard staged build:

- **Phase 1 — Feasibility / opportunity study.** From the market survey: is there demand? Screen the
  idea on **market, technical, and financial** feasibility (+ managerial, legal/environmental).
  Output: go/no-go.
- **Phase 2 — Detailed Project Report (DPR).** Full plan: market plan, technical plan
  (technology, location, capacity, process), organizational plan, **financial plan** (cost of
  project, means of finance, projected cash flows), implementation schedule.
- **Phase 3 — Appraisal & financing.** Evaluate the DPR (financial appraisal — payback/NPV/IRR,
  see Unit 4; risk/sensitivity), arrange finance, then implement.

**The seven aspects of project feasibility** (common exam list): market, technical, financial,
economic, managerial, legal, environmental/ecological. `likely`.

## 3. Commercialization
Moving a validated invention from prototype to market revenue.
- **Steps:** prototype → pilot/test market → scale-up production → launch → grow.
- **Technology readiness** — is the tech mature enough to sell? (links Unit 1 S-curve/transition).
- **Business model** — how the venture creates, delivers, and **captures** value (revenue model,
  cost structure, channels). The capture step is what turns innovation (Unit 1) into money.
- **Go-to-market** — pricing, channel (Unit 2's Place/Promotion), early-adopter targeting (Unit 1
  diffusion: cross the chasm).
- **Routes:** start a venture, license the IP (Unit 5), or sell/assign it.
- **Funding ladder:** bootstrapping → friends/family → angel → venture capital → debt/IPO. `settled`
